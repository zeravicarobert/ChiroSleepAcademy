# 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Chiropractic Sleep Academy™ is Dr. Robert Zeravica's effort to establish a new chiropractic category at the intersection of sleep, breathing, airway structure, cranial and spinal biomechanics, neurological respiratory control, and whole-body structural stability.

CSA is not intended to position chiropractic as a replacement for sleep medicine, CPAP, oral appliances, medication, or surgery. Its central argument is that the established sleep-medicine ecosystem is incomplete because it usually concentrates on measuring disordered sleep and managing airway failure after it has become clinically significant, while giving insufficient attention to the structural and biomechanical conditions that may affect breathing before, during, and after conventional intervention.

Dr. Zeravica's emerging intervention hierarchy is:

* **Early intervention:** structural, biomechanical, cranial, cervical, postural, neurological, and breathing-system assessment and care.
* **Mid-stage intervention:** CPAP, oral appliances, home sleep testing, and other equipment-based management.
* **Late intervention:** pharmaceutical and surgical intervention.

CSA's signature conceptual destination is an:

> **Unsubluxated Structural Breathing System™**

The objective is not merely to "restore" breathing, but to **unsubluxate, expand, establish, and stabilize the structural breathing system so that physiology can function with less mechanical interference.**

CSA currently exists as several overlapping things:

1. A new chiropractic and airway intellectual category.
2. A personal clinical method built from Dr. Zeravica's chiropractic experience.
3. An educational academy for chiropractors.
4. A testing and outcomes-data platform.
5. A treatment and retreat experience centered on Tecopa.
6. A potential research, conference, publication, licensing, and professional-training organization.
7. A long-term brand and movement intended to demonstrate that biomechanics is a missing component of sleep and airway care.

# 2. THE ORIGIN STORY

Dr. Zeravica's foundation is a 26-year chiropractic career that originally focused primarily on spine-centered musculoskeletal care.

Throughout that career, patients frequently reported that after their first chiropractic adjustment they had "slept like a baby." Those observations created an early experiential connection between chiropractic care, neurological regulation, physical comfort, and sleep.

Dr. Zeravica later attended sleep conferences and entered the professional sleep ecosystem. He concluded that chiropractic, cranial mechanics, posture, whole-body biomechanics, and structural airway considerations were either marginalized or absent from mainstream discussions.

CSA emerged from the resulting question:

> What is sleep medicine missing when it examines the airway, sleep stages, oxygen, respiratory events, CPAP, oral appliances, and surgery—but does not adequately examine the structural breathing system that contains, positions, and mechanically influences those functions?

CSA is therefore not merely a new service. It is Dr. Zeravica's response to a perceived category-level omission.

# 6. CLINICAL AND SCIENTIFIC ARCHITECTURE

CSA's clinical theory is a whole-system model. It is not limited to a single vertebra, cranial suture, nerve, muscle, airway dimension, or device.

## 6.1 The structural container

CSA examines the physical system through which breathing must occur:

* Nasal airway.
* Oral airway.
* Pharyngeal airway.
* Tongue compartment.
* Maxilla.
* Mandible.
* Cranial base.
* Cervical spine.
* Thorax.
* Diaphragm.
* Abdomen.
* Pelvis.
* Whole-body posture.

The CSA premise is that respiratory physiology operates inside a mechanical container. The shape, tension, movement, position, and stability of that container may affect breathing efficiency.

## 6.2 The neurological controller

The automatic breathing controller is centered in the brainstem, especially the medulla and pons.

Project educational language currently uses:

> **The medulla is the breathing rhythm engine. The pons is the pattern shaper.**

The medulla generates and organizes fundamental respiratory rhythm. The pons helps smooth and shape transitions between inspiration and expiration.

Relevant structures discussed in Project materials include:

* Dorsal respiratory group.
* Ventral respiratory group.
* Pre-Bötzinger complex.
* Pontine respiratory centers.
* Central chemoreceptors.
* Peripheral chemoreceptors.

## 6.3 Sensors and feedback

The respiratory-control system receives information through:

* Central chemical sensing.
* Carotid bodies.
* Aortic bodies.
* Lung stretch receptors.
* Irritant receptors.
* Mechanoreceptors.
* Blood oxygen and carbon-dioxide feedback.
* Mechanical information from the airway, chest wall, and respiratory muscles.

## 6.4 Respiratory motor pathways

Project materials identify:

* Phrenic nerve, C3–C5, controlling the diaphragm.
* Intercostal nerves controlling thoracic respiratory muscles.
* Accessory motor pathways involving the scalenes, sternocleidomastoid, and abdominal muscles.
* Hypoglossal nerve control of tongue musculature.
* Vagus- and glossopharyngeal-related pharyngeal and laryngeal control.

The CSA view is that respiratory control must coordinate more than the lungs. It must coordinate:

* Airway valve.
* Tongue posture.
* Diaphragm pump.
* Rib-cage mechanics.
* Abdominal pressure.
* Pharyngeal stability.
* Carbon-dioxide and oxygen chemistry.

## 6.5 Airway mechanics and neurological control

Earlier wording separated a "hardware/container problem" from a "software/autonomic-control problem." Dr. Zeravica objected that both are embodied in physical biological structures.

The improved CSA distinction is:

* **Structural-mechanical system:** airway shape, cranial relationships, tongue space, cervical position, thoracic mechanics, diaphragm, fascia, and posture.
* **Neurological-control system:** brainstem centers, nerves, sensory feedback loops, motor output, and respiratory stability.

Both systems are biological hardware that perform different functions.

## 6.6 Loop gain

Loop gain is used to explain instability in the ventilatory-control system.

The Project metaphor is a thermostat:

* A stable thermostat makes a small correction to a small change.
* A high-loop-gain thermostat overcorrects, overshoots, and then corrects in the opposite direction.

Applied to breathing during sleep, a highly reactive control system may repeatedly overshoot and undershoot respiratory needs.

CSA therefore recognizes that apnea may include both:

* Mechanical collapse or restriction.
* Instability in respiratory-control response.

## 6.7 Broader whole-body coupling

CSA is interested in emerging evidence and models involving mechanical coupling among:

* Abdomen.
* Venous pressure.
* Spine.
* Cerebrospinal fluid.
* Cranial contents.
* Brain movement.
* Respiration.

This area is potentially important to the CSA intellectual framework but must be clearly separated into:

1. Established anatomy and physiology.
2. Emerging research.
3. Dr. Zeravica's clinical hypotheses.
4. Outcomes that have actually been documented.

# 10. "HEAL THE HEALER FIRST"

CSA's foundational enrollment principle is:

> **Heal the healer first.**

A chiropractor should experience the sleep-airway evaluation, testing, and structural process personally before being trained to explain, recommend, or deliver it to patients.

The intended progression is:

1. Chiropractor enters as a patient.
2. Chiropractor sees and feels the process.
3. Chiropractor receives objective testing.
4. Chiropractor undergoes care.
5. Chiropractor reviews pre- and post-data.
6. Chiropractor learns how to identify similar patients.
7. Chiropractor develops a sleep-airway division.
8. Chiropractor initially works through CSA.
9. Chiropractor gradually becomes competent and independent where appropriate.
10. CSA maintains standards, data, education, and possibly licensing or certification.

This creates personal conviction before professional promotion.

# 22. RISKS

## Evidence risk

CSA could lose credibility by presenting hypotheses as settled mechanisms or by claiming causation from uncontrolled pre- and post-treatment observations.

## Regulatory risk

Sleep testing, diagnosis, imaging, chiropractic scope, healthcare advertising, telehealth, data ownership, and professional referrals are regulated areas.

## Brand risk

"Treating sleep apnea" may attract attention but can imply a claim broader than the documented evidence or professional scope supports.

## Founder-dependence risk

CSA currently depends heavily on Dr. Zeravica's personal clinical judgment, story, technique integration, and communication ability.

## Protocol-reproducibility risk

A method may produce results in the founder's hands but remain difficult to teach consistently.

## Offer-complexity risk

Consultation, HST, CBCT, travel, Tecopa treatment, bodywork, follow-up chiropractic care, professional training, data participation, and business development may be too many simultaneous moving parts for an initial offer.

## Partnership risk

Equity or participation granted before clear milestones, vesting, documentation, and ownership definitions could jeopardize CSA.

## Data risk

Owning or aggregating clinical data creates privacy, cybersecurity, consent, contracting, and research obligations.

## Distraction risk

The EDD business, personal brand, VA work, CSA, Tecopa, content creation, research, legal work, and multiple technologies can compete for founder attention.
