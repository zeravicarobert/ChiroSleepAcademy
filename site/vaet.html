VAET

Viscerocranial Airway Expansion Therapy — an advanced, structural approach to the cranial and nasal structures that surround your airway.

A structural technique · not a treatment for a medical diagnosis

What VAET is

VAET is a set of advanced structural and cranial techniques focused on the area around the nasal airway. It includes careful, controlled balloon-assisted procedures. The aim is to work with the structures that shape breathing — and to measure what we find, objectively, over time.

Why nasal structure matters

You breathe through your nose most of the day and night. The bones and passages of the nose and mid-face help decide how easily air moves. When that structure is tight or off-balance, breathing can be harder. VAET is one structural way we work with that area.

How the process works

  • Evaluate first. We start with a structural airway exam, history, and — when appropriate — a home sleep test and CBCT imaging.
  • Plan carefully. VAET is used as part of a larger structural plan, not on its own and not for everyone.
  • Work gently. The balloon-assisted procedure is controlled and specific to the structures involved.
  • Measure again. We track structural and symptom changes over time so we can see what's actually happening.
Honest about claims. VAET is a structural approach. It does not diagnose or treat sleep apnea, does not claim to cure any condition, and does not replace CPAP, oral appliances, or medical or surgical care. It is meant to work alongside your other providers.

Common questions

Is VAET a cure for sleep apnea?

No. VAET is a structural technique, not a treatment for a medical diagnosis. We don't claim to cure sleep apnea or any other condition. We evaluate and work with structure, and we coordinate with your medical and dental team.

Does VAET replace my CPAP or oral appliance?

No. VAET is not a replacement for CPAP, oral appliances, medication, or surgery. If those are part of your care, keep working with the providers who prescribe them.

Is it for everyone?

No. VAET is used only when a full structural and sleep evaluation suggests it's appropriate, and always as part of a larger plan.

How do you know if it's helping?

We measure. Structural findings, symptoms, and objective testing are tracked over time so change is something we can actually observe.
