Structural Sleep & Airway Chiropractic.

Sleep and breathing problems are usually looked at through CPAP, dental appliances, medicine, or surgery. Those all matter. But the shape of your head, jaw, nose, and neck matters too — and structure is where chiropractic can help.

Your airway is partly shaped by your structure

Air moves through your nose, behind your jaw, and down past your neck. The bones and posture around that path help decide how open or tight it is. When the skull, jaw, nose, or neck sit a certain way, breathing can get harder — especially at night.

That's the idea behind Structural Sleep & Airway Chiropractic. We look at the whole structure around the airway, not just the lower back.

Three principles

i.

Skull-down

We start at the top — skull, face, nose, and jaw — because those structures shape the airway.

ii.

Sleep-first

Sleep and breathing lead the conversation. A home sleep test gives us real numbers to work from.

iii.

Structure-focused

We measure posture, cranial structure, and the airway, then track changes over time — not guesswork.

What we evaluate

Sleep & airway history

Snoring, fatigue, mouth-breathing, grinding, and screening questionnaires.

Structural airway exam

Face, jaw, nose, tongue, and head posture — the structures around breathing.

Home sleep test (HST)

Ordered through a partner and read by a physician — the objective baseline.

CBCT airway imaging

A 3D look at the airway and craniofacial structure when it's appropriate.

Posture & cranial screen

Forward-head posture, cranial and TMJ findings, and structural balance.

Objective tracking

We re-measure over time so change is something we can see, not just feel.

Where structure fits

Chiropractic can be part of the picture — earlier.

Airway care runs along a path. Structural care may help earlier and works alongside medicine and dentistry — never against them. Chiropractors do not diagnose or treat sleep apnea; we evaluate structure and coordinate care.
