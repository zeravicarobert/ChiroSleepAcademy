A patient came to me for low back pain. I helped his back, and he told me he slept like a baby that night. I filed it away.

Then another man came in for the same thing — and this time, honestly, I didn't get his back better. He came back and said the exact same words. That stopped me. If I didn't fix his back and he slept better anyway, then whatever was happening on my table wasn't only about the pain. It was something structural — and it kept happening, patient after patient, until I couldn't ignore it.

Structural Airway Chiropractic

The airway is anatomy. And chiropractors deal with anatomy.

When someone can't breathe well at night, the world moves fast to equipment, then to medication and surgery. Those are real, valuable tools — and I work alongside the doctors and dentists who provide them. But there's a phase of care that comes first: evaluating and addressing the structure itself.

Early

Structure & alignment

The structural upper-airway obstruction (SAO) — the anatomy behind the breathing.

Middle

Support

CPAP and oral appliances — support the airway during sleep.

Late

Alteration

Medication and surgery — the most aggressive tier, when needed.

Early → Middle → Late

You can't skip the beginning of care.

The Mission

There was no seat for a chiropractor at the sleep table. So I built one.

When I went looking for answers, I found the real education was offered to physicians and dentists — not to us. I could have gotten bitter about that. Instead, I got to work.

Like my mentor Daymond John and his “For Us, By Us” philosophy, I stopped waiting for a place at the table and built one. I created Chiropractic Sleep Academy — to give doctors of chiropractic the training, the model, and the seat we'd been denied.
