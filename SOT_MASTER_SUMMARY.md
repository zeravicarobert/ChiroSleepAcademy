## 1. PROJECT IDENTITY

**Project:** Sacro Occipital Technique (SOT), cranial work, CMRT, airway/sleep integration, education, research, collaboration, and professional positioning.

**Primary user:** Dr. Robert Zeravica, DC (“Dr. Z”).

**Core objective:** Master, modernize, validate, teach, and strategically apply SOT—especially pelvic categories, cranials, TMJ, CMRT, primary respiratory mechanics, and airway/sleep care—within Dr. Z’s Chiropractic Sleep Academy (CSA) and VAET ecosystem.

## 2. CORE SOT SYSTEM

SOT is treated as a broad chiropractic/osteopathic system, not merely pelvic blocking.

Included:

* Pelvic Categories I, II, III.
* Pelvic blocks, including sacral blocks.
* Dollar Sign and Crest Sign analysis.
* Palpation working zones.
* Cervical palpation: styloids, transverse processes, spinous processes.
* Sacral, pelvic, spinal, cranial, dural, ligamentous, muscular, visceral, and respiratory relationships.
* Cranials: SOT, Applied Kinesiology, intraoral, post-inflation, TMJ, and CFR-related procedures.
* SB+/SB− procedures.
* Iliofemoral and psoas work.
* CMRT—Chiropractic Manipulative Reflex Technique—also called **Bloodless Surgery**.
* Primary Respiratory Mechanism/Impulse (**PRM/PRI**).
* Reciprocal tension membranes, dural tension, CSF movement, craniosacral rhythm, and airway relationships.
* Lovett Brothers relationships, especially sacrum–occiput, L5–C1, coccyx–sphenoid, and TMJ–SIJ coupling.

Preferred wording: **pelvic blocks**, not simply “blocks.”

## 3. PELVIC CATEGORIES AND CERVICAL PALPATION

Dr. Robert Monk’s *Simplified SOT*, especially page 32, was discussed.

Conclusion:

* Cervical palpation applies to **all three SOT categories**.
* **Category I:** cervical findings primarily reflect dural/cranial tension and altered respiratory mechanics.
* **Category II:** findings reflect compensatory ligamentous stress and pelvic instability.
* **Category III:** findings more directly indicate local segmental fixation, pain, or disc-related dysfunction.

The same cervical landmarks may be examined in every category; their meaning changes with the category.

## 4. CRANIAL AND AIRWAY INTEGRATION

The project repeatedly connects:

* Pelvis → dura → spine → occiput → sphenoid → maxilla → TMJ → nasal airway.
* Cranial motion, cranial buttresses, facial structure, breathing, snoring, sleep-disordered breathing, posture, and neurological regulation.
* TMJ balance with SIJ/pelvic balance.
* CFR, SOT cranials, AK cranials, balloon-assisted nasopharyngeal adjustment, and VAET.

**VAET:** Viscerocranial Airway Expansion Therapy.

VAET builds upon CFR and SOT while emphasizing:

* Airway-aperture expansion.
* Craniofacial and viscerocranial mechanics.
* Nasal/nasopharyngeal airflow.
* Cranial liquid drainage, including CSF and sinus/postnasal drainage.
* TMJ, cervical, pelvic, postural, and respiratory-system integration.

Position established in prior correspondence:

* CFR is chiropractic.
* SOT has both chiropractic and osteopathic roots.
* VAET extends CFR/SOT toward airway, sleep, TMJ, craniofacial, and structural-breathing applications.

SOT is a foundational component of **Chiropractic Sleep Academy** and Dr. Z’s “Chiropractic 2.0” concept.

Strategic positioning:

* Translate SOT into modern biomechanical, neurological, physiological, respiratory, and measurable language.
* Avoid reducing SOT to speculation or historical jargon.
* Solve recognizable problems: sleep, airway, TMJ, craniofacial mechanics, posture, neurological regulation, and treatment plateaus.
* Develop reproducible examination, treatment, documentation, outcome, and teaching systems.
* Build standardized digital education and a modern academy.

Key synthesis:

> **CBP measures the frame; SOT measures the rhythms.**

Integration:

* CBP objectively measures posture and structural alignment.
* SOT identifies pelvic, dural, cranial, respiratory, and compensatory dysfunction.
* SOT may explain why structural correction plateaus.
* CBP measurements may help validate SOT-driven change.
