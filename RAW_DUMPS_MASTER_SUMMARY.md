## 4. Chiropractic Sleep Academy

### 4.1 Category objective

CSA is intended to create and define a professional category that can be described as:

**Sleep & Airway Chiropractic**

The project is not merely about adding sleep screening to ordinary chiropractic practice. It proposes re-teaching chiropractic from the skull downward, beginning with:

1. Sleep
2. Airway
3. Cranial and neurological control
4. Cervical and postural biomechanics
5. Whole-body structural relationships

The academy's framework challenges the assumption that sleep apnea is only a sleep-laboratory, CPAP, oral-appliance, pharmaceutical, weight-loss, or surgical matter.

Its position is that these approaches may be valid but incomplete because airway function also has:

* Cranial
* Cervical
* Mandibular
* Postural
* Neurological
* Respiratory-motor
* Structural
* Whole-body biomechanical dimensions

### 4.2 Central language

Important current expressions include:

* **Unsubluxated Structural Breathing System™**
* **Expand**
* **Unsubluxate**
* **Establish Structural Breathing Stability**
* **Chiropractic 2.0 of breathing**
* **Sleep-first**
* **Airway-first**

The early/middle/late framework is particularly important:

* **Early intervention:** Biomechanical, structural, cranial, postural, behavioral, screening, and preventive work

This is a positioning model, not yet a universally validated clinical hierarchy. It must be stated carefully in professional and research contexts.

## 5. VAET and the Clinical Model

### 5.1 Current concept

VAET refers to Dr. Zeravica's developing **Viscerocranial Airway Expansion Technique/Therapy**.

The model combines aspects of:

* Cranial work
* Airway expansion
* Cervical biomechanics
* TMJ relationships
* Tongue and hypoglossal function
* Posture and forward-head posture
* Pelvic and whole-body structural patterns
* Visceral relationships
* Sleep testing
* Imaging
* Clinical observation
* Follow-up outcome measurement

The current premium clinical experience has been described as four sessions over two days at Death Valley Hot Springs/Tecopa, priced at approximately **$3,500**.

An uploaded strategic plan explicitly treats this Tecopa/VAET retreat as a separate business from the disability-certification operation and recommends sequencing it after the certification infrastructure is stabilized.

### 5.2 Treatment identity

The treatment model is not intended to promise that it "cures sleep apnea." Its stronger and more defensible developing claim is that it addresses biomechanical and structural contributors that may be overlooked by conventional sleep-apnea pathways.

The clinically responsible formulation is:

**CSA screens, measures, documents, and addresses structural and biomechanical contributors to breathing and airway function while coordinating with appropriate medical, dental, sleep, and diagnostic providers.**

### 5.3 Ventilatory-control framework

The project increasingly distinguishes between:

* The structural airway container
* Respiratory rhythm and pattern generation
* Chemoreceptor feedback
* Respiratory motor output
* Tongue and pharyngeal stabilization
* Loop gain and ventilatory instability
* Sleep-stage and positional influences

Uploaded graphics and prompts show that CSA is developing educational material around the medulla, pons, carotid bodies, aortic bodies, phrenic nerve, hypoglossal nerve, diaphragm, intercostals, tongue, soft palate, and pharyngeal airway muscles.

The project rejects an oversimplified hardware-versus-software metaphor when both structural tissues and neurological control structures are physical anatomy. A better distinction is:

* **Airway structure and mechanical behavior**
* **Ventilatory regulation and motor control**

## 6. Measurement, Diagnostics, and Data Strategy

CSA is intended to be outcomes-oriented rather than relying only on testimonials or philosophical claims.

### 6.1 Home sleep testing

The current founding model is:

* Patient pays CSA **$300**
* The amount covers the HST, vendor expense, setup, coordination, CSA administration, and report-of-findings process
* It does not include CBCT
* CSA can absorb a vendor cost up to approximately **$50 per test**

Required HST information should include:

* Full PDF report
* Raw data when available
* AHI
* RDI when available
* Lowest oxygen saturation
* Oxygen-desaturation measures
* Time below 90% oxygen saturation
* Snoring data
* Positional data
* Physician interpretation

### 6.2 CBCT

CBCT is separate and has generally been discussed as approximately **$300–$750**, depending on the provider and required imaging.

Preferred payment workflow:

* Patient pays CSA
* CSA pays the dentist or imaging provider
* Direct payment to the dentist may be allowed case by case

CSA should require:

* Written radiology or CBCT report
* DICOM or raw scan files
* Airway measurements
* Relevant craniofacial findings

### 6.3 Report of findings

The CSA report-of-findings process should synthesize:

* HST findings
* CBCT airway findings
* Symptom history
* Bed-partner or witness observations
* Snoring and apnea reports
* Sleep position
* Relevant structural findings
* Posture
* Cranial and mandibular relationships
* Appropriate referral needs
* Treatment options and limitations

### 6.4 Data ownership and research

A major project priority is for CSA to maintain lawful access to usable outcome information, especially de-identified before-and-after data, subject to:

* HIPAA
* Patient consent
* Vendor agreements
* Appropriate data-use language
* Research ethics
* Professional and legal review

The long-term objective is to:

* Track outcomes
* Compare pre- and post-intervention measures
* Build case series
* Present posters and conference material
* Publish observational findings
* Demonstrate whether meaningful patterns exist
* Refine or reject parts of the clinical model based on evidence

* Appeals and identity-verification issues
* Patient education
* Coordination with imaging, treatment, or other providers

### 7.2 Current pricing architecture

The latest recurring model is approximately:

* **$400 initial certification/evaluation**
* **$150 monthly** for ongoing servicing, coaching, extensions, or related support

An uploaded strategy document describes a three-tier patient value model:

* Tier 1: $400 medical certificate and EDD submission
* Tier 2: $150 monthly, averaging about 3.5 months
* Tier 3: approximately $1,000 referral commission from a Closer Academy conversion, when appropriate

This third tier is not intended for every patient. Even the strategic plan warns that presenting an entrepreneurial referral too early to a financially distressed patient could appear exploitative and suggests waiting until stabilization and demonstrated interest.

### 7.3 Strategic tension

Alex Mandossian's supplied plan recommends aggressively scaling the certification business through:

* Union relationships
* Lunch-and-learns
* Facebook groups
* Videos
* Testimonials
* Attorney relationships
* A patient coordinator or VA
* A formalized offer

Its core message is: **build the machine before running ads.**

The plan identifies California union trade workers as the primary target, with Boilermakers Local 154 as an existing relationship to formalize.

However, Dr. Zeravica has subsequently stated that he does not want to make active growth of the EDD business his principal strategic project. He prefers it to grow organically through existing referrals while he directs his creative and authority-building energy toward his personal brand, Tecopa, VAET, CSA, clinical measurement, and professional sleep-and-airway advocacy.

Therefore, the Mandossian plan should be treated as an **available execution map**, not as an automatically adopted strategic command.

### 7.4 Documentation integrity

Uploaded SOAP-note materials demonstrate an important project standard: clinical notes must distinguish what was actually observed from what the patient merely reported.

One corrected telehealth note states only that a telehealth/phone evaluation occurred, records the patient's reported symptoms, uses diagnosis M54.50, and avoids adding unperformed physical-examination findings.

Another uploaded version contains statements such as the patient being alert, oriented, coherent, and functionally limited. Those details require actual observation or documentation and should not be added retrospectively merely to strengthen a file.

This supports a governing documentation rule:

**Do not manufacture examination findings, measurements, testing, functional observations, or clinical certainty that were not actually obtained and documented.**
