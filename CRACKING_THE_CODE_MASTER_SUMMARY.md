## Triangulated barter

Triangulated barter involves creating value among three or more parties rather than requiring a direct exchange between two people.

For example:

* one person provides access;
* another supplies expertise;
* another contributes marketing or technology;
* and each receives something of greater value than what they contributed.

Dr. Z has considered how unused:

* event tickets;
* memberships;
* coaching access;
* professional services;
* introductions;
* and marketing capabilities

could be exchanged or combined to create deals.

# 22. RISKS TO AVOID

The project identifies several strategic dangers.

## Chasing too many opportunities

Dr. Z generates many ideas and recognizes opportunities quickly.

Without prioritization, this can lead to:

* fragmentation;
* unfinished projects;
* diluted authority;
* conflicting offers;
* and excessive operational burden.

## Becoming the fulfillment department

Dr. Z should avoid creating deals in which he performs all of the work while partners receive most of the leverage.

## Confusing access with opportunity

Knowing a prominent person or attending an expensive program does not itself produce value.

Access must be converted into:

* a relationship;
* a contribution;
* a collaboration;
* a learning asset;
* or an executable opportunity.

## Overstating credentials or results

Statements concerning Jay Abraham, Daymond John, clinical outcomes, research, or business results must be accurate and supportable.

## Premature monetization

A potentially valuable relationship can be damaged by proposing compensation before establishing relevance, trust, and mutual benefit.

## Misaligned partnerships

Not every chiropractor, dentist, marketer, or business coach is automatically a suitable partner.

The arrangement must have:

* complementary assets;
* aligned values;
* clear economics;
* defined roles;
* and enforceable expectations.

## Compliance risk

Creative deal structures must not override healthcare law, professional ethics, referral restrictions, or patient protections.
