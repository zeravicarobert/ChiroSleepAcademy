## 2. CENTRAL PARADIGM

Breathing is not merely pulmonary ventilation.

It is coordinated integration of:

1. **Neurology**
2. **Musculoskeletal biomechanics**
3. **Biochemistry**

Core distinctions:

* **Ventilation:** air movement.
* **Respiration:** gas exchange and cellular use.
* **Breathing:** total neurological, structural, muscular, airway, pressure, and chemical system.
* **The airway is structural.**
* Sleep apnea is not only an airflow, obesity, CPAP, dental, surgical, or pharmaceutical problem.
* It may involve cranial, cervical, mandibular, hyoid, thoracic, rib, diaphragmatic, abdominal, pelvic, neurological, and chemical dysfunction.

PRB’s challenge to conventional sleep medicine:

> Sleep medicine primarily tests, diagnoses, and manages apnea through sleep studies, CPAP, oral appliances, drugs, and surgery. PRB adds the missing biomechanical breathing infrastructure.

Intervention hierarchy:

* **Early:** structural and biomechanical intervention.
* **Mid:** equipment.
* **Late:** pharmaceuticals and surgery.

## 8. AUTOMATIC BREATHING CONTROL

The automatic controller is centered in the brainstem:

* **Medulla:** rhythm generation; DRG, VRG, pre-Bötzinger complex.
* **Pons:** pattern shaping.

Sensors:

* **Carotid bodies:** oxygen alarm sensors.
* **Medullary chemoreceptors:** CO₂/pH sensing.
* **Aortic bodies:** additional blood-gas sensing.

Motor pathways/effectors:

* phrenic nerve C3–C5 → diaphragm;
* intercostal nerves → rib mechanics;
* accessory muscles → scalenes, SCM, abdominals;
* hypoglossal nerve → tongue and airway dilators;
* vagal/glossopharyngeal systems → pharyngeal/laryngeal function;
* soft palate and pharyngeal muscles → airway stabilization.

Sleep apnea may combine:

* structural airway collapse;
* impaired tongue/airway-motor behavior;
* unstable respiratory drive;
* altered chemoreflex sensitivity;
* elevated loop gain;
* disturbed pressure mechanics;
* fragmented sleep.

The project rejects treating “software” and “hardware” as fully separate: neurological control operates through physical tissues, pressures, nerves, muscles, airway geometry, and chemistry.

## 9. SNORING

Preferred metaphor:

> **Snoring is the smoke alarm.**

Proposed model:

* restricted airway or cranial/structural movement contributes to turbulent airflow;
* soft-tissue flutter produces sound;
* vibration may transmit repetitive mechanical input through cranial and fascial structures;
* snoring warns of airflow instability, tissue vibration, sleep fragmentation, and possible airway collapse.

A more speculative concept suggested that vibration may also act as rhythmic mechanical stimulation; this remains hypothesis, not established therapy.

## 10. NITRIC OXIDE

Nasal breathing supports nitric-oxide delivery into inspired air.

NO is relevant to:

* vascular signaling;
* airway physiology;
* pulmonary blood-flow matching;
* antimicrobial defense;
* oxygen delivery;
* sleep and breathing regulation.

CSA/PRB messaging should connect nasal airway expansion, airflow, tongue posture, craniofacial mechanics, and nasal NO without overstating that manipulation directly “restores” systemic NO.

Preferred verbs remain **unsubluxate, expand, establish, stabilize**, rather than “restore.”
