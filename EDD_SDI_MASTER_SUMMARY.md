## 1. Project Identity

**Project name:** CA EDD SDI
**Project owner and clinical provider:** Dr. Robert Zeravica, DC
**Primary operating model:** California temporary-disability evaluation, certification, documentation, and claim-support services delivered primarily through telemedicine.

The project helps eligible California workers navigate **California Employment Development Department State Disability Insurance**, generally referred to as:

* CA EDD SDI
* CA SDI
* Temporary Disability Benefits
* California Worker Disability Certification

The preferred public-facing language is **"CA EDD SDI benefits"** or **"Temporary Disability Benefits."** The word **"disability"** should generally not be used alone when clearer language is available.

The project is not merely a form-completion service. It combines:

1. Clinical consultation and evaluation.
2. Diagnosis within Dr. Zeravica's lawful chiropractic scope.
3. Medical certification when clinically supported.
4. Claim-process guidance and case management.
5. Employer and supplemental-insurance documentation.
6. Ongoing monthly support when appropriate.
7. Screening for sleep apnea, snoring, and insomnia.
8. A potential longer-term personal or professional transition path for selected patients.

## 2. Core Mission

The project's central purpose is to help qualified California workers protect their income while they are temporarily unable to perform their customary work because of a clinically supported health condition.

The intended patient experience is:

**Confused, injured, financially vulnerable worker → organized consultation → documented diagnosis and functional limitations → appropriate certification → guided EDD process → supported recovery or transition.**

The project should position Dr. Zeravica as a knowledgeable clinical guide who understands both:

* The patient's condition and work limitations.
* The practical realities of the CA EDD SDI system.

It should not imply that benefits are guaranteed, that a diagnosis automatically proves work disability, or that every person seeking time away from work qualifies.

## 4. Core Patient Population

### Primary population

California W-2 workers who have paid into the State Disability Insurance system and are temporarily unable to perform their regular or customary work because of an appropriately documented condition.

### Important subgroups

* Union trade workers.
* Boilermakers.
* Pipefitters and plumbers.
* Electricians.
* Ironworkers.
* Sheet-metal workers.
* Teamsters and warehouse workers.
* Operating engineers.
* Laborers.
* Oil-field and industrial workers.
* Nurses and other physically demanding healthcare workers.
* School-district and public-sector employees where SDI coverage applies.
* Workers living outside California who earned qualifying California wages.
* Workers with private disability policies through companies such as AFLAC, MetLife, Colonial Life, or Guardian.
* Veterans who may separately require assistance with service-connected VA claims.

### Boilermaker context

Boilermakers are an important established population. Some work seasonal or project-based schedules, such as two or three months working followed by periods without work.

The project recognizes that:

* Some boilermakers legitimately become unable to continue working because of musculoskeletal conditions.
* Many do not understand the CA EDD SDI system.
* They need more than a narrow explanation of a claim form.
* They benefit from a broader "best-interest" overview of available benefit systems, work choices, recovery planning, and future options.

The project should never imply that workers should manufacture or exaggerate a condition to coordinate benefits with a preferred work schedule.

## 9. Clinical and Documentation Standard

The project's documentation principle is:

**Document only what was actually reported, observed, reviewed, or performed.**

Do not invent:

* Orthopedic tests.
* Neurological findings.
* Range-of-motion measurements.
* Palpation findings.
* Gait observations.
* Mental-status findings.
* Imaging interpretations.
* Functional limitations not stated by the patient.
* Physical-examination findings that cannot be established through the actual telemedicine encounter.

An uploaded "documented data only" SOAP note demonstrates this conservative standard. It records the patient's reported symptoms, aggravating and relieving factors, telehealth encounter, diagnosis, and plan without adding an unperformed physical examination.

A second uploaded note explicitly states that it uses only documented information and does not add unperformed examination findings.

### Recommended clinical reasoning chain

Every certification file should make the following relationship understandable:

**Condition → symptoms → objective or corroborating information where available → functional limitations → essential job duties affected → period of temporary inability to perform customary work → treatment and follow-up plan.**

A diagnosis alone is not enough. Imaging alone is not enough. The record should explain how the patient's condition limits the actual work they ordinarily perform.

Frequently discussed diagnostic codes include:

* M51-series codes — Intervertebral-disc disorders, selected according to documented findings.

## 11. Chiropractic Scope and Compliance

This is the project's highest-risk area.

The uploaded marketing plan states that chiropractors are recognized as practitioners for CA SDI certification but warns that certification must remain within chiropractic scope. It recommends confirmation with a California healthcare attorney before scaling and states that the professional license is the foundation of the business.

The project's compliance position should be:

* Dr. Zeravica may evaluate and certify conditions that fall within his lawful scope.
* The record must support the diagnosis, functional limitation, and period of incapacity.
* He should not certify a condition merely because EDD permits chiropractors to access the certification system.
* Conditions primarily outside chiropractic scope may require evaluation or certification by a physician or another appropriate practitioner.
* Complex diagnoses such as certain psychiatric, cardiovascular, infectious, obstetric, or vestibular conditions require special caution.
* Prior project experience includes a denial involving vertigo where chiropractic certification was challenged.
* Attorney review should be obtained for the overall business model, advertising claims, referral relationships, compensation arrangements, telehealth procedures, and scope boundaries.

This summary does not independently verify the legal interpretation contained in the uploaded plan. The plan's legal statements should be treated as issues requiring professional legal confirmation rather than final legal conclusions.

## 13. Standard Operational Workflow

### Stage 1: Referral or inquiry

Most leads arrive by phone or text through an existing patient or coworker.

The office gathers enough information to decide whether a formal consultation is appropriate. The office should not promise certification before the clinical evaluation.

### Stage 2: Pre-consultation intake

The patient provides:

* EDD receipt number when available.
* Dates of work and disability.
* Job requirements.
* Clinical records.
* Imaging.
* EDD notices.
* Employer or insurer forms.

### Stage 3: Telemedicine consultation

Dr. Zeravica:

* Confirms identity.
* Takes the history.
* Reviews symptoms and function.
* Reviews occupational demands.
* Reviews available records.
* Determines whether the condition is within scope.
* Establishes a diagnosis where clinically supported.
* Determines whether temporary work incapacity is supported.
* Establishes an appropriate follow-up plan.

### Stage 4: Documentation

Create a contemporaneous clinical note that identifies:

* Encounter method.
* Time spent.
* Subjective history.
* Information actually observed or reviewed.
* Assessment.
* Diagnosis.
* Functional restrictions.
* Work-status decision.
* Plan.

### Stage 5: Certification

When supported:

* Complete the practitioner portion.
* Submit online when the patient has supplied a valid receipt number.
* Use paper forms when required.
* Record the date and method of submission.
* Save copies or confirmation information.

### Stage 6: Follow-up and case management

Track:

* EDD requests.
* Identity-verification notices.
* Extension forms.
* Employer letters.
* Private-insurance forms.
* Appeals.
* Return-to-work changes.
* Work performed during the certified period.
* New conditions or material clinical changes.

### Stage 7: Return to work or extension

At each follow-up, determine whether the patient:

* Remains unable to perform regular work.
* Can perform modified work.
* Can return part-time.
* Can return without restriction.
* Needs referral to another practitioner.
* Requires a new claim rather than continuation of the existing claim.

Project conversations repeatedly reference:

* **DE 2500A:** Continued-claim or extension-related documentation.
* **DE 2517:** Identity-verification request.
* Supplemental practitioner forms or EDD requests for additional information.
* Appeal forms following a denial.
* Employer letters.

Common problems include:

* EDD paperwork arriving after the stated response deadline.
* Documents delayed or lost in transit.
* Identity documents repeatedly requested.
* Driver's license and utility-bill verification.
* Certification submitted late.
* Claimant mailing evidence more than once.
* No tracking update for mailed documents.
* Denial entered before replacement documents were processed.
* Claimants being unable to reach EDD by telephone.
* Work performed during a claimed disability period.
* Workers' compensation overlap.
* Unemployment and SDI timing conflicts.
* Confusion between Paid Family Leave and Disability Insurance.
* Provider-type or scope challenges.
* Claims reaching the maximum payable period.
* Attempted continuation after a significant return-to-work interval.

### Mailing and appeal evidence

Claimants should retain:

* Copies of everything mailed.
* Tracking receipts.
* Screenshots of carrier history.
* EDD notices and envelopes.
* Dates documents were first received.
* Dates documents were mailed.
* Delivery confirmations.
* Copies of explanatory letters.
* Names or reference numbers from any calls.

A recurring project principle is to create a simple timeline showing that the claimant acted promptly, even when mail delivery or EDD processing caused the delay.

## 15. Claim-Timing Concepts

Project conversations have used the following operational concepts:

* The claimant identifies the requested disability start date, but the practitioner must independently determine whether the date is clinically supportable.
* The claim effective date is important for the available benefit period.
* A substantial return-to-work period may require a new claim rather than an extension.
* The project has previously used a 60-day return-to-work threshold as a practical distinction, but this should be checked against current EDD rules in each case.
* California SDI generally replaces a percentage of qualifying wages, but the exact percentage and maximum weekly benefit change over time.
* Paid Family Leave and Disability Insurance serve different purposes and generally should not be treated as simultaneous benefits for the same period.
* Unemployment represents being ready and available to work, whereas DI represents inability to perform regular work; overlapping representations can create problems.
* Working during a certified period must be accurately reported.
* A claimant cannot simply choose a date for strategic reasons if the clinical evidence does not support incapacity beginning on that date.

Any precise benefit percentage, contribution rate, wage base, maximum payment, filing deadline, or current form requirement should be verified against current official EDD information before being placed in patient-facing materials.

## 16. Appeals and Denials

The project supports patients in organizing appeals but does not act as a law firm.

A strong appeal package may include:

1. The denial notice.
2. A concise appeal statement.
3. A dated chronology.
4. The original clinical certification.
5. Updated clinical records.
6. Imaging or specialist records where relevant.
7. Functional-capacity explanation.
8. Proof of timely mailing or attempted compliance.
9. Copies of identity documents.
10. Tracking and delivery evidence.
11. Explanation of delays outside the claimant's control.
12. A request that EDD reconsider the decision based on the complete record.

The appeal should distinguish between:

* A procedural denial.
* Missing documentation.
* Identity failure.
* Late submission.
* Clinical insufficiency.
* Scope or provider-type objection.
* Benefit exhaustion.
* Work or wage conflict.
* Overpayment.

The office should not tell a patient that filing an appeal automatically reverses the recorded denial. Automated telephone systems may continue to state "denied" until the appeal is formally processed and decided.

The Stay-at-Home model has included basic language such as:

At times, the project has described 90-day phases or multiple 90-day stages. Those stages should be presented as care-planning or coaching concepts—not as automatic entitlement to a specific duration of benefits.

The clinical record must support each certified period independently.

## 19. Difference Between CA EDD SDI and Workers' Compensation

The project treats these as distinct systems.

### CA EDD SDI

Generally concerns wage replacement for a non-work-related condition that temporarily prevents a worker from performing regular or customary work.

### Workers' compensation

Generally concerns work-caused injury or illness and includes a different regulatory and treatment structure.

Prior project discussions have identified workers' compensation concepts such as:

* Primary treating physician.
* Doctor's First Report.
* PR-2 reports.
* Requests for authorization.
* Utilization review.
* Permanent and stationary status.
* Work restrictions.
* Treatment-visit limitations.

Those workers' compensation procedures should not automatically be imported into an EDD SDI case.

However, when a condition may be work-related, the office must carefully determine whether a workers' compensation referral or disclosure is required.
