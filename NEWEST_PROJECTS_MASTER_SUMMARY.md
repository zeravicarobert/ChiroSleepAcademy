# 1. Executive Summary

The project is evolving from a collection of related Dr. Z businesses into a deliberately separated ecosystem with three primary components:

1. **CA EDD SDI and worker-disability certification**
   An established, remote, revenue-producing professional service that Dr. Zeravica intends to retain and allow to grow primarily through existing-patient referrals rather than aggressive expansion.

2. **Dr. Robert Zeravica, DC / VAET at Tecopa**
   The personal-authority and patient-treatment brand centered on Dr. Zeravica's clinical identity, airway biomechanics, cranial work, structural breathing, VAET, lifestyle, and in-person patient experiences.

3. **Chiropractic Sleep Academy—CSA**
   The future-growth, intellectual-property, professional-education, research, data-collection, and category-creation vehicle intended to establish chiropractic as the missing early biomechanical intervention within sleep, airway, and sleep-apnea care.

The central strategic change is that **SDI is no longer supposed to consume the project's growth energy**. It remains valuable and must be made legally defensible, compliant, documented, efficient, and operationally stable. However, the primary creative and expansion effort is moving toward:

* the Dr. Z personal brand;
* VAET;
* Chiropractic Sleep Academy;
* professional education for chiropractors;
* measurable treatment outcomes;
* conference visibility;
* and development of a defensible body of intellectual property and evidence.

* what Peter is responsible for;

* how he earns into CSA participation;

## Project owner

**Dr. Robert Zeravica, DC**, also referred to as Rob or Dr. Z.

* and developer of original language, frameworks, offers, teaching systems, and clinical concepts.

## Core strategic thesis

Modern sleep medicine generally enters after a sleep or airway problem is established and focuses on testing, equipment, appliances, medication, or surgery.

Dr. Z's proposed category reframes the sequence:

* **Early intervention:** biomechanics, cranial structure, spinal structure, airway space, tongue function, posture, respiratory mechanics, neurological coordination, and structural breathing stability.
* **Mid intervention:** CPAP, oral appliances, sleep testing, and other equipment-supported management.
* **Late intervention:** medication, invasive procedures, and surgery.

CSA's message is not that existing sleep medicine is entirely wrong. It is that the prevailing model is **incomplete because it underrepresents the biomechanical and structural breathing system**.

The proposed strategic position is therefore:

> Chiropractic can occupy the missing early-intervention position in sleep, airway, and breathing-system care.

CSA is not intended to be merely another sleep-apnea course.

> The chiropractic assessment and correction of the structural breathing system.

The category includes two interacting dimensions:

## Structural-mechanical dimension

* nasal and airway space;
* cranial relationships;
* cervical structure;
* forward-head posture;
* rib cage;
* diaphragm;
* abdominal pressure;
* tongue posture;
* soft palate;
* jaw and TMJ;
* upper-airway dilator mechanics;
* spinal and pelvic influences.

## Neurological-control dimension

* brainstem respiratory centers;
* medulla;
* pons;
* pre-Bötzinger complex;
* chemoreceptor response;
* loop gain;
* hypoglossal motor output;
* phrenic pathways;
* cranial nerve involvement;

CSA's concept does not reduce one side to "hardware" and the other to "software." Both are embodied biological systems. The more accurate distinction is:

* **airway-container and mechanical-effector problems**, and
* **respiratory-control and neuromotor-coordination problems**.
