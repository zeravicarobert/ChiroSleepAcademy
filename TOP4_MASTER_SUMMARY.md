## 1. Executive Summary

**Top4 is Dr. Robert Zeravica's project-management and execution system.** It is not merely one clinical program or business offer. Its purpose is to compress a large number of projects, ideas, obligations, and reminders into a small, controlled set of active priorities.

The central problem Top4 is designed to solve is not lack of ideas, strategy, education, or opportunity. It is **execution dilution** caused by:

* Too many simultaneous initiatives.
* Blurred boundaries between projects, roles, ideas, and tasks.
* Repeated reconsideration of decisions.
* Perfectionism and excessive advisory input.
* Emotional and administrative background load.
* Treating everything important as though it must be a current priority.

The intended operating principle is:

> **Only a few projects should receive active execution at one time. Everything else must be classified, parked, delegated, sequenced, or deliberately left alone.**

The project currently contains two intertwined layers:

1. **The Top4 operating system itself** — prioritization, project classification, weekly focus, delegation, metrics, and decision control.
2. **The businesses and initiatives being managed through Top4**, principally:

   * CA EDD SDI clinical operations.
   * ChiroCat-to-ChiroTouch migration.
   * SimpleTalk implementation.
   * EDD database management.
   * Chiropractic Sleep Academy™ and VAET.
   * The Peter/Dr. Z CSA participation and operating agreement.
   * Dr. Z's personal and professional brand.
   * Future clinical research, evidence gathering, and sleep-conference positioning.

Top4 exists to help Dr. Z:

This includes clinical evaluation, disability certification, documentation, EDD servicing, patient communication, extensions, return-to-work processes, billing, and operational compliance.

* Intake, scheduling, portal, and communication systems.

### 3.3 Future Projects

Legitimate projects that are not supposed to compete with the current operating spine.

Examples include:

* VAET education and DC training.
* Dentist collaboration.
* Sleep-conference strategy.
* CSA practitioner education.
* Formal clinical data registry.
* Content and influencer strategy.
* Tecopa clinical experience.
* CSA licensing, certification, or membership models.

### 3.4 Idea Parking Lot

Promising concepts that have not yet earned active-project status.

The parking lot protects ideas from being lost while also protecting active execution from interruption.

### 3.5 Tasks

Individual actions that belong inside an established project.

Examples:

* Call a vendor.
* Review a contract clause.
* Export a patient list.
* Test a telephone workflow.
* Draft an intake question.

A task should not be treated as its own project unless it has multiple dependent actions, a defined outcome, and an owner.

### 3.6 Reminders

Date- or condition-based prompts rather than projects.

Examples:

* Renew a service.
* Follow up with an attorney.
* Check on a document.
* Call a patient on a specified date.

### 3.7 Things Pretending to Be Projects

Items that feel significant but lack:

* A defined outcome.
* A completion condition.
* An owner.
* A current time horizon.
* A reason to be active now.

This category is important because many of Dr. Z's ideas are strategically meaningful but are not yet operational projects.

# 4. Operating Philosophy

## 4.1 Importance Is Not Priority

Top4 distinguishes between:

* Something being valuable.
* Something belonging in the future.
* Something that must be done now.

CSA, VAET, research, speaking, education, personal branding, and broader ecosystem development may all be important. That does not mean they should all receive equal execution attention during the same week or quarter.

## 4.2 Sequence Matters

One uploaded strategic plan strongly argues that the existing certification business should finance later clinical infrastructure:

> "The V.A.E.T. Phase 1 at $3,500 is a beautiful vision — and the certification revenue is what funds it. Sequence matters."

The same source treats the certification arm and the physical VAET/Tecopa experience as separate businesses with different requirements. The certification operation can function remotely, while VAET requires physical space, clinical infrastructure, and a higher patient commitment.

This is an external strategic recommendation, not necessarily Dr. Z's final current decision. More recent project discussions indicate that Dr. Z does **not** want to aggressively expand CA EDD SDI through active marketing. He prefers that it continue primarily through organic referrals while he develops:

1. Dr. Robert Zeravica, DC as a brand.
2. VAET at Tecopa.
3. Chiropractic Sleep Academy.
4. Clinical outcome data.
5. A conference and professional-authority strategy.

Therefore, the confirmed principle is **sequence and protection of the funding engine**; the unresolved issue is whether the EDD business should be actively scaled or deliberately maintained as an organic-referral operation.

## 4.3 Execution Before More Advice

A recurring diagnosis in the project record is that Dr. Z needs execution capacity more than additional strategic input.

The uploaded Alex Mandossian plan says:

> Dr. Z "needs executors more than additional advisors right now."

This aligns with the Top4 division of labor:

* **Dr. Z:** vision, strategy, clinical authority, major decisions, relationships, intellectual property, and final approval.
* **Executor/operations person:** implementation, follow-up, systems, task completion, documentation, and maintenance.

Recommended structure:

1. What was completed?
2. What is blocked?
3. What decisions are required from Dr. Z?
4. What is the one primary project for the coming week?
5. What measurable result will define a successful week?

The prior operating sequence was:

* **Week 1:** ChiroTouch.
* **Week 2:** SimpleTalk.
* **Week 3:** EDD spreadsheet/database.

This was intended to prevent the executor from being spread across all projects simultaneously.

## 6.5 Decision Log

The Decision Log exists to stop repeated reconsideration.

Each entry should contain:

* Decision.
* Date.
* Decision maker.
* Reason.
* Assumptions.
* What the decision replaces.
* Conditions that would justify reopening it.
* Related project.
* Required implementation action.

The desired intervention ladder is:

* **Early:** structural and biomechanical intervention.
* **Mid:** devices and equipment.
* **Late:** medication and surgery, when indicated.

This is a strategic positioning framework, not yet a validated comparative-treatment hierarchy.
