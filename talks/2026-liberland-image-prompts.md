# Image prompts — Liberland talk ("Three Things That Keep You In Alignment")

Paste any block into ChatGPT (or another image tool) as-is.

**Warning:** image tools garble text. Keep words few and check every letter. If
the text comes out wrong, ask for the image with **no text** and add the words
yourself in Canva.

---

## OPENING IMAGES

Job of the opening image: hold the room while Robert talks over it. Almost no
words — if there is text, they read it instead of listening.

**Run order:** open on A in silence for three seconds. Then talk. Switch to B on
the line "geometry governs breath."

### A — The eight hours nobody watches (recommended)

```
Create a quiet, cinematic illustration for the opening slide of a health talk.

A single human head and shoulders in side profile, lying on a pillow in a dark room, asleep, calm. The face is simplified and anonymous — line art, not a photograph, not a specific person.

The only light in the image is a soft luminous channel tracing the breathing passage: entering at the nose, curving behind the palate, down through the throat. It glows gently, like a lit path through the dark.

Everything else is deep, quiet darkness with a hint of blue.

Mood: still, serious, beautiful. Not clinical. Not scary. Not spiritual or new-age. No glowing chakras, no auras, no energy beams.

No text anywhere in the image. Wide 16:9.
```

### B — The title card

```
Create a minimal title slide for a professional talk.

Deep navy background, almost black. Centered, in clean modern serif type, one line only:

GEOMETRY GOVERNS BREATH. BREATH GOVERNS SLEEP.

Below it, much smaller and in thin gold letters: DR. ROBERT ZERAVICA, DC

A single thin gold horizontal rule between the two lines. Nothing else — no icons, no illustration, no decoration.

Enormous empty space around the text. Confident and restrained, the way a serious conference slide looks.

Wide 16:9. Spelling exactly as written.
```

### C — The single breath line

```
Create a striking, simple opening image for a talk about breathing.

A single human profile in thin line art, upright, eyes closed, seen from the side. Emerging from the nose is one continuous flowing line that curves and loops away across the frame, suggesting a breath in motion — elegant, unbroken, like a single pen stroke.

Background: warm off-white, almost paper. The line work is deep navy with one thin gold accent along the breath line.

Mood: calm, intelligent, gallery-quality. Minimal. Enormous empty space.

No text. No medical labels. No anatomy detail. Wide 16:9.
```

## THE TEACHING IMAGE (worth the most effort)

Teaches the whole model in one picture. Print it — it works on stage with no
screen.

```
Create a clear medical education illustration comparing two side-profile views of a human head and neck, shown side by side.

LEFT PANEL: head balanced over the shoulders. The breathing passage from the nose, behind the palate, down the throat is highlighted as a smooth, open channel. Small caption: "OPEN"

RIGHT PANEL: the same head pushed forward, chin down, as if looking at a phone. The same breathing passage is now visibly narrowed and bent at the throat. Small caption: "NARROWED"

Style: clean anatomical line art, the way a good textbook diagram looks. Muted colors, one accent color for the airway channel only so the eye goes straight to it. Neutral, factual, non-alarming.

No brand names. No text beyond the two captions.
```

## THE THREE STEPS

```
Create a clean, professional health-education graphic. Three steps, left to right, on one horizontal line.

STEP 1: a simple line-art human head in side profile, mouth closed, with a soft arrow showing air entering through the nose. Label underneath: "CLOSE YOUR MOUTH"
STEP 2: the same head, now upright and stacked over the shoulders, with a faint "ghost" version behind it slumped forward to show the contrast. Label underneath: "LIFT YOUR HEAD"
STEP 3: the same head with a small, simple sleep-tracking ring icon beside it and a gentle rising line graph. Label underneath: "FIND OUT"

Style: minimal medical line art, thin confident strokes, lots of white space, deep navy and warm gold on an off-white background. Calm and clinical, not cartoonish. No stock-photo people. No clutter.

Keep all text minimal and spelled exactly as written. Wide format, 16:9.
```

## THE HANDOUT CARD

```
Design a simple vertical card, like a printed handout, with a calm and premium feel.

Then three numbered lines with plenty of space between them:
1. CLOSE YOUR MOUTH
2. LIFT YOUR HEAD
3. FIND OUT

At the bottom, small: "Dr. Robert Zeravica, DC"

Style: off-white paper texture, deep navy text, one thin gold rule between sections. Generous margins. Elegant and understated, like a good clinic's stationery. No icons, no photos, no decoration.

Portrait format, spelling exactly as written.
```

```

Right third: clean space for text reading "THREE THINGS THAT KEEP YOU IN ALIGNMENT"

Style: modern conference branding. Deep navy background, warm gold line work, high contrast, plenty of empty space. Serious and professional. Not medical, not spiritual, not fitness-industry.

Leave the lower right corner clear for a speaker name to be added later.
```

## House style, if you generate anything else

- Deep navy, warm gold, off-white. Thin confident line art.
- Enormous empty space. Minimal is the whole look.
- Never: stock-photo people, cartoon style, glowing auras or chakras, red
  alarm-colored anatomy, fear imagery.
- Never put a claim in an image. Images show structure — they do not promise
  outcomes.
