## 1. What This Is About

Rob and Peter are discussing Peter's possible role in two separate business
lanes:

1. **Rob's existing California EDD SDI (State Disability Insurance) disability
   business** — already real, revenue-generating, and owned by Rob. Peter did
   not create it and is not asking for income from it.
2. **Chiropractic Sleep Academy (CSA)** — the emerging future business
   ecosystem (sleep, airway, chiropractic education, VAET, high-ticket
   treatment, lectures, memberships, products, affiliates, events, and related
   revenue). Peter wants a role in building and monetizing this future CSA
   umbrella.

The goal of the discussion is a **written agreement / draft contract** between
Rob and Peter, signed by both, and later reviewed by an attorney. The agreement
should:

- Clarify Peter's role.
- Protect Rob's existing business and intellectual property.
- Protect Peter for the value he creates.
- Define how Peter earns into CSA participation.
- Create a performance / warning / exit structure if Rob is not satisfied.

### Core Principle

> **SDI is Rob's existing asset. CSA is the future growth vehicle. Peter's SDI
> help is proof-of-value, not SDI ownership.**
>
> **Rob keeps what he already created. Peter can earn into what he helps build.**

SDI** — to demonstrate value, reduce Rob's workload, free Rob's time for CSA,

Peter's SDI-related help may include:

   agencies, or other outside entities requesting disability-related forms.

   viable, saleable, defensible, compliant, and audit-ready.
8. Watching Rob operate on his laptop (Simple Talk, ChiroCat, ChiroTouch)

### Rob's Reordered Priorities for Peter

**Primary first executive function:**

> Help make Rob's business as legally protected, defensible, compliant,
> audit-ready, and "bulletproof" as reasonably possible — **with attorney
> involvement.**

**Second priority:**

> Peter should **observe Rob's actual workflow first** — laptop, Simple Talk,
> ChiroCat, ChiroTouch, payment systems, patient communication, forms,

**Honest-wording guardrail:** No business can literally be guaranteed
"bulletproof" or "audit-proof." The realistic, contractable goal is to make the
business as **defensible, organized, documented, compliant, and legally
protected as reasonably possible.**

## 6. Peter's 40% Request — Reframed as Earned, Not Given

Peter initially wanted 40%. Clarified framing:

> **Peter may earn *up to* 40% of defined CSA economic participation through
> performance-based milestones, vesting, and continued contribution.**

Peter's 40% request spans: (1) equity / ownership, (2) net-profit
participation, (3) sale proceeds — but **not** immediately and **not** vaguely.

**Peter's logic:** He hasn't created the revenue yet because he doesn't want to
build the full business machine before written terms exist. He wants terms
*before* execution so he doesn't lose leverage after building it.

**Rob's protection:** Peter should not hold 40% forever before the business
exists and before performance is proven.

### The Central Compromise

> **Peter can earn toward 40%, but it must be milestone-based, vested,
> conditional, and revocable for underperformance.**

## 11. Performance, Warnings, Cure & Termination

Both parties want a structure that protects Rob if he's not satisfied with
Peter's performance. Because some concerns are subjective, warnings and cure
terms should be **made as objective as possible.**

### Written-Warning Structure

1. Rob may issue written warnings if Peter fails to meet duties, deliverables,
   communication standards, timelines, or performance expectations.
2. Each warning identifies:
   - The specific duty or deliverable not met
   - The facts supporting the concern
   - The correction required
   - The deadline to cure
   - The consequence if not cured
3. Peter gets a reasonable cure period unless the issue is serious misconduct.
4. Proposed cure periods:
   - **7 days** — communication / admin issues
   - **14 days** — operational issues
   - **30 days** — larger deliverables
5. **Three unresolved written warnings** within a defined period could let Rob
   terminate Peter's acting role, future vesting, and future CSA participation.

### Immediate Termination Without Cure (serious issues)

1. Dishonesty
2. Fraud
3. Theft
4. HIPAA / privacy violation
5. Misuse of patient data
6. Unauthorized clinical claims
7. Unauthorized use of Rob's name, image, likeness, or professional identity
8. Unauthorized financial commitments
9. Unauthorized contracts
10. Damage to Rob's license or reputation
11. Legal exposure
12. Refusal to follow written limits of authority

### Making Subjective Concerns Objective

- **"Losing bearing"** = failing to follow agreed priorities, repeatedly
  shifting away from deliverables, creating confusion, missing meetings,
  failing to provide updates, or acting outside approved authority.
- **"Overstepping"** = speaking for Rob without approval, making promises to
  third parties, publishing content without approval, contacting patients
  outside approved workflows, or claiming authority not granted in writing.

> protected. Rob wants protection **before** granting participation because CSA
