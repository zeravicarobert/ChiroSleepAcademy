sold back to Robert for $7,500.**

## 4. The Case Against

Stated plainly, because $7,500 before a website exists deserves scrutiny.

1. **This audience is not CSA's customer.** CSA sells to **chiropractors**
   (CLAUDE.md § 3). Anarchapulco is a liberty/crypto/health-freedom crowd with
   close to zero DCs in it. Every dollar here buys patient revenue and
   visibility — not doctor-training pipeline. That is a legitimate thing to buy.
   It is not the 90-day goal.
2. **The funnel still does not exist.** The site isn't live. Goal 6 from May is
   still open. Sending a 45-minute main-stage audience to a destination that
   doesn't exist yet repeats the Liberpulco leak at four times the cost. **If
   Robert commits to February, the site has to be live before February — that
   becomes a hard dependency, not an aspiration.**
3. **Opportunity cost.** $7,500 is roughly a Tecopa VAET trial's gross, or a
   serious chunk of a real DC-facing launch. It could also fund the Texas / Alec
   Zeck trip several times over — and Alec's audience is warmer, cheaper, and
   already booked.
4. **Early Bird urgency is a sales mechanic.** September 15 is their deadline,
   not a law of nature. It is fine to treat it as real, and also fine to ask
   what happens on September 16.

## 5. Risk Items to Clear Before Signing

Not reasons to decline — reasons to do the homework first.

1. **Practicing on patients in Mexico.** "Turning the conference into a real
   working clinic" means rendering hands-on care in a foreign country. Confirm
   with counsel and with the malpractice carrier: does the policy cover
   treatment rendered outside the US? Most do not by default. Whatever the
   answer, get it in writing before paying.
2. **Informed consent and documentation.** Event sessions still need an intake,
   a consent form, and a record — the same discipline as the clinic. This also
   protects the outcomes-data story CSA is building.
3. **Scope framing on stage.** A main stage is exactly where a cure claim would
   do the most damage. The talk must hold every § 5 red line: no cure claims, no
   diagnosis, no attacks on CPAP, oral appliances, ENTs, or sleep medicine.
   Write the talk against those lines from the first draft, not as a later edit.
4. **What is a session at an event?** Define it in writing before selling any:
   a **test experience**, not a treatment plan and not a case. Same discipline
   as the Tecopa Step 4 inclusion/exclusion list.

## 6. Questions to Send Miranda Now

Sending these commits to nothing and costs nothing. They also signal a serious
counterparty rather than an easy yes.

1. **Attendance.** What was 2026's attendance, and what is projected for 2027?
   How did the move to Puerto Vallarta affect it?
2. **Recording rights.** Is the 45-minute main-stage talk professionally
   recorded, and may Robert use the recording freely on his own channels?
   *(This is the single most important question in the list.)*
3. **Lead capture.** Do sponsors receive attendee contact details, or a
   scan/opt-in mechanism? Can the Calendly capture email addresses Robert keeps?
4. **Clinic logistics.** Square footage, privacy, a table or plinth, power,
   sink/water access, hours of access, security for equipment overnight.
5. **Workshops.** How many, how long, what room capacity, and are they on the
   printed programme?
6. **Payment terms.** Is the $7,500 payable in installments? What is the refund
   or transfer position if the event moves or Robert cannot attend?
7. **Price.** Is there flexibility on the rate, or on what's included at that
   rate, for a first-time sponsor?
8. **After September 15.** What does the same package cost then? Useful to know
   the real size of the discount.
9. **Who else is sponsoring in health?** Is there another airway, sleep, dental,
   or bodywork sponsor — competing or complementary?
