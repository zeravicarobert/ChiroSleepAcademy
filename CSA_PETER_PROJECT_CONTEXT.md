A preferred concept is:

## Participation Concept

Peter may earn into a maximum **40% CSA participation**.

The 40% should not be treated as:

* An immediate gift
* Automatic ownership
* Unconditional equity
* Compensation merely for being present
* Ownership of Dr. Z's preexisting businesses or intellectual property

The participation must be tied to written conditions and measurable performance.

## Need to Define "Participation"

The term "participation" remains legally ambiguous and must be defined.

It could refer to one or more of the following:

* Equity ownership
* Membership interest
* Profit participation
* Revenue participation
* Phantom equity
* Contractual economic interest
* Vesting rights
* Voting rights
* Management rights

The agreement must not use "40% participation" without explaining exactly what Peter receives and does not receive.

## Earn-In Structure

The preferred model is performance-based vesting or an earn-in process.

Potential components include:

* Time-based service
* Milestone completion
* Creation of defined systems
* Legal/compliance implementation
* Revenue or profitability thresholds
* Product-launch milestones
* Documentation standards
* Research infrastructure
* Team formation
* Operational independence
* Reporting and accountability
* Good-standing requirements

The final milestone schedule has not yet been established.

## Written Warning Letters

Peter likes the idea that Dr. Z can issue written warning letters if Peter is not meeting the contractual conditions required to earn into the 40% CSA participation.

The warning process should:

* Identify the unmet condition
* State the supporting facts
* Cite the relevant agreement provision
* Explain what corrective action is required
* Provide a reasonable cure period
* State what happens if the problem is not cured
* Create a written record
* Permit Peter to respond
* Prevent arbitrary forfeiture based only on emotion or undocumented dissatisfaction

Warnings should not be used as a weapon or as a substitute for objective performance standards.

The agreement should distinguish among:

* Minor performance deficiencies
* Material breach
* Misconduct
* Fraud
* Confidentiality violations
* Unauthorized use of funds or assets
* Abandonment of duties
* Incapacity
* Good-faith disagreement
* Failure caused by Dr. Z, lack of resources, or external conditions

## Protection of Earned Rights

Once participation is validly earned and vested, the agreement should define whether it can later be forfeited and under what limited circumstances.

There should be a distinction between:

* Earned and vested interests
* Unearned future participation
* Compensation already owed
* Reimbursable expenses
* Future profit distributions
* Ownership of work product
* Rights after termination
