## Review areas (the scope)

### 1. HIPAA + California CMIA
- Federal HIPAA **and** California's Confidentiality of Medical
  Information Act (the California layer on top of HIPAA).
- Secure/encrypted platforms for telehealth and file sharing.
- Storage of patient data and sleep-test reports for long-term,
  de-identified outcomes tracking.
- Business Associate Agreements with HST/imaging/tech vendors.

### 2. California telehealth rules
- Standard-of-care, informed-consent, and documentation requirements
  for telehealth delivered by a **DC** in California.
- What a chiropractor may and may not do over telehealth in California.
- Cross-state considerations if a doctor-client or patient is out of
  state during a Zoom consult.

### 3. Scope of practice (DC-specific)
- California Board of Chiropractic Examiners scope boundaries.
- Keeping the "structural, not diagnostic" line clean around sleep and
  airway.
- Referral logic and co-management language with DDS / MD / ENT / sleep
  medicine that does not overstate the DC role.

### 4. Disability / ADA requirements
- ADA and California accessibility obligations for the practice, the
  website/funnel, and telehealth delivery (including web accessibility).
- Reasonable-accommodation and effective-communication requirements.

### 5. Documentation
- Clinical documentation standards for the CSA workflow:
  Consult → Structural Airway Exam → Home Sleep Test → Report of
  Findings → Case Presentation.
- Informed consent, financial consent, and records-retention practices.

### 6. Marketing / advertising claims (the "ethics proofread")
This is central. Please review all public-facing copy against
**FTC advertising-substantiation rules** and **California Business &
Professions Code §651** (false/misleading advertising by licensed
practitioners, including chiropractors).
- Landing page and website (ClickFunnels 2.0), email sequences, offer
  pages, social copy, workbook/lead-magnet content.
- My own hard red lines I want enforced: **no cure claims**, never claim
  chiropractic cures sleep apnea, no attacks on CPAP/oral
  appliances/surgery/MDs/DDS/sleep medicine, no exaggerated evidence,
  honest about what is proven vs. still being proven.
- Substantiation review: does my evidence actually support each claim.

### 7. Payment methods — HRA / HSA cards
CSA accepts **HRA (Health Reimbursement Arrangement) cards and HSA
(Health Savings Account) cards** as methods of payment.
- Which CSA services qualify as IRS "qualified medical expenses" and
  can legitimately be paid with HSA/HRA funds.
- Substantiation and documentation required (e.g., whether a Letter of
  Medical Necessity is needed, itemized receipts, coding).
- How to keep education/coaching revenue vs. clinical-service revenue
  properly separated so HSA/HRA card acceptance is compliant and not
  misrepresented.
- Any advertising-claims risk in *how* HSA/HRA eligibility is presented
  to patients and doctor-clients.

### 8. Business structure & pathway (flag for scope)
- CSA sells a stepped pathway (free alignment call → paid sleep/airway
  baselines → VAET trial → guided case plan) **and** a doctor
  training/representation side. I want the terms, disclosures, and
  exclusions reviewed for consistency and compliance.
- There is a possible operating-partner arrangement (equity/vested,
  revocable) that may need corporate/contract review — please tell me
  if that is inside your practice or needs separate counsel.
