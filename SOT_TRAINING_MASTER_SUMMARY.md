## 1. Project Identity

This folder develops **Dr. Robert Zeravica, DC’s modern SOT-centered chiropractic system**, integrating:

* Sacro Occipital Technique (SOT)
* Applied Kinesiology (AK)
* Cranial Facial Release (CFR)
* Primary Respiratory Mechanism/Impulse (PRM/PRI)
* Craniosacral, TMJ, pelvic, postural, airway, neurological, and autonomic biomechanics
* Sleep-disordered breathing and obstructive sleep apnea
* **VAET — Viscerocranial Airway Expansion Therapy**
* **CSA — Chiropractic Sleep Academy**

The central mission is to make SOT and cranial biomechanics clinically understandable, professionally credible, measurable, and relevant to modern sleep and airway care.

## 2. Core Thesis

Modern sleep medicine often emphasizes:

* Testing
* CPAP and equipment
* Oral appliances
* Medication
* Surgery

Dr. Z’s position is that this model is incomplete because it often underemphasizes **structural breathing biomechanics**.

The body’s airway, breathing, sleep, cranial motion, nervous system, posture, pelvis, TMJ, tongue, cervical spine, respiratory muscles, and autonomic control function as one integrated system.

**Core message:**

> Biomechanics is the missing pillar in sleep and airway care.

Dr. Z’s intervention is positioned as **early structural intervention**:

* Early: chiropractic, posture, cranial, airway and neurological correction
* Mid: CPAP, oral appliances and other equipment
* Late: medication and surgery

## 4. SOT Training Framework

The project seeks to modernize and mainstream SOT through a **Skull-Down** model rather than treating the pelvis, spine or skull as isolated regions.

Major components include:

* SOT pelvic Categories I, II and III
* Pelvic blocking
* Sacrum–occiput relationships
* Lovett Brother relationships:

  * Sacrum–occiput
  * L5–C1
  * Coccyx–sphenoid
  * TMJ–sacroiliac relationships
* Logan Basic and ilio-tuberous ligament work
* Cranial sutures and reciprocal tension membranes
* Dural tension and the “dural trampoline”
* CSF movement and “Liquid Light”
* PRM/PRI
* Viscerocranium versus neurocranium
* TMJ mechanics
* Tongue and hypoglossal nerve function
* Cervical lordosis and forward-head posture
* Foot, pelvis, spine, cranial and airway integration
* Proprioceptive and post-adjustment stabilization
* Whole-body vibration where appropriate

The system should explain not merely **what adjustment is performed**, but:

1. What biomechanical dysfunction is suspected.
2. How it affects breathing, sleep or neurological control.
3. How it is assessed.
4. What correction is applied.
5. What objective change follows.

## 5. VAET

**VAET = Viscerocranial Airway Expansion Therapy.**

VAET is Dr. Z’s preferred clinical brand for his advanced cranial-airway work.

It incorporates:

* CFR/BNS-style balloon procedures
* SOT and AK
* Cranial, facial and TMJ biomechanics
* Cervical and postural correction
* Pelvic and whole-body integration
* Airway-aperture expansion
* Sinus and post-nasal drainage concepts
* CSF and cranial-fluid dynamics
* Structural breathing stability

VAET is intended as a concentrated **two-day cash service**, preferably delivered by Dr. Z near **Tecopa Hot Springs/Las Vegas**, followed by approximately three months of coordinated local care.

Qualified alternatives may include experienced CFR or BNS practitioners, but CSA remains the coordinating hub.

## 6. Ongoing Local Care

Post-VAET care should involve a qualified local chiropractor with training in:

* SOT or AK craniopathy
* Pelvic categories and blocking
* Craniosacral techniques
* CBP-style cervical lordosis and forward-head correction
* Foot Levelers/postural-chain concepts
* Disc Centers of America or equivalent referral relationships when needed

The dental provider should preferably participate in an airway, sleep or craniofacial-pain organization such as AACFP or a comparable group.

A flexible **CSA Provider Qualification Checklist** should be created now and later become part of formal CSA training and certification.

## 7. Sleep and Breathing Physiology

The folder treats sleep apnea as both **mechanical and neurological**.

Important physiology includes:

* Brainstem respiratory control in the medulla and pons
* Central and peripheral chemoreception
* Hypoglossal nerve and tongue-dilator function
* Phrenic, intercostal and accessory respiratory motor pathways
* Diaphragm, chest wall and upper-airway mechanical effectors
* Loop gain and ventilatory instability
* Autonomic regulation
* Respiratory reflex arcs
* CSF and venous pressure transmission
* Abdominal, spinal and cranial mechanical coupling
* Nitric oxide from nasal breathing
* Airway resistance and collapsibility
* Sleep-stage effects on neuromuscular tone

Guyton physiology should ground the curriculum:

> Guyton Physiology for the Skull-Down Doctor

This provides conventional physiological support for respiratory mechanics, autonomics, CSF, reflexes, sleep and neurological control.

## 8. Clinical Evaluation

Proposed assessment elements include:

* Sleep and snoring history
* Witnessed apnea, gasping and daytime sleepiness
* Home sleep testing, preferably two nights
* AHI and event duration
* Hypopneas and central events
* Mallampati photograph
* Tongue and cranial nerve XII screening
* TMJ opening and closing
* Facial and cranial posture
* Forward-head posture
* Cervical lordosis
* Lateral radiograph or CBCT when appropriate
* Occiput, shoulders, iliac crests and feet
* Pelvic category assessment
* Navicular drop and postural-chain findings
* Pre/post objective measurements

Potential care includes cranial releases, TMJ suspended stretch, maxillary/zygomatic work, palatal and suture work, ocular and auricular procedures, pelvic blocking, spinal/extremity correction and postural stabilization.

## 9. Evidence Strategy

The project distinguishes historical suppression from scientific proof.

**Wilk v. AMA** demonstrates unlawful efforts by organized medicine to contain or eliminate chiropractic. It does not prove every chiropractic claim.

Therefore, CSA should not rely only on philosophy, history or testimonials. It should build:

* Standardized examinations
* Reproducible protocols
* Pre/post measurements
* Home-sleep-test outcomes
* Imaging and airway metrics where appropriate
* Patient-reported outcomes
* Case reports
* Prospective datasets
* Conference presentations
* Publishable research

The long-term objective is to present at sleep conferences and demonstrate that structural biomechanics is missing from conventional airway care.
