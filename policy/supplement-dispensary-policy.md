# CSA Policy — Practitioner Dispensary

**Status:** **Decided** by Robert (Dr. Z), 2026-08-21.
**Applies to:** CSA and Dr. Z's clinical practice.
**Related:** CLAUDE.md §5 (red lines), §8 (pathway & pricing),
`content/cortisol-circadian-rhythm-and-the-hpa-axis.md` §11.

---

## 1. The decision, in one paragraph

CSA will use a **practitioner dispensary account** — an online dispensary
through which a patient orders professional-grade supplements directly from the
platform or manufacturer. **CSA holds no inventory.** The dispensary is
**disclosed plainly** to every patient who uses it. It is positioned as a
**convenience for patients already under care** — not a pillar of the model, not
a marketing channel, and **not a step in the CSA pathway.**

---

## 2. What this is — and what it is not

**It is:**
- A practitioner account with a dispensary platform or manufacturer.
- A way for an existing patient to obtain professional-grade product without
  guessing on Amazon.
- A small, disclosed, secondary convenience.

**It is not:**
- Inventory. CSA does not buy, stock, ship, or return product. Ever.
- A step in the pathway. It never appears between Steps 1–6 (CLAUDE.md §8).
- A line item in a Tecopa proposal, a Guided Case Plan quote, or any CSA fee.
- A revenue target. It is never forecast, never quota'd, never a KPI.
- Part of the CSA doctor-training offer. CSA does not teach DCs to sell
  supplements and does not build a downline.
- A treatment for sleep-disordered breathing, cortisol rhythm, or anything
  structural.

---

## 3. The four hard rules

**Rule 1 — No inventory.** The patient orders from the platform. Product ships
from the platform. Returns are handled by the platform. If a model requires CSA
to hold stock, CSA does not do it.

**Rule 2 — Existing patients only.** The dispensary is offered to people already
under Dr. Z's care. It is never used to acquire patients, never mentioned in
outreach, never on a landing page, never in a lead magnet, never in a funnel
email.

**Rule 3 — Never inside the clinical close.** Product is never introduced during
the Report of Findings or the case presentation (Module 3). Those conversations
are about findings, structure, and the care plan. Mixing a product margin into
the moment a patient decides on a $15K case plan is the single fastest way to
contaminate both. If product comes up at all, it comes up in a **separate, later
conversation** the patient initiates or clearly wants.

**Rule 4 — The recommendation must survive the margin test.** Before any
recommendation: *would I say the exact same thing if I earned nothing on it?* If
the answer is anything but a clean yes, don't say it.

---

## 4. Disclosure standard

Disclosure is **verbal at the moment of recommendation** and **written wherever
a link or account is shared.** Not buried, not a footnote.

**Verbal script:**

> "If you want it, I keep a practitioner dispensary account — it's an online
> pharmacy for professional-grade product, you order direct and it ships to you.
> I should tell you plainly that I do make a small margin when you order through
> it. You are under no obligation at all, and it doesn't change anything about
> your care either way. Buy it anywhere you like — I just want you getting a
> product that's actually what it says it is."

**Written line** (for any dispensary link, email, or handout):

> *Disclosure: Dr. Zeravica maintains a practitioner dispensary account and
> receives a margin on products ordered through it. Use of the dispensary is
> entirely optional and is not part of any CSA service, fee, or care plan.*

**If a patient declines:** it is never raised again. Not once.

---

## 5. Claims — the same red lines apply

Everything in CLAUDE.md §5 applies to product conversations without exception.

**Never say or imply:**
- That any supplement treats, corrects, resets, or cures obstructive sleep
  apnea, snoring, an airway, or a cortisol rhythm.
- That a product is part of, required by, or improves the results of VAET,
  CFR-BNS, or any structural care.
- That the HST, CBCT, or structural findings indicate a supplement need.
- Anything about "adrenal fatigue"
  (`content/cortisol-circadian-rhythm-and-the-hpa-axis.md` §6 — CSA does not use
  the term).

**The standing CSA rule, unchanged by this decision:**

> CSA does not convert a sleep-and-airway clinical conversation into a supplement
> recommendation.

The dispensary exists off to the side of that conversation, not inside it.
Structural care is the deliverable. Product is a convenience, and nothing else.

---

## 6. Why the narrow version

Stated plainly so future-Robert and future-Claude both remember the reasoning:

1. **Credibility is the asset.** CSA is trying to make chiropractic a serious
   voice in the sleep-and-airway conversation. "DC selling supplements" is the
   exact stereotype that keeps chiropractors out of that room. A disclosed,
   no-inventory, off-to-the-side dispensary is defensible to a sleep physician.
   A supplement-forward practice is not.
2. **Every clinical recommendation gets read through the money.** Disclosure is
   what keeps the patient's trust intact — and it's what lets Dr. Z make a
   product recommendation without it costing him the structural case.
3. **It's low-ticket and CSA is premium-tiered** (CLAUDE.md §4). Supplement
   margin is small; fulfillment is a headache. Attention belongs on doctor
   training and guided case plans, which is where the actual revenue is.
4. **The lineage cuts both ways.** Dr. Z was trained in blood chemistry
   interpretation by Biotics' clinical chemist (CLAUDE.md §6). That's real depth
   — and it is exactly why the restraint reads as informed rather than naive.
   Depth is the reason to be careful here, not a license to sell.

## 7. Open items to decide

These do not block the decision. They need answers before the first order.

1. **Platform.** Options: a dispensary aggregator (e.g. Fullscript, Emerson
   Ecologics) vs. a **direct Biotics Research practitioner account**. Biotics is
   the lineage-consistent choice and Dr. Z knows the line; an aggregator carries
   more brands and handles fulfillment across manufacturers. Decide one — do not
   run several.
2. **Margin posture.** Standard practitioner margin, or discount passed to the
   patient with a thinner margin? A visibly thin margin makes the disclosure in
   §4 land even better.
3. **Licensure/state rules.** Confirm what Dr. Z's licensing state(s) and board
   allow regarding chiropractic product sales, markup limits, and required
   disclosure. **Verify with the board or counsel — do not assume.**
4. **Never bill it.** Product is cash, patient-direct, and is never submitted to
   insurance or bundled into an insurance-billed service.
5. **Online disclosure.** If a dispensary link ever appears in an email or on a
   page, the §4 written disclosure goes with it, every time (FTC endorsement
   guidance).

---

## 8. What would trigger revisiting this

Re-open the policy only on an explicit, written decision by Dr. Z. Signals that
would prompt a re-look:

- A patient or DC reports feeling sold to. **Stop and reassess immediately.**
- Dispensary revenue starts influencing clinical recommendations, scheduling, or
  attention.
- A partner (dentist, MD, HST vendor) raises it as a concern.
- Anyone proposes private-labeling a "CSA" formula — that is a **different
  decision entirely** and is not covered by this policy.
