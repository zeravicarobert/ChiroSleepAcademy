### Before 10:00 — four things from this chair

These are the whole day. If they don't happen, the drive is a gamble.

**1. Call the gas supplier first. Do not drive on hope.**

Novi Sad has industrial gas depots (Messer Tehnogas, Linde, SIAD and local
welding-supply houses). Wednesday, so they're open — typically ~07:00–15:00,
often with a hard midday cutoff on filling. Ask exactly this:

- Do you have **CO2 with a siphon / dip tube** (*sifonska boca* — "tečni CO2,
  sa uronskom cevi")? **This is the make-or-break question.** A cryo gun needs
  *liquid* draw. A standard CO2 cylinder without a dip tube only gives vapour
  and the gun will not work — same gas, wrong cylinder, wasted trip.
- Can a walk-in **buy or exchange** a cylinder, or does it need a company
  account / existing cylinder to swap? (Serbian depots often only *fill* or
  *exchange* — they won't hand a stranger a bottle.)
- What size, what price, and can you take it away today?
- Any paperwork needed (ID, firm details, VAT number)?

> **If the answer is no, or "come back with an account" → don't drive for CO2.**
> The day switches to Plan B (bucket B, below) and Novi Sad becomes a family
> day only — still worth it, just with different expectations.

**2. Text Ljilja — and ask the one question that unlocks everything.**

Don't ask "where is the land?" Ask for the two identifiers that make it
findable in ten minutes:

> "Ljilja — do you know the **katastarska opština** (cadastral municipality)
> and the **broj parcele** (parcel number) for the land? Or the name on the
> title? If you have a photo of any old paper, send it."

With those two numbers the parcel can be pulled up on the Republic Geodetic
Authority's public cadastre (**eKatastar**, `katastar.rgz.gov.rs`) — location,
size, class, and **whose name is currently on it**. Without them, "somewhere
near Novi Sad" is a rumour, not an asset.

**Flag on the name "Melnice":** the Melnica that shows up in Serbia is in the
Petrovac na Mlavi area — that's **south-east of Belgrade**, not near Novi Sad.
That contradicts the "nearer Novi Sad than Beograd" memory. So either it's a
different place with a similar name, a hamlet/field name (*potes*) rather than
a village, or the memory of the direction is off. **Don't resolve this by
guessing — it's the first thing to ask Ljilja.** Getting this wrong could mean
looking for land 250 km from where it actually is.

**The second question for Ljilja, and it's the important one:**

> "Was there ever an **ostavinski postupak** (probate) — did anyone go to a
> **notar** (notary) after the death?"

In Serbia, inheritance does not transfer title automatically. If probate was
never opened, the land is still in a dead relative's name and Robert is an
heir *on paper only* — no right to sell, lease, or borrow against it. That is
the actual status question. Everything else (is it good land, is it worth
anything) is downstream of it.
