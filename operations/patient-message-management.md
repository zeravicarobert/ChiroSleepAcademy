## The real fix: triage + faster (not colder) replies

You don't have a channel problem to solve by moving people — you have a
volume-and-order problem. Two things fix it:

1. **Triage** so a scheduling text and an urgent question stop looking
   identical at 9pm.
2. **Saved replies you personalize** — a 5-second head start on each
   message, not a robotic script. You still add the name, the detail, the
   warmth. The template just kills the blank-screen delay.

---

## Triage order (answer in this sequence)

Work top-down, not chronologically.

| Tier | What it is | Target response |
|---|---|---|
| **1 — Safety** | Possible emergency, severe symptoms, crisis | Immediately: redirect to 911 / urgent care. Don't manage clinically over text. |
| **2 — Time-sensitive scheduling** | Same/next-day appt, cancellation, "running late" | Same session — a slot is perishable |
| **3 — Results / active case** | HST or CBCT results, care-plan questions, SDI/TTD certification follow-up | Same day — your revenue-and-trust core |
| **4 — New inquiries** | "How much," "do you treat X," first contact | Same day — warm leads cool fast |
| **5 — General / FYI** | Thank-yous, non-urgent questions, far-out scheduling | Batch once daily |

**Rule of thumb:** Tier 1 is the only thing that interrupts your day.
Everything else is handled in one or two fixed passes (suggest ~12pm & 5pm).

## SMS + patient health info — consent

Patient health info over plain SMS is fine **when the patient has signed a
consent** acknowledging they understand the risks of unencrypted texting and
choose it anyway. That signed communication consent is the standard way
providers do this, and it's what makes it OK.

Keep it clean:
- **Get it in writing and keep it on file** for each patient who texts about
  health info.
- **Honor opt-outs** — if they later say stop, stop.
- **Minors** → the parent/guardian signs.
- Consent covers the *channel*; it doesn't change your record-keeping or
  documentation duties. Log what matters into the chart, not just the thread.
- Still use judgment on truly sensitive content — some things are better by
  call or visit.

*Not legal advice. If you want, I'll draft a one-page SMS communication
consent form you can have patients sign — say the word.*
