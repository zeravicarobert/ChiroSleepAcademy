If you've ever laid awake listening to your husband snore… or pause his
breathing… this is for you.

Many women notice the signs of sleep apnea years before their husbands take it
seriously.

Most couples are told the same two things:
- "He needs to lose weight."
- "He needs a CPAP mask."

Both can matter. But here's what's rarely explained clearly:

For many people, snoring and sleep apnea are also driven by the **airway itself**
— not weight alone, and not effort.

That's why weight loss doesn't always fix it.
That's why CPAP works well for some men and is hard for others to stick with.
And that's why the worry doesn't go away.

I put together a short, free guide for wives and partners that explains:
- When snoring becomes a real medical concern
- Why some approaches don't fully solve it
- What to understand about the airway — so you can make the most informed choice
  about masks, machines, and other options

No scare tactics. No sales pitch. Just clarity.

## ⚠️ Guardrail notes (why the edits)

The post is strong. Four things kept it inside the red lines (CLAUDE.md §5):

1. **Don't dismiss weight or CPAP.** The original *"airway problems — not weight
   problems… or equipment problems"* overstates. Weight really does drive many
   cases, and CPAP is first-line care that helps millions. I changed it to
   *"also driven by the airway itself — not weight alone,"* and *"works well for
   some… hard for others to stick with."* Same insight (the airway is the
   overlooked piece), no knock on the device. This protects your credibility
   with the MDs and dentists you also want to respect you.

2. **"Root cause" is retired language.** Your own positioning (§18) drops
   "addresses the root cause" as a cure-style claim. Changed *"don't address the
   root cause"* → *"don't fully solve it."*

3. **"Resigning yourself to masks or machines" → softened.** The original reads
   as anti-CPAP. Now: *"make the most informed choice about masks, machines, and
   other options."* Pro-patient, not anti-device.

4. **"Over a decade" authority line — confirm it.** Don't claim a timeframe that
   isn't exactly true (no fake authority, §5). Your *sleep-airway* focus is
   newer; your chiropractic career is 25+ years. I reworded to *"a chiropractor
   for over 25 years."* Use whichever is true; just don't overstate the sleep-
   specific years.
