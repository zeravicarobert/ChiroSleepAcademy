# CSA Doctor Alignment Call — Script & Playbook

**Step 1 of the CSA pathway (see CLAUDE.md §8).** Free. 20–30 minutes on Zoom.
**Purpose:** A real alignment conversation — screen whether this DC is a fit as
(1) a patient, (2) a business-builder, or (3) both — and, when it's honest to do
so, invite them into the **$300 CSA Sleep Baseline** (Step 2) as the next step.

> This is **not** a sales call disguised as a consult. If they're not a fit, you
> say so and end warmly. Credibility is worth more than any single close (§5).

---

## Before the call (Peter owns setup)

- Confirm Zoom link sent + calendar hold.
- Have their intake in front of you: how they found CSA, are they on the 28-opt-in
  list or the warm bench (Parker / Relationship sheet).
- One-line goal in your head: **"Which of the three doors is this DC walking
  through — and what is the single honest next step for them?"**
- Have the Sleep Baseline booking link ready so Peter can send it the moment the
  call ends.

---

## Mindset (for you, not read aloud)

- You are the doctor-teacher, not the salesman. Curiosity beats pressure.
- Heal the Healer First: the DC becomes the patient before they become a partner.
- Everything you recommend, you recommend clinically — because it's the right next
  step, not because it's the next price point.
- Honest about what's proven and what's still being proven (§4).

---

## 1. Open — set the frame (2 min)

> "Thanks for making the time. This is an alignment call, not a pitch — so let me
> tell you exactly what I want to do with our 25 minutes. I want to understand
> your own sleep and airway story first, because that's how CSA works: I help the
> chiropractor before I ever talk about helping their patients. By the end we'll
> both know whether there's a natural next step — and if there isn't, I'll tell you
> that straight. Sound good?"

*Director note: naming the honesty up front lowers their guard and makes the
eventual invite feel earned, not sprung.*

---

## 2. Their story — discovery (8–10 min)

Ask, listen, don't teach yet. Take notes Peter can file.

**Sleep & airway history:**
- "How's your own sleep these days — honestly?"
- "Any snoring, gasping, waking up unrefreshed? Does your partner notice anything?"
- "Mouth-breathing, daytime fatigue, brain fog in the afternoon?"

**Prior workup:**
- "Have you ever done a sleep study? CPAP, an oral appliance, a CBCT?"
- "Anyone ever look at your airway structurally?"

**Their practice & why CSA caught them:**
- "What made you open my email / take this call?"
- "In your practice — are you seeing sleep and airway show up in your patients
  already? SOT, AK, CBP, posture folks tend to."

*Listen for which door they're walking through:*
- **Patient door:** their own sleep story is live and unresolved.
- **Builder door:** they're energized about the practice/division opportunity.
- **Both door:** the strongest fit — they feel it personally AND see the model.

---

## 3. Reflect back — name the door (3 min)

Say what you heard. This is the doctor-teacher moment.

**If patient-first:**
> "Here's what I'm hearing. Before this is a business conversation, it's a *you*
> conversation — your own sleep has some real flags. And that's exactly where CSA
> starts. I don't help chiropractors build a sleep division from theory. I help
> them live the model first, on themselves."

**If builder-first:**
> "You clearly see where this goes for your practice — and you're right. But the
> reason CSA works is that you can't teach a patient what you haven't lived. So the
> door in is the same either way: we start with your own data."

**Either way, you converge on the same honest next step: their own baseline.**

---

## 4. The pivot — why the Sleep Baseline is the honest next step (3 min)

This is the hinge. Frame it clinically, not commercially.

> "You already know the X-ray is what turns a guess into a case. In the sleep and
> airway world, the equivalent is a **Home Sleep Test** — objective data on what's
> actually happening while you sleep. Right now, everything we've talked about is
> subjective. I don't want to build anything on a guess, and neither do you.
>
> So the real next step isn't a course or a program. It's getting *your* number.
> That's the **CSA Sleep Baseline**."

*Note on scope (§5): you order and coordinate the HST and read it through the
structural-airway lens. The sleep-physician interpretation is done by the vendor's
reading MD. DCs don't diagnose OSA — your role is structural. Say this plainly if
they ask; it builds trust.*

---

## 5. The offer — what it is, what it isn't (3 min)

Be concrete and calm. Price stated once, without flinching.

> "The Sleep Baseline is **$300**, founding rate. Here's exactly what you get:
> - A home sleep test ordered through CSA — a partner handles the device and the
>   physician read, so it's clean and legitimate.
> - I personally review your report through the structural-airway lens and walk you
>   through what it means — where your airway and structure fit into the picture.
> - That report becomes the anchor for everything after it. It's the X-ray of your
>   sleep case.
>
> What it is **not**: it's not a diagnosis I'm making — the reading physician does
> that. It's not a commitment to anything beyond the baseline. You get your data,
> you get my read, and *then* you decide if you want to go further. No pressure to."

---

## 6. Ask for the next step — then book it (2 min)

Direct, clean, then silent.

> "So here's what I'd suggest: let's get your baseline ordered this week. Does that
> feel like the right next step for you?"

**...then stop talking. Let them answer.**

- **Yes →** "Great. Peter will send you the link to get started right after this
  call, and I'll have your report reviewed within [X] days of it coming back."
  *(Peter books it live — don't let it drift to 'later.')*
- **Maybe / "let me think" →** see §7.
- **Not now →** honor it warmly, §8.

---

## 7. If they hesitate (handle, don't push)

**"Let me think about it."**
> "Totally fair. Can I ask — is it the timing, the money, or that you're not sure
> it's worth it yet? I'd rather answer the real question than have you sit on it."
*Then answer the actual objection. Usually it's uncertainty, not price.*

**"$300 — what exactly am I paying for vs. just getting a test somewhere?"**
> "Honest answer: the test itself is a commodity. What you're paying for is that
> I coordinate it and then read it through a lens no sleep lab uses — the
> structural and airway picture — and connect it to what you do as a chiropractor.
> The number is the data. My read is the meaning."

**"Can insurance cover it?"**
> "There are insurance and cash paths depending on the vendor — Peter will lay out
> the options with you. The $300 is the founding coordination-and-review rate; we'll
> be straight with you about what the test billing looks like."

*Never oversell. If it's genuinely not the moment, that's fine.*

---

## 8. If they're not a fit (graceful exit)

> "Honestly? I don't think the baseline is your next step right now, and I'd rather
> tell you that than sell you something. Let's stay in touch — when your own sleep
> or your practice makes this feel timely, the door's open."

*Add them to the nurture list (Peter). A clean 'not now' is a future yes.*

---

## After the call (Peter owns handoff)

- **If yes:** send Sleep Baseline booking link within 10 minutes. Log the sale.
  Calendar the report-review call.
- **If maybe:** one follow-up touch scheduled within 3 days.
- **If no:** move to nurture list, note why, revisit in 60–90 days.
- Update the weekly tracker: **calls held → baselines booked** (the one number).
