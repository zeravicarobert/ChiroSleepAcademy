## 2. The outline

### Structural Airway Chiropractic — proposed CE course

**Format:** modular. Can be delivered as a 2-hour introduction, a full day, or
a multi-module certification track.
**Audience:** Doctors of Chiropractic. No prior sleep training assumed.

**Module 1 — Anatomy & Physiology of the Sleep-Airway System**
*Drafted and in use.*
- The frame: skull-down, sleep-first
- Upper airway anatomy as a collapsible tube
- The structural layer — where the Doctor of Chiropractic legitimately works
- Physiology of sleep and breathing
- Reading a sleep report without bluffing: AHI, ODI, oxygen desaturation
- Screening instruments and how to actually score them: Epworth, STOP-BANG,
  Mallampati, BOLT
- The scope line — what a DC assesses, what a physician diagnoses

**Module 2 — Report of Findings**
*In development.*
- Translating objective data into a patient conversation
- What the structural exam adds to the sleep study
- Presenting findings without overclaiming

**Module 3 — Case Presentation & Ethical Close**
*Drafted and in use.*
- Presenting a pathway as a clinical roadmap, not a menu
- The role split that protects the doctor-patient relationship
- Handling objections ethically
- Explicit red lines

**Module 4 — Structural Technique for the Airway**
- The viscerocranium: why the facial skeleton is the airway skeleton
- Pharyngeal arch derivatives — maxilla, mandible, TMJ, hyoid, larynx as one
  developmental series
- Cranial base versus vault: what is assessed and what is intervened on
- Endonasal and manual approaches, and their limits

**Module 5 — Collaboration & Referral**
- Working with dental sleep medicine, ENT, and sleep physicians as peers
- Referral protocols, including for medically urgent findings
- Where the DC hands off, and where the DC holds the case

**Module 6 — Measurement & Evidence**
- Building a pre/post dataset that survives peer review
- Honest reporting, including non-responders
- Why the field's structural claims have not yet earned their evidence, and
  what it would take

### Where this touches functional neurology

Offered as overlap, not as a claim on the Institute's territory:

- Brainstem respiratory control — the pre-Bötzinger complex, dorsal and
  ventral respiratory groups, chemoreceptor drive
- Sleep-disordered breathing as a chronic arousal and autonomic load
- Sensory input from cervical and cranial structures modulating neurological
  output
- Sleep depth and its relationship to glymphatic clearance
