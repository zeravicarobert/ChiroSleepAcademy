## 2. Why He Matters More Than Any Other Name on the CSA Bench

CSA's thesis is *skull-down, sleep-first*. The honest weak point in that thesis
has always been **mechanism**: why should structural work matter to sleep?

The answer CSA has leaned on is airway patency. That's real — but it's the
same claim every dental sleep practice makes. It doesn't differentiate CSA, and
it puts chiropractic in the position of doing a worse version of what a dentist
does better.

**CSF is a second mechanism, and it is the one that is uniquely
chiropractic-adjacent.** Cranial work, SOT, CFR, and VAET have all invoked CSF
and cranial motion for decades — historically with weak evidence and a lot of
overclaiming. That history is exactly why CSA needs a real scientist in the

Zappaterra is plausibly the single best-positioned person alive to help CSA

## 3. The Science Bridge — What Is Actually Established

Three legs, all real, all published. Use these; do not go further than these.

**Leg 1 — Breathing is a CSF pump.**
Inspiration is a major driving force for CSF flow. In the forced-breathing
work, significant CSF flow was observed with inspiration, and breath-hold
suppressed it. The proposed mechanism is that negative intrathoracic pressure
during inspiration lowers intracranial pressure, drawing CSF headward; forced
expiration reverses it. Cardiac pulsation drives the fast oscillation, but
respiration contributes disproportionately to net flow.

**Leg 2 — Clearance is sleep-dependent.**
Glymphatic clearance depends on the *depth* of NREM sleep. Reduced deep NREM
and frequent arousals from lighter stages degrade it. Sleep is increasingly
framed as an active state for brain fluid transport, in which CSF movement,
vascular and respiratory mechanics, and lymphatic drainage jointly govern
clearance of neurotoxic solutes.

**Leg 3 — OSA is associated with glymphatic dysfunction.**
Glymphatic impairment in OSA is now at meta-analysis level for severe OSA, with
associated cognitive correlates, and is being investigated as a mediator
between sleep disorders and neurodegeneration.

**The honest chain:**
> Disordered breathing at night → altered respiratory mechanics in exactly the
> window when clearance is supposed to happen → measurable glymphatic
> dysfunction in OSA populations.

**The honest gap — say this out loud:**
> Nothing in that chain demonstrates that a chiropractic adjustment, cranial
> work, or VAET changes CSF dynamics. That link is **unproven**. It is a
> hypothesis, and it is precisely the thing to bring him as a question rather
> than a claim.

From `CLAUDE.md` §5, sharpened for a CSF researcher who will notice
immediately:

  glymphatic clearance.** Unproven. Saying it in front of him would end the

The asset here is being the chiropractor who knows the difference between what
