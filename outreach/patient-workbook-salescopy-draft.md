## ⚠️ Why this can't ship as written

**1. It makes an easy-fix / cure-style promise (red line §5).**
*"Fix this one thing, and getting great sleep finally becomes easy!"* Sleep apnea
is not one glitch fixed by one thing, and nothing about it is "easy." CSA forbids
guaranteed outcomes and oversimplification.

**2. It's a health claim on a medical condition (biggest risk).**
Selling a $27 workbook to *"identify and overcome hidden sleep-disordered
breathing"* implies a layperson can self-treat a medical condition (OSA). That's
both a red line and a real **FTC/health-claim + patient-safety** problem — it could
steer someone away from a proper diagnosis. A workbook can **educate**; it cannot
help someone "overcome" sleep-disordered breathing.

**3. The voice is the exact opposite of CSA's brand (§4).**
"Glitch," "fancy beds," "easy!", "desperately needs to heal" — that's hype +
fear-based marketing + internet-bro energy. CSA's voice is *"a respected clinician
explaining something important to a peer."* No hype, no fear.

**4. Wrong product name.** The **Foundations Workbook is DC-facing** (for
chiropractors). This is a *patient* product — call it something else (e.g., a
patient sleep-airway guide), or you conflate the curriculum with a consumer item.

## ⚠️ Strategic flag (you decide)
This is **sufferer-bait hype marketing** — which is the **opposite of the
positioning you locked today** (`content/positioning-pain-to-sleep.md`): *market to
decision-makers, infrastructure language, empathy-opens-the-door-but-elevate.* A
"one weird glitch, fix it easy for $27" page pulls in price-shoppers and
relief-seekers and cheapens the premium, credible brand you've been building all
session. **Recommendation: keep the patient product, drop the hype register.**
Educate at $27; let credibility — not urgency — do the selling.
