# Reply Template — Inbound VAET Inquiry (post-event)

**Created:** 2026-08-18
**Use for:** People who met Robert at Liberpulco (or any future event) and
messaged asking about VAET / the work he did on them.
**Goal of the reply:** Get off DMs and onto a short Zoom, without promising
anything clinical.

---

## Ethical guardrails — read before every send (CLAUDE.md § 5)

- **No cure claims.** VAET does not cure sleep apnea. Do not imply it.
- **No diagnosis.** Robert does not diagnose OSA. His role is structural.
- **No attacks.** Never position VAET against CPAP, oral appliances, surgery,
  or their physician. It is the structural layer *alongside* their care.
- **No hype.** Doctor-teacher voice. Calm, clear, specific.
- **A session is not a treatment plan.** Say so.

---

## Version A — general inquiry ("what was that thing you did?")

> Hey [Name] — great to meet you in Acapulco, and thanks for reaching out.
>
> What I do is called VAET. It's a structural approach — I work skull-down on
> the cranium, spine, and posture, and how those affect the airway you breathe
> through at night. Most of my work is with people who snore, wake up tired,
> grind their teeth, or have been told they stop breathing in their sleep.
>
> To be straight with you: I'm not treating sleep apnea and I'm not going to
> promise you a cure. What I do is look at the structure, find what's
> restricting the airway mechanically, and work on that. Some people get a lot
> out of it. Some need a dentist, an ENT, or a sleep physician alongside me,
> and I'll tell you honestly if that's you.
>
> The way I start with everyone is a short Zoom — no charge. I ask about your
> sleep and breathing history, do a visual airway and posture screen, and tell
> you whether this is even worth your time. If it is, I'll walk you through what
> the next step looks like.
>
> Want me to send a couple of times?
>
> — Dr. Robert Zeravica

## Version B — they already know they have sleep apnea / are on CPAP

> Hey [Name] — good to hear from you, and thanks for telling me about the CPAP.
>
> First thing: keep using it and keep working with whoever prescribed it. I'm
> not here to replace that, and I'd be wary of anyone who told you otherwise.
>
> What I add is the structural side. CPAP holds the airway open with pressure.
> It doesn't change the skull, spine, or posture that shaped the airway in the
> first place. That's the piece I work on — and it's a piece most people with
> sleep apnea have never had anyone look at.
>
> I can't tell you what that will do for your numbers. Nobody honest can, until
> we look. What I can do is a free Zoom where I take your sleep and airway
> history and run a visual structural screen, then tell you plainly whether
> there's something here worth pursuing.
>
> Want me to send a few times?
>
> — Dr. Robert Zeravica

## After the reply

1. Log the person in the `CSA Relationship Strategies` sheet — name, contact,
   where you met, what they asked for, date of first reply.
2. Get the Zoom on the calendar. A reply without a booked call is a lost lead.
3. If they go quiet after 5–7 days, one short nudge. Then stop.
4. If they're a DC (or married to one, or work in a practice) — flag it. That
   person is worth far more to CSA as a doctor than as a patient.

## Notes

- These are drafts to adapt, not scripts to paste. Robert's own voice beats a
  template every time.
- Do not batch-send. Each of these people met Robert in person and will notice
  a form letter.
- If anyone describes symptoms that sound urgent — witnessed apneas plus
  cardiac history, severe daytime sleepiness while driving — tell them to see a
  physician now. Don't sit on it waiting for a Zoom slot.
