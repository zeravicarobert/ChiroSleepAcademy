## The one thing to know first

**Toyota Financial Services does not refinance its own loans.**

TFS (Toyota Motor Credit Corp) originates and services loans. It does not
re-write a loan it already holds at a better rate. So "refinancing with
Toyota" is not a thing that exists.

What refinancing actually means here:

> A **new lender** approves you, sends TFS a payoff check, TFS closes the
> loan and releases the lien, and the new lender becomes the lienholder on
> the California title.

Your job is therefore two things:
1. Get an accurate **10-day payoff quote** from TFS.
2. Get a **better-priced loan** from someone else to cover it.

TFS's role is passive. They just get paid off.

## Whether refinancing is even worth it

Run this before applying anywhere. Refinancing is worth doing when **the
rate drops meaningfully and the term does not stretch**.

**The trap:** most refinance offers lower your monthly payment by extending
the term, not by cutting the rate. A lower payment over more months can cost
more in total interest than the loan you started with. Judge every offer on
**total remaining interest**, not on the monthly payment.

Decision rule:

- **Rate drops 2+ points, same or shorter term** → clearly worth it
- **Rate drops 1–2 points, same term** → worth it, run the math
- **Rate drops <1 point** → usually not worth the paperwork
- **Payment drops only because the term got longer** → this is not a win

Also check for a **prepayment penalty** on the current TFS contract. Most
TFS retail installment contracts are simple-interest with no prepayment
penalty, but confirm it on your specific contract before assuming.

Average auto refinance rates are running around **7.8–8%**, slightly below

## Where to shop

Apply to **3–5 lenders inside a 14-day window.** Multiple auto-loan
inquiries in that window are scored as a single inquiry by FICO, so
shopping aggressively costs you nothing extra in score.

**Credit unions first** — they consistently price auto paper below banks:
- **PenFed** — anyone can join
- **DCU** — does not surcharge used vehicles; same rate new or used
- **Navy Federal** — eligible if there is any military service in the family
- **A local Los Angeles–area credit union** — often the best rate of all, and
  more willing to talk to a self-employed borrower face to face

**Marketplaces** (one application, multiple offers — useful for a fast read
on where you actually price):
- LendingTree, Caribou, RateGenius, AutoPay

**Skip the dealership** for refinancing. Dealer-arranged financing marks up
the rate; that is how they make money on it.

## Honest read on the approval odds

A few things in your own records will show up on a lender's credit pull, and
you should walk in knowing about them rather than being surprised:

- **A debt-relief program appears active.** "National Debt Assistan" charged
  $259 on 2/11/26. Enrollment in a debt settlement or management program is
  visible to lenders and typically drops the score materially — the NFCC
  puts it as high as 100 points. It does not disqualify you, but it will
  affect pricing, and some lenders decline outright.
- **An open collection.** Viking Client Services, $9,492.75, on the Avis
  Budget account.
- **A prior auto loan written off by a credit union**, per your own notes.
  That is the single most damaging item for an auto lender specifically,
  because it is the same product class.
- **Unpaid citations and tolls** — the LAPD citation (#J267868), the toll
  road notices, the 405 Express Lanes violation. These generally do not hit
  credit, but an expired-registration citation can create a title/registration
  problem that stalls a lien transfer. Worth clearing regardless.

**What this means practically:** most refinance lenders want **600+** and a
DTI under roughly 36–49%. Pull your actual score before applying — Credit
Karma or your card issuer will show it free. If it is under 600, refinancing
now will likely produce either denials or offers worse than what TFS already
gives you, and the better move is to fix the score first and revisit.

None of this is a reason not to try. It is a reason to **know the number
before you spend applications on it.**

## Step-by-step

1. **Pull the credit score.** Free, five minutes. This gates everything.
2. **Get the TFS payoff quote** in writing — portal or phone.
3. **Confirm current APR, payment, months remaining** from the TFS statement.
4. **Value the truck** on KBB and Edmunds using the VIN and true mileage.
   Note the new engine.
5. **Do the math** — is the achievable rate 1+ point better at the same or
   shorter term? If no, stop here. That is a legitimate finish.
6. **Apply to 3–5 lenders in one 14-day window.** Credit unions first.
7. **Compare on total remaining interest**, never on monthly payment.
8. **Accept the best offer.** New lender pays TFS directly.
9. **Verify TFS shows a $0 balance** and the account is closed. Do not assume.
10. **Confirm the lien release and title transfer** to the new lender with
    the California DMV. This is the step people skip and regret.
11. **Update the insurance** — Infinity needs the new lienholder on the
    policy, or the new lender may force-place expensive coverage.
12. **Cancel any TFS autopay** so a payment does not draft after payoff.
