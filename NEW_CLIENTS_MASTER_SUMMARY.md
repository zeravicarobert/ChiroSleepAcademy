# 5. CLIENT ONBOARDING WORKFLOW

For a former patient or client beginning a new California EDD SDI claim, the process discussed includes:

## Initial Contact

The patient contacts Dr. Z or is referred to him.

Dr. Z speaks directly with the person and determines whether the case may be appropriate for further evaluation.

## First Check-In

The first check-in includes:

* Creating or reactivating the patient's ChiroCat EHR account.
* Completing required consent forms.
* Collecting basic identifying and claim information.
* Beginning the administrative onboarding process.

## Second Check-In

The second check-in includes:

* Completing the health questionnaire.
* Gathering the clinical and functional information needed for the physician evaluation.
* Preparing the patient for the formal doctor visit.

## Start Claim Service

The "Start Claim" fee is intended to include:

* Doctor visit.
* Clinical evaluation.
* Diagnosis, when supported.
* Treatment plan.
* Determination and ordering of temporary disability when clinically appropriate.
* California EDD SDI medical certification.
* Submission or completion of the applicable EDD physician documentation.

The service should not be described as merely "filling out a form." It includes professional evaluation and physician-level decision-making.

# 7. REFUND AND OUTCOME LANGUAGE

A proposed funnel included a refund policy if a claim was denied, minus a 10% administrative fee.

This requires careful review because:

* A physician cannot guarantee EDD approval.
* EDD, not Dr. Z, determines claim eligibility and payment.
* A denial may result from nonmedical factors outside Dr. Z's control.
* Refund language must distinguish professional services already performed from administrative or outcome-based guarantees.
* California legal, healthcare, fee, and advertising rules must be reviewed by an appropriate attorney.

No guaranteed-approval language should be used without legal review.

# 8. CLIENT PSYCHOLOGY AND WELLNESS CONVERSION

Dr. Z has observed that EDD disability clients are not necessarily in a healing, wellness, or personal-development mindset when they first seek certification.

At the beginning, they are usually focused on:

* Getting their claim handled.
* Replacing lost income.
* Avoiding administrative mistakes.
* Understanding what EDD requires.
* Managing fear, stress, or uncertainty.
* Getting temporary relief from work obligations.

Dr. Z's preferred approach is not to force a broader wellness offer during the initial disability-certification consultation.

Instead:

1. Plant a seed during the first consultation.
2. Complete the immediate disability-related service.
3. Later invite the person to a separate Wellness Consultation.
4. Use that separate consultation to discuss health, sleep, weight, stress, function, mindset, or other services.

The Wellness Consultation is a separate offer and should not be confused with the disability-certification visit.
