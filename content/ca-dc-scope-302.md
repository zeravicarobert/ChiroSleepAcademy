## 1. The governing law

The scope of chiropractic in California is defined by:

- **The Chiropractic Initiative Act of 1922** (voter-enacted; the statute
  the Board operates under), and
- **California Code of Regulations, Title 16, §302 ("Practice of
  Chiropractic")** — the regulation that spells out what is and isn't in
  scope. §302 in its current form comes largely from a **stipulated
  judgment** (a court-approved settlement) that rewrote the definition.

Sources: [Board of Chiropractic Examiners — regulations PDF](https://www.chiro.ca.gov/laws_regs/regulations.pdf) ·
[Dynamic Chiropractic — the §302 definition](https://dynamicchiropractic.com/article/44624-the-new-definition-of-chiropractic-scope-of-practice-in-california)

## 2. ALL the methods & techniques §302 allows (plain-language inventory)

### A. The core act
- **Adjust and manipulate** the spinal column **and other joints** of the
  body, and in the process **manipulate the muscle and connective tissue**
  related to them.

### B. Diagnosis authority (the key clause for CSA)
- A DC **"may diagnose"** any condition, disease, or injury in any
  patient **"so long as such … diagnosis is done in a manner consistent
  with chiropractic methods and techniques"** and **does not constitute
  the practice of medicine.**
- This is broad on its face — but it is bounded by the two phrases:
  *chiropractic methods* and *not practice of medicine.* Those two
  phrases are the entire ballgame for the HST question (§4 below).

### C. Enumerated diagnostic tools/equipment
- **X-ray** — for diagnosis, **not** treatment.
- **Thermography** — for diagnosis.
- **Diagnostic ultrasound** — for **neuromusculoskeletal** diagnosis only.
- **Physical / orthopedic / neurologic examination** — standard exam
  procedures are within scope.

### D. Adjunctive treatment measures ("all necessary mechanical, hygienic,
and sanitary measures incident to the care of the body"), including but
not limited to:
- air, cold, **diet**, **exercise**, heat, light, **massage**, physical
  culture, **rest**, **ultrasound**, water, and **physical therapy
  techniques** — used in the course of chiropractic adjustment/manipulation.

### E. Other recognized activities
- **Nutrition / dietary advice and food/vitamin recommendations**
  (dietary supplements) — within scope as health guidance.
- **Employ and supervise chiropractic assistants / students.**
- Use of supports, tape, braces, etc., incident to care.

### F. Explicitly EXCLUDED (a CA DC may NOT):
- **Practice surgery**, or **sever or penetrate tissue** (this is the
  clause that blocks venipuncture/blood draw as a chiropractic act).
- **Prescribe drugs** / practice medicine (from the Medical Practice Act).
- **Deliver a child / practice obstetrics.**
- Use X-ray or thermography for **treatment**.

## 3. Where the HST question actually sits

Two separate questions — don't conflate them:

**Q1 — May a DC USE / ORDER an HST device?**
§302's enumerated diagnostic devices are X-ray, thermography, and
NMS ultrasound. An HST measures **respiratory airflow, effort, and blood
oxygen** — cardiopulmonary parameters, not obviously "neuromusculoskeletal."
The device is **not enumerated.** That doesn't automatically forbid it
(the exam clause is broad), but it isn't clearly granted either.

**Q2 — May a DC render a DIAGNOSIS from the data?**
Yes *if* the diagnosis is "consistent with chiropractic methods" and not
"practice of medicine." Diagnosing the **structural upper-airway
obstruction (SAO)** — an anatomical finding — is the strongest fit.
Diagnosing **"OSA,"** an ICD-coded sleep-medicine disease, leans toward
practice of medicine.

**Honest read:** the position is *arguable and reasonable*, not settled.
The blood-analogy is weaker in CA than assumed (blood draw is excluded as
tissue penetration). CSA's best ground is the **anatomy/SAO framing**, and
the answer must come from the Board, not from us.

## 4. How to get a DEFINITIVE answer (do this first — cheapest, fastest)

Order of operations, least to most expensive:

1. **Written inquiry to the CA Board of Chiropractic Examiners.** Ask the
   scope question precisely (draft in
   `outreach/2026-07-09-ca-board-scope-inquiry.md`). Board staff opinions
   aren't binding law, but a written answer is strong evidence of good
   faith and often settles the practical question.
2. **CA healthcare / administrative-law attorney opinion letter.** A
   formal written legal opinion you can rely on and show if challenged
   (see §6 for how to find the right one).
3. **Malpractice carrier's risk/legal line.** Your professional-liability
   insurer (e.g., NCMIC) has a risk-management legal line — free to
   policyholders and specifically expert in DC scope. Call them too; their
   answer also tells you whether you're *covered* doing this.

Do all three. They triangulate the real ceiling.

## 5. How to EXPAND §302 to clearly include HST / sleep testing

If the answer is "not clearly in scope," changing the scope is a real —
but long — game. Three legitimate paths:

1. **Board rulemaking petition (Administrative Procedure Act).** Any
   person may petition the Board to adopt or amend a regulation (Cal. Gov.
   Code §11340.6). You'd petition to add sleep/airway diagnostic testing
   to §302's recognized methods. The Board must respond; it may open
   formal rulemaking (notice, comment, hearing).
2. **Legislation.** Amend the Chiropractic Act via a bill — the route
   **AB-2143** used for the lab-test question. Requires a legislative
   author/sponsor and, realistically, the backing of a professional
   association.
3. **Coalition through the CA Chiropractic Association (CalChiro / CCA).**
   Neither of the above works solo. The practical move is to bring this to
   the state association as a scope-modernization issue and let CSA be the
   clinical/evidence engine behind it. This also builds allies instead of
   going it alone.

**Realistic timeline:** rulemaking is 12–24+ months; legislation a
multi-year effort. **Strategic implication:** don't wait on scope
expansion to build CSA. Build on the **SAO/anatomy framing under existing
§302**, get the opinion letters, and run the scope-expansion campaign in
parallel as a long-term category-building move — not a launch dependency.

## 6. Finding the right attorney (what to look for, and where)

**The right specialty (in priority order):**
1. **California healthcare / professional-licensing attorney** who
   represents **chiropractors specifically** before the Board of
   Chiropractic Examiners.
2. Failing that, a **healthcare regulatory / administrative attorney**
   with scope-of-practice experience.

**Where to find them:**
- Ask your **malpractice carrier (NCMIC)** for their panel counsel — they
  keep lists of DC-defense attorneys by state.
- **California Chiropractic Association (CalChiro)** — ask for their
  general counsel or a referral; they deal with scope constantly.
- **State Bar of California** certified Legal Specialist referral;
  search "administrative law" + healthcare.
- Colleagues: DCs who run functional-medicine/lab-heavy practices in CA
  have already vetted scope attorneys — ask in those networks.

**Screening questions when you call:**
- "Do you represent chiropractors before the CA Board of Chiropractic
  Examiners?" (must be yes)
- "Have you handled scope-of-practice / practice-of-medicine boundary
  questions?" (must be yes)
- "Can you write an opinion letter I can rely on and produce if
  challenged?"
- Flat fee for a scoped opinion letter vs. hourly? (a bounded scope
  question should be a few hours / low four figures)

**The exact questions to put to the attorney (bring this list):**
1. May a CA DC **order and/or administer** a home sleep test (HST)?
2. May a CA DC **interpret HST data** and render a **diagnosis of a
   structural upper-airway obstruction (SAO)** consistent with §302?
3. Is diagnosing/labeling **"OSA"** specifically the practice of medicine
   in CA? Where is the line, and what language keeps me on the right side?
4. In a **cash-only** model, which insurance/AASM requirements fall away,
   and which medico-legal standard-of-care duties remain?
5. What **referral / co-management protocol** do I need to meet the
   standard of care for severe cases (see referral protocol asset)?
6. What must my **consent forms and clinical documentation** say?
7. Do I need a **collaborating/supervising physician** for any part of
   this, and if so, for what exactly?

## 7. Bottom line

- CSA's strongest, defensible position **today**: diagnose and manage the
  **structural airway obstruction (SAO)** under §302's anatomy/chiropractic-
  methods clause, cash-based, with a written referral protocol for severe
  cases — and **confirm the ceiling** with the Board + an attorney + the
  malpractice carrier before public copy asserts it.
- **Scope expansion** to name HST explicitly is a worthy long game via
  petition/legislation/association — run it in parallel, don't gate the
  launch on it.
