## Verified citation (checked 2026-07-16)

- **Study:** "Postmortem Evidence of Lymphatic Vessels Located at the Boundary
  of Central and Peripheral Nervous Systems in the Cervical Spine."
- **Journal:** *Cellular and Molecular Neurobiology* (2026).
  **DOI: 10.1007/s10571-026-01744-4.**
- **Timeline:** Received Mar 6, 2026 · Accepted May 4, 2026 · Published online
  **Jun 5, 2026**.
- **Lead/senior:** Shalin S. Bhatt (lead), Brent T. Harris (senior/pathology),
  **Georgetown University School of Medicine** — 20-author group.

### What the study actually found (get this right)
- **Cadaveric / postmortem** study: dissection + immunohistochemistry in
  formalin-fixed human donors; low-pressure peroxide–dye injections via the
  thoracic duct and left jugular lymphatic trunk.
- **Podoplanin-positive** (lymphatic-marker) vessel structures found in
  **4 of 9 donors**, near the **craniovertebral junction**, **cervical spinal
  nerve exit points (intervertebral foramina)**, and **brachial plexus roots**.
- The vessels are positioned to **interface with, but remain distinct from,**
  CNS compartments — i.e., they **approach** the CNS boundary, they do not
  demonstrate drainage *into* or *out of* it.
- Interpreted as challenging the view that the CNS entirely lacks lymphatic
  drainage — an anatomical finding, not a functional/flow demonstration.

### Accuracy guardrails specific to CSA
1. **Postmortem anatomy, small n (4/9).** It shows *presence and location* of
   vessels in some cadavers — not physiology, not flow, not living function.
2. **"Near," not "through."** Vessels approach CNS boundary structures but
   remain distinct. No intradural lymphatics, no demonstrated CSF outflow.
3. **No treatment studied.** Nothing about adjustments, VAET, cranial work,
   or the neck being manipulated.

## The responsible CSA interpretation

The safe takeaway: **the cervical spine may be a neuro-vascular-lymphatic
boundary zone** where peripheral lymphatic structures extend along spinal
nerves and approach CNS boundary tissues. That places the neck in a richer
anatomical role than "bones, joints, discs, muscles, nerves" — which supports
CSA's argument that the cervical spine, cranial base, airway, dura/meninges,
venous/lymphatic drainage, breathing mechanics, and nervous-system regulation
should be **studied together, not as isolated silos.**

**Punchy framing:** *The neck is not just a mechanical bridge. It may be a
neuro-vascular-lymphatic gateway.*

## Do NOT say (hard red lines for this paper)

- ❌ Chiropractic adjustments drain CSF or lymph.
- ❌ VAET moves lymphatic fluid / opens CNS lymphatic channels.
- ❌ Cranial or cervical work treats sleep apnea.
- ❌ There is direct intradural lymphatic drainage / demonstrated CSF outflow.
- ❌ This proves the cervical spine controls neurofluid dynamics.

## Say instead (defensible statements)

> Emerging anatomy is showing that the cervical spine is a boundary zone where
> peripheral lymphatic structures approach central-nervous-system compartments.
> This strengthens the case that cervical structure, cranial mechanics, airway
> function, neurofluid movement, and autonomic regulation deserve to be studied
> together — not separated into isolated specialties.

> This paper does not prove that chiropractic care moves CSF or treats sleep
> apnea, but it supports that the cervical spine is anatomically positioned at a
> critical interface between peripheral lymphatic structures and CNS boundary
> tissues.

Bottom-line honest posture:
> This does not prove my treatment outcome. It shows the cervical spine is
> anatomically more important than the sleep industry usually admits.
