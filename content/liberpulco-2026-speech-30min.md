## How this differs from the 15-minute version

The 15-minute talk was a flag-plant: name the category, name early intervention,
two QR invitations, done. The 30-minute version keeps that spine and adds four
things there was never time for:

1. **Who I am** — a real credibility story (heritage, service, why I became the
   patient first). This crowd bonds over personal sovereignty stories.
2. **The three problems** — insomnia, snoring, apnea — named plainly so people
   recognize themselves.
3. **What structural early intervention actually looks like** — the CSA pathway,
   step by step, so it's concrete, not a slogan.
4. **The sovereignty frame** — your airway is the most personal thing you own;
   early structural care is taking responsibility before the system has to. This
   is the slide tuned specifically for Liberpulco.

**Guardrails (non-negotiable — see CLAUDE.md §5):** No cure claims. No attacks
on CPAP, oral appliances, surgery, dentists, ENTs, MDs, or sleep medicine. DCs
don't diagnose sleep apnea — the role is *structural*. Stay a collaborator who
*adds* a layer, never a competitor who tears one down. No "I'm not licensed here
so I'll do it on Liberland" improv — keep it clean and professional.

## SLIDE 10 — What Early Actually Looks Like (NEW) · [14:30–17:00]

**On screen:**

> 1. SCREEN — mouth, palate, jaw, tongue, posture, cranial alignment (by hand)

> 3. IMAGE — a 3D CBCT scan when the picture needs to be seen

**Delivery:** This is the slide that turns a slogan into something real. Be

> "Let me make this concrete, because 'early intervention' is easy to say and
> harder to show.
>
> **Step one — I screen it with my hands.** I look in the mouth. I feel the roof

> throat. I look at posture and how the head sits on the neck. Five minutes tells
> me a lot.
>
> **Step two — we test it.** A home sleep test. You sleep in your own bed, and it
> gives us an objective picture. Think of it as the airway's version of the
> X-ray — the picture almost nobody bothers to take early.
>
> **Step three — when we need to see it, we image it.** A 3D scan — a CBCT — so we
> can actually look at the airway from every angle, on a screen, together.

> **Step four — we correct what's structural, and we refer what isn't.** If it's
> a dental issue, it goes to a dentist. If it's medical, it goes to a physician.

*GUARDRAIL: the referral step is essential — it's what makes you a collaborator,
