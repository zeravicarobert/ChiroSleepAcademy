# CSA Severe-Case Referral & Co-Management Protocol

**For:** Dr. Robert Zeravica / Chiropractic Sleep Academy
**Created:** 2026-07-09
**Status:** Draft v1 — clinical/legal safety net for the structural
airway pathway. Review with the CA scope attorney and malpractice carrier
before adopting.

> **Why this exists.** Cash-pay removes insurance rules; it does **not**
> remove the duty of care. Owning the structural airway evaluation means
> owning the responsibility to recognize when a case exceeds the
> structural lane and route it appropriately. A written referral protocol
> is what makes the "I diagnose the structural obstruction and refer the
> dangerous cases" position defensible — and what protects the patient.

---

## 1. Principle

CSA / Dr. Z evaluates and manages the **structural airway obstruction
(SAO)** within chiropractic scope. When objective findings or clinical
red flags indicate a condition that is severe, medically urgent, or
outside chiropractic scope, CSA **documents the finding and refers/
co-manages** with the appropriate licensed provider. Referral is a
feature of good care, not a failure of it.

---

## 2. Red-flag triggers — refer / co-manage when ANY are present

**By objective HST data:**
- **AHI or RDI > 30** (severe range), or
- **ODI showing frequent/deep desaturations** (e.g., SpO₂ dropping below
  ~88%, or sustained hypoxemia), or
- Findings suggesting significant **central** (not purely obstructive)
  events.

**By comorbidity / medical risk:**
- Known or suspected **cardiovascular disease**, arrhythmia,
  uncontrolled hypertension, heart failure.
- **Type 2 diabetes**, morbid obesity, or other high-risk metabolic
  status.
- Prior **stroke/TIA**, or neurological red flags.
- Pregnancy.

**By safety:**
- **Excessive daytime sleepiness with driving/occupational risk**
  (commercial driver, heavy machinery, pilot) — potential public-safety
  duty; escalate promptly.
- Reported **witnessed apneas with gasping/choking**, morning headaches,
  severe fatigue impairing function.

**By scope:**
- Any finding requiring **prescription therapy, CPAP titration,
  surgery**, or a **formal medical (ICD) diagnosis** for the patient's
  needs (e.g., insurance, disability, DOT certification, appliance Rx).

## 3. Referral targets (build this bench BEFORE you need it)

You need a *small* standing network — not "partners," just professionals
you can send to and trust:

| Need | Refer to |
|---|---|
| Medical diagnosis of OSA / severe cases / CPAP | **Sleep physician (MD/DO, board-certified sleep medicine)** — 1 is enough to start |
| Surgical airway / ENT structural issues | **ENT / airway surgeon** |
| Oral appliance (post-diagnosis) | **Airway-trained dentist (DDS)** |
| Primary care / cardiometabolic risk | Patient's **PCP** (or refer to establish one) |
| HST device + physician read (turnkey) | **Sleep Doctor** partnership (already active) |

**Action:** identify at least **one sleep physician and one ENT** in your
area (or telehealth) who will accept referrals, *before* launch. This one
step converts the biggest liability into a managed pathway.

> **These double as power partners.** A sleep MD and an ENT are not just a
> liability safety net — they are strategic relationships. Referrals flow
> **both ways** over time (they see structural/airway cases you're built
> for), and their willingness to accept CSA referrals is itself a
> credibility signal. Build the two relationships deliberately, not just
> as a compliance checkbox.

## 4. The workflow

1. **Screen & evaluate** — CSA structural airway exam + HST.
2. **Classify** — does the case have any §2 red flag?
   - **No red flag →** manage in the structural lane (SAO diagnosis,
     structural care, education, tracking).
   - **Red flag →** proceed to step 3.
3. **Document** the specific finding and the reason for referral.
4. **Refer / co-manage** — send to the appropriate provider (§3) with the
   objective data; keep the structural care going in parallel where
   appropriate.
5. **Close the loop** — obtain the consulting provider's findings; note
   them; adjust the plan. Communicate with the patient throughout.
6. **Record-keep** — retain referral documentation (defensibility +
   outcomes tracking).

---

## 5. Documentation & consent (confirm exact language with attorney)

- **Informed consent** stating clearly that CSA provides **structural /
  chiropractic** airway evaluation and care, that a **home sleep test is
  an objective structural-screening tool**, and that a **medical diagnosis
  of sleep apnea and medical treatment are provided by physicians** to
  whom the patient may be referred.
- **Scope statement** in the patient paperwork: what CSA does and does not
  do.
- **Referral acknowledgment** the patient signs when a referral is
  recommended (and a note if a patient declines referral).
- **Standardized SAO report** template documenting objective data,
  structural findings, classification, and plan.
