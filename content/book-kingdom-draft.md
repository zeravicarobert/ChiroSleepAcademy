# Chapter One — The Consequence Standard

A technique either changes something you can measure, or it does not. That is
where this book begins, and it is where every honest clinical conversation
eventually has to return.

Chiropractic is rich with technique systems. Each has its own language, its own
founder, its own philosophy of what the body is and how it heals. A young doctor
is quietly asked to choose a camp — to become a "SOT doctor," a "CBP doctor," an
"AK doctor" — and to defend that camp against the others. I have sat in those
rooms. I have felt the pull to belong. And I have come to believe the question
itself is wrong.

The question is not *Which technique is true?* The question is *What is the
measured clinical consequence?*

Those are not the same question, and confusing them is the source of most of the
noise in our profession. A technique can carry a beautiful, sweeping explanation
of the body and still produce very little you can measure. Another can be wrapped
in modest, almost apologetic language and reliably move the needle. If we judge by
the story, we will be fooled in both directions. If we judge by the consequence,
we will not.

**What I mean by clinical consequence.** I mean an observable, repeatable change —
one you can see in the patient's own report *and* in something objective. Range of
motion. Posture. Sleep and airway numbers: AHI, oxygen levels, an Epworth score.
A before, an after, and the honesty to record the times nothing changed. A
consequence is not a feeling in the room and it is not the doctor's conviction. It
is a result that would show up whether or not anyone believed in it.

Every technique carries two things, and we have to hold them apart. The first is
**what it does** — the consequence. The second is **what it says about why** — the
explanation, the mechanism, the philosophy. The first earns exactly the standing
its measurements support. The second we hold loosely, with curiosity, never
letting it outrun the evidence. Most of the trouble in technique culture comes from
letting the *explanation* borrow the authority that only the *consequence* has
earned.

Here is what this book is not. **It is not an attack on the technique systems or
the people who built them.** I use many of them every day. I have watched them
help patients I could not have helped otherwise. The founders of these systems were,
in most cases, serious clinicians who saw something real and gave it a name. To
mock that would be both ungrateful and untrue. This is not mockery. It is closer
to clinical sanitation — keeping the instrument, cleaning off what should not be
on it.

Because that is the posture I want you to take: **keep what works.** Most
techniques produce a response at least some of the time. That response is real, and
it is worth keeping. The filter in this book was never built to throw techniques
away. It was built to separate the response you can trust from the story you cannot
prove — to keep the first and set the second gently down. When a technique earns
its place by consequence, it stays, and it is honored for it.

What the filter does refuse is a specific kind of certainty. It refuses dogma
dressed as fact. It refuses the claim that cannot, even in principle, be shown
wrong. And it refuses the oldest reflex in our field — *if it didn't work, you
didn't do it right* — because that sentence quietly removes a technique from the
reach of evidence altogether. A method that can never fail can never be tested, and
a method that can never be tested cannot be trusted the way a patient deserves.

None of this is unique to chiropractic, and none of it is evil. Every system —
medical, dental, spiritual, scientific — drifts the same way. Give any good idea
enough time and devoted people and it grows a sacred vocabulary, a founder who
must not be questioned, and a set of claims that explain far more than they can
demonstrate. That is not corruption. That is human nature meeting a good idea.
Our job is not to shame it. Our job is to filter it.

So the standard I am proposing is almost embarrassingly simple, and that is the
point. **The standard is not a belief. It is the measured result.** When someone
asks whether a technique is legitimate, I no longer argue the mechanism. I ask what
we measured, before and after, and whether it held up when we looked again. If it
did, we keep it, and I will happily use it while remaining honest that I may not
yet know *why* it works. If it did not, no amount of philosophy will put it back.

This is why the Chiropractic Sleep Academy is owned by no technique tradition —
and it is exactly that independence that lets us keep the best of all of them.
When you are not defending a camp, you are free to draw from every camp and let
the results decide. You are free to be grateful to a founder and still measure his
method. You are free to hold a bold idea and a high evidentiary bar at the same
time.

The chapters that follow build the filter piece by piece and then turn it on my
own toolkit, in public, first. I will show you what passes and what I keep. I will
also show you where I still measure more story than proof, because a standard you
only apply to others is not a standard at all.

We begin, as we always will, with the same question. Not *is it true?* — but
*what changed, and can we show it?*
