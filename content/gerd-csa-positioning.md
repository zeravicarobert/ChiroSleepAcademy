## Core positioning (canonical)

GERD matters to CSA because reflux is often not just a stomach issue. It may
also be a sleep, airway, pressure, diaphragm, posture, vagal, and structural
breathing-system issue.

In the medical model, GERD is usually managed through lifestyle changes,
acid-reducing medication, and sometimes surgery. In the CSA model, that may be
true, but it is incomplete. When reflux symptoms appear at night alongside
snoring, mouth breathing, choking, poor sleep, sore throat, hoarseness, or
sleep apnea, the chiropractor should recognize that the digestive system and
airway system may be functionally connected.

CSA does not claim to treat GERD directly. CSA teaches doctors to recognize
GERD-like symptoms as part of the larger sleep-airway-biomechanical picture.

---

## Guardrails (so this stays credible)

- ✅ Frame GERD as a **recognition/screening** cue that the airway–digestive–
  breathing systems may be connected.
- ✅ Respect the medical model (meds/lifestyle/surgery have their place — the
  CSA claim is "incomplete," not "wrong").
- ❌ Do NOT say CSA/VAET/chiropractic treats, cures, or resolves GERD.
- ❌ Do NOT tell anyone to stop acid-reducing medication or skip medical care.
- ❌ Do NOT assert a proven mechanism (vagal/diaphragm/pressure links are a
  reasonable clinical hypothesis to investigate, not established CSA outcomes).

## Where it connects
- **Screening (Pillar 7):** add nighttime reflux to the symptom cluster
  (snoring, mouth breathing, choking, sore throat, hoarseness) that flags a
  sleep-airway workup.
- **Structural container (§6):** diaphragm, intra-abdominal pressure, posture,
  and vagal tone are already in the CSA model — GERD is one more expression of
  that system.
