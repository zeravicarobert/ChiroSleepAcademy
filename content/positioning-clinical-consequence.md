# Positioning Principle — CSA Judges Clinical Consequence, Not Truth Claims

**Robert-authored, 2026-07-16. Canonical governing principle.**

> **CSA does not judge truth claims. CSA judges clinical consequence.**

**Companion principle (independence, Robert 2026-07-16):**
> **CSA is not afraid of Scripture, science, or technique traditions — because it
> is not owned by any of them.**

These two lines work as a pair. *Independence* (owned by none) is what makes the
*consequence* stance possible: because CSA isn't captured by any Scripture,
science camp, or technique lineage, it can draw from all of them and judge only by
measured result. Not-owned → free to engage → judge by consequence.

---

## What it does
It moves every argument off the unwinnable ground ("is this *true*?") and onto the
measurable ground ("what's the *consequence* we can observe?"). That single move
protects CSA from:
- **Cult accusations** — CSA isn't asking anyone to *believe*; it measures.
- **Religious pushback** — faith stays in the personal register; the academy
  evaluates outcomes, not doctrine.
- **Technique wars** — CSA doesn't crown a "one true technique"; it asks what the
  measured result was (fits "protocol level, not technique-ego level").
- **Philosophical dead ends** — no need to win metaphysics to do the work.

## Why it fits CSA
- It's the operating form of **"how CSA uses science: test a hypothesis, measure
  before/after"** (`csa-training-notes.md`) and the **outcomes registry**
  (AHI/ODI/SpO2 before and after).
- It matches the **Core Policy** (education, standards, no disparagement) and the
  **red lines** (§5) — no cure claims, respect all professions.
- It lets CSA hold speculative models (cranial pump, PRI, "energy") **without
  betting credibility on them** — they're explored, and judged only by consequence.

## ⚠️ The one nuance (so it can't be misused)
"Does not judge truth claims" = CSA won't arbitrate **metaphysical, philosophical,
religious, or technique-superiority** claims. It does **NOT** mean "anything goes"
or "if it seems to work, claim it." The evidence/honesty red lines still stand:
- Consequence must be **honestly measured** (before/after, including
  non-responders) — not asserted.
- A good consequence in one case shows structure *contributed there* — **never** a
  cure claim, never "we proved the mechanism true."
- So: **judge by consequence, report the consequence honestly.** The principle
  raises rigor; it doesn't lower it.

## Where to use it
- A **line in the Core Policy** / doctrine (governance).
- A **disarming answer** when a skeptic, MD, or rival technique challenges the
  mechanism: *"CSA doesn't argue the mechanism is true — we measure the clinical
  consequence and report it."*
- DC teaching, to keep students out of technique wars.
