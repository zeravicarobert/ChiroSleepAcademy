The story

In 1963, the American Medical Association created a Committee on Quackery. Its documented purpose was blunt: to contain and eliminate the chiropractic profession. For over a decade, organized medicine worked to keep Doctors of Chiropractic out — boycotted, isolated, labeled.

It did not work. In Wilk v. AMA, a federal court found the AMA liable for an illegal conspiracy under the Sherman Antitrust Act and issued a permanent injunction — ordering the AMA to publish the ruling in its own journal. The profession they tried to eliminate was vindicated in federal court.

Chiropractic did not survive by becoming something else. It stayed itself — its own scope, its own philosophy — took the hit, and won.

One precision point. The AMA disbanded the Committee on Quackery around 1974 — but never publicly repudiated the boycott, and the court found its effects continued (which is why the injunction issued years later). They retired the committee, not the agenda.
