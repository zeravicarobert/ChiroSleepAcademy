The Skull-Down Mandate

For a century, Doctors of Chiropractic studied the spine. For decades, dentists studied the jaw. For decades, medicine studied the apnea event itself.

Each saw a real part. None saw the whole.

The body breathes as one integrated system — from the skull to the feet. Sleep and airway aren't only a throat problem or a jaw problem. They're shaped by the whole structural frame the airway sits in.

That whole-body structure is an under-addressed dimension of sleep and airway care. CPAP and oral appliances hold the airway open — and for many patients they're the right tool. But they work only when they're used, and a large share of patients can't or won't tolerate them. What none of these tools address is the structure around the airway.

That is the gap the Doctor of Chiropractic is equipped to assess and manage — measured with testing, and coordinated with the dentist and physician as peers.

This is the Skull-Down model: assess the whole chain — cranial mechanics, cervical curve, thoracic and diaphragmatic movement, pelvis, and even foot posture and gait — because each may influence how the airway performs at night.

A body breathes. Our job is to make sure the structure lets it.
