# Positioning — Pain Management → Sleep Management (market to decision-makers)

**Robert-authored, 2026-07-16.** A positioning shift for both the DC and the
market. Aligns with the premium positioning in CLAUDE.md §4 and the
Academy·Architecture·Infrastructure triad.

---

## The core moves

1. **Chiropractic moves from *pain* management to *sleep* management** — so DCs
   showcase their **biomechanical skill** at its best. Sleep/airway is where
   structural thinking shines; pain is commoditized.

2. **Infrastructure language attracts decision-makers, not sufferers.**
   Words like *system, structure, infrastructure, architecture* signal a builder's
   frame — they pull in people who **decide and invest**, not people who only want
   relief.

3. **Market to decision-makers, not pain/suffering patients.**
   A decision-maker has agency, budget, and a future orientation. A "sufferer"
   frame attracts price-shoppers and relief-seekers. Premium positioning talks to
   the person making the call.

## Why it's strong
- **Elevates the DC** out of the crowded, discounted pain market into a
  higher-value clinical division (sleep/airway) — exactly CSA's thesis.
- **Matches premium pricing** (§4: "premium to implement, high-ticket for full
  transformation"). Decision-maker language and premium price belong together.
- **Ties to the triad:** Academy (teach), **Architecture** (how findings relate),
  **Infrastructure** (nothing missed) — infrastructure language is already yours.
- **Great for DC-facing, B2B, and partner copy** (dentists, employers, veterans'
  orgs, conferences) — audiences that think in systems and decisions.

## ⚠️ One nuance to hold (don't lose the empathy door)
This is an **audience + language** call, and it applies cleanly to the
**DC-facing / professional / premium** track. On the **direct-patient** track it
needs care:
- Your best patient hooks are **empathy-led** — the worried wife ("you're not
  overreacting"), the exhausted partner. That empathy is what gets a real person to
  *raise their hand.*
- So the pattern is: **empathy opens the door, infrastructure frames the offer.**
  Meet the sufferer with empathy at first touch; then elevate them into a
  decision-maker with structure/infrastructure language as they move down the
  funnel. Don't strip empathy out of first-touch patient copy — you'd lose the
  people who most need to act.
- Net: **market the *offer* to decision-makers; still meet the *person* with
  empathy.** Both, in sequence — not either/or.

## Where this shows up
- DC white papers + seminar copy → full decision-maker / infrastructure language.
- Patient first-touch (wives guide, social posts) → empathy first, then elevate.
- Pricing / premium positioning → decision-maker framing throughout.
