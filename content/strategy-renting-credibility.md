## The core distinction

People are not primarily paying to learn Dr. Z's methods. They are paying to
temporarily **attach themselves to his authority** — reputation, framework,
relationships, endorsement.

> **Teaching them work:** "Here is how to do what I do."
> **Renting them credibility:** "You may use my authority to help the market trust
> what you do."

They can learn the work almost anywhere. What they **can't** easily reproduce is
the **credibility that makes the market believe the work.**

## What they're really buying

Not (only) the skill — the association. The right to say things like:
> "I am trained, recognized, and operating within Dr. Robert Zeravica's
> Chiropractic Sleep Academy framework."

That association helps a DC gain patient trust, enter the sleep conversation,
stand apart from an ordinary chiropractic office, and approach dentists,
physicians, veterans, conferences, or employers with more legitimacy.

The credibility-producing assets being leveraged:
- **Dr. Robert Zeravica, DC** — the name/mark
- 26+ years in chiropractic
- sleep-and-airway specialization + clinical experience
- **VAET** and the structural-breathing concepts (IP)
- the military / ANGLICO story
- professional relationships / network
- existing reputation and body of work

**Key idea:** the name is not a label on finished work. Dr. Z's **accumulated
credibility is itself one of the assets** that makes the work commercially
believable.

## The Jay Abraham principle underneath

Credibility is an **underutilized asset that can be leveraged without being sold
outright.** Dr. Z keeps ownership of the authority-producing asset; another party
gets a **limited, conditional right to benefit** from it.

Forms it can take:
- licensing the framework
- certifying practitioners
- endorsing selected providers
- co-branding an offer
- granting network access
- limited use of name, story, or marks
- a recognized role within the ecosystem

> You are not merely transferring knowledge. You are granting **controlled access
> to accumulated trust.**

## The essential rule — "renting" means boundaries

They may benefit from the credibility, but they do **not** own, control, or carry
it away. Every arrangement should be bounded by:
- limited purpose
- limited duration
- performance requirements
- quality control
- revocation rights
- no transfer to others
- no claim of ownership
- no continued use after the relationship ends

## Where this applies in the ecosystem

- **DCs joining CSA (Step 6).** They buy clinical education *and* the licensed
  right to operate under the CSA name/framework — conditional, quality-controlled,
  revocable. This is the backbone of the certification/membership model.
- **Peter.** Contributes execution, organization, infrastructure — but part of the
  work's value comes from association with Dr. Z's credibility. Consistent with
  Peter's **vested, milestone-based, revocable** terms already noted (CLAUDE.md
  §15): he benefits from the credibility without owning it.

## ⚠️ Guardrails / open items
- **Protect the asset first.** Renting credibility only works if the credibility
  stays clean — every licensed/certified DC reflects on Dr. Z. Quality control and
  revocation aren't fine print; they're what keeps the asset worth renting.
- **Legal:** licensing marks, certification, and endorsement carry real liability
  (a certified DC's conduct can reflect on CSA). Attorney must paper the
  boundaries above before any name/mark/endorsement is granted. Ties to IP
  worksheet + Master Summary Priority 1 (legal).
- **Red lines still apply** to anything a licensee says under the CSA name — no
  cure claims, no anti-device language. Licensing = you're now responsible for
  *their* claims too.
