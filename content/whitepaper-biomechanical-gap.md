## Executive Summary

Sleep apnea is increasingly addressed through oral appliance therapy. While this
has expanded access to care, outcomes remain inconsistent.

The reason is not device failure — it is system failure.

This white paper identifies a missing layer in sleep apnea care:
**biomechanical systems integration.**

CSA proposes a coordinated, interdisciplinary model that:

- preserves professional scope
- improves outcomes
- reduces liability
- and creates new value for Dentists, Doctors of Chiropractic, and Manufacturers.

---

## The Current Training Silos

### Dentistry
- Trained by manufacturers
- Focused on: airway patency · occlusion · appliance delivery
- Limited exposure to wholistic stomatognathic biomechanics

### Chiropractic
Trained through post-graduate programs:
- **CBP** (spinal sagittal-curve correction)
- **SOT / AK** (pelvic-cranial category organization)
- **CFR / BNS** (viscerocranial-neurocranial mechanics)
- **Foot Levelers** (ground-up load transfer through the foot arches)
- Rarely integrated into appliance workflows

### Manufacturers
- Expert in device design
- Train clinicians on: fitting · compliance · device mechanics
- Typically unaware of: spinal compensation · postural load shifts ·
  cranial–cervical consequences

## The Missing Layer: Biomechanical Systems Integration

Sleep apnea is:
- not a jaw-only disorder
- not a dental-only disorder
- not a single-modality condition

It is a **whole-body biomechanical disorder with airway expression.**

The missing layer is:

> How changes in the jaw affect the spine, pelvis, cranium, and load transfer —
> over time.

No existing discipline owns this integration. Chiropractic Sleep Academy does.

## The Corrected Model (with DC services included)

**Phase 1 — Entry & Screening (DDS)**
Airway screening · CBCT imaging · initial patient education · referral for
whole-system evaluation.

**Phase 2 — Whole-System Evaluation (DC)**
Posture (CBP) · pelvis & SIJ (SOT / AK) · cranial mechanics (CFR / BNS) ·
foot-to-spine load transfer (Foot Levelers).

**Phase 3 — Targeted DC Services (Right Time, Right Dose)**
Structural correction · neuromechanical organization · cranial airway
facilitation · load redistribution.

**Phase 4 — Appliance Co-Selection (DDS + DC)**
Appliance chosen based on dental tolerance and biomechanical consequences;
manufacturer guidance applied within biomechanical context.

**Phase 5 — Appliance Delivery (DDS)**

**Phase 6 — Reassessment & Iteration (DC)**
Evaluate compensation · adjust care plan · feed biomechanical data back to DDS &
manufacturer.

## Why Manufacturers Should Partner With Chiropractic on Biomechanics

Devices perform best when their designers understand:
cervical loading patterns · sagittal balance · cranial strain responses ·
pelvic compensation · ground reaction forces.

This is not dental knowledge. This is **biomechanical knowledge.**

CSA proposes **bidirectional education:**
- Manufacturers teach device mechanics.
- Chiropractors teach biomechanical consequences.

This feedback loop improves device design, clinical outcomes, and brand
reputation.

## ⚠️ Editor's Guardrail Notes (not part of the published paper)

The paper is strong and scope-safe (DDS does dental, DC does structural — no
cure claims, no diagnosing overreach). Four notes before it goes out:

1. **⭐ The one that matters — the "Universal / Innate / Systems Intelligence"

   *"Reveals Systems Intelligence as the long-missing link uniting Universal
   Intelligence and Innate Intelligence within Chiropractic Philosophy."*

   CLAUDE.md §18): *Universal Intelligence* and *Innate Intelligence* are
   chiropractic-**philosophy** (Palmer) terms. To **dentists, MDs, and device
   manufacturers**, that language reads as insider metaphysics and can cost the
   paper its credibility with the exact people it's trying to win. Keep it for
   the **DC-facing** version of this paper, where it's an asset. → I can make a
   **two-version split**: an *industry white paper* (secular, above) and a
   *DC-facing edition* that keeps the Universal/Innate/Systems-Intelligence
   thread. Say the word.

   ("Manufacturers **Must Be Educated by** Chiropractors," "typically unaware,"
   "Manufacturers **cannot** design… without") read as talking down to the very
   partners you want. I softened to "should partner," "perform best when." Same
   point, keeps them at the table. (Red line §5: respectful, collaborative.)

3. **"Reduces liability" is a benefit claim.** Fine as a *proposed* benefit of
   coordination; don't state it as a guarantee. Consider "may reduce liability
   exposure through documented co-management." (A malpractice attorney should
   bless any liability language before print.)

4. **"Whoever owns coordination owns the category" → softened** to "serves the
