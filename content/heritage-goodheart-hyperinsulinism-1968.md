## What the article actually says

**Clinical claim:** A wide range of vague complaints — fatigue, the ~3 p.m.
crash, morning irritability, mental fog, moodiness, "rubber legs," anxiety /
nervous exhaustion, and certain headaches — are frequently downstream of **low
blood sugar driven by hyperinsulinism** (overactivity of the islets of
Langerhans). Sugar and refined carbohydrates *cause* the dysfunction and must be
restricted.

**Sleep-relevant lines (the key ones for CSA):**
- *"Insomnia unrelated to emotional tensions... is often caused by a low blood
  sugar secondary to a hyperinsulinism."*
- A **histamine cephalalgia** with excruciating pain "when the blood sugar drops
  to a low level **at night** and the patient is generally **awakened by this
  pain**."
- The nervous system depends on an adequate oxygen supply, which is "inexorably
  tied to the level of sugar in the blood."

**Treatment model:** Goodheart pairs a **hyperinsulinism diet** with **structural
correction** — Gonstead analysis, an anterior dorsal subluxation at D6/D8, and
De Jarnette's adjusting method, plus occipital / spinal nerve-fiber attention.

**Objective-testing angle:** The whole argument is anchored on a then-new, cheap,
in-office objective test — Ames **Dextrostix** finger-stick blood sugar — that let
the DC *measure* instead of guess.
