# Video Script — "What Sleep & Airway Care Actually Is"

**Audience:** Patients and everyday people who've been told they snore, or who
wake up exhausted. Not doctors.
**Length:** ~3.5–4 minutes
**Where it goes:** Texted directly to people who ask, plus the website and the
CSA channel later.
**Tone:** Doctor-teacher. Calm, direct, zero hype. You are explaining something
important to a person, not selling them.

---

## Before you hit record

- **Phone is fine.** Vertical if it's going out by text, horizontal if it's
  going on the site. If you only shoot one, shoot **vertical** — this one is
  going out by text first.
- **Eye level.** Prop the phone up so the lens is at your eyes, not below your
  chin.
- **Light from in front of you.** A window facing you beats any ring light.
- **Quiet room, phone close.** Bad audio kills a good video; mediocre picture
  doesn't.
- **Wear what you wear in the office.** White coat or clinic shirt. Look like
  the doctor.
- **Don't memorize it.** Read it twice, then say it in your own words. If you
  stumble, pause and re-say the sentence — cut later or leave it in. Real beats
  polished.
- **One take is fine.** This does not need to be perfect. It needs to exist.

## The script

### [0:00–0:20] Open — name the problem, not yourself

> If you snore, or somebody has told you that you stop breathing at night — or
> you sleep eight hours and still wake up exhausted — I want to give you a
> different way to think about what's going on.
>
> I'm Dr. Robert Zeravica. I'm a chiropractor, and most of what I do now is
> sleep and airway.

*Shot note: just you, talking to the lens. No logo intro. No music. Start on the
sentence.*

---

### [0:20–1:00] The frame — why structure

> Most people get told the same three things. It's your weight. It's your age.
> It's just how you sleep.
>
> Here's what usually gets skipped. Your upper airway — the part behind your
> tongue, in your throat — is the one section of your whole breathing passage
> with **no bone holding it open.** It stays open because of muscle tone, and
> because of the structures around it. Your jaw. Your cranium. Your tongue
> posture. Your neck. The way you carry your head.
>
> So when we're asleep and that muscle tone drops, the structure around the
> airway is what's left. And that structure is exactly what a chiropractor is
> trained to see.
>
> That's the layer I work on.

*Shot note: if you have a skull model or a CBCT airway image, hold it up or cut
to it here. If you don't, don't wait — just say it.*

---

### [1:00–1:25] The honest line — say it early, say it plain

> Now let me be straight with you about my lane, because this matters.
>
> **I don't diagnose sleep apnea. I don't treat sleep apnea.** A physician does
> that. If you get a sleep study through me, a board-certified physician reads
> it and makes that call — not me.
>
> And if you're on CPAP, or you wear an oral appliance, and it's helping you —
> keep using it. I'm not here to talk you off of anything that's working. I work
> **alongside** your doctor and your dentist, not around them.
>
> What I add is the structural piece.

*Shot note: slow down here. This is the sentence that earns trust with the
skeptical person watching, and with their doctor if it gets forwarded.*

---

### [1:25–2:35] What actually happens — the four steps

> So here's what it looks like if you come see me.
>
> **First, we talk.** That conversation is free. Your sleep, your breathing,
> what's been going on, what you've already tried. If this isn't your problem,
> I'll tell you, and I'll point you somewhere better.
>
> **Second, a structural airway exam.** Hands-on. I look at your jaw, your
> cranium, your neck, your posture, how your tongue sits, how you hold your
> head. Plus the standard sleep screening questionnaires.
>
> **Third, if it's warranted, a home sleep test.** You sleep in your own bed
> with a small device. No sleep lab, no wires, no overnight in a strange room.
> That data goes to a physician who reads it. For sleep and breathing, that test
> is what an X-ray is for a spine — it's the objective piece. Without it we're
> guessing, and I don't guess.
>
> **Fourth, we sit down and I go through all of it with you.** Your history, the
> exam, the sleep study. What I see. What I can help with structurally. And who
> else you may need on your team — because sometimes that's a dentist, sometimes
> that's an ENT, sometimes that's a sleep physician.

*Shot note: count the four on your fingers. It reads clearly on a phone screen.*

---

### [2:35–3:10] Who this is for

> Who should be paying attention to this?
>
> If you snore. If you wake up gasping or with a dry mouth. If you get morning
> headaches. If you grind your teeth. If you breathe through your mouth. If
> you're on CPAP and fighting it every night. Or if you just wake up tired
> every single day and you've stopped expecting anything different.
>
> You don't need all of those. One or two is enough of a reason to look.

---

### [3:10–3:40] Close — one small ask

> I'm not going to promise you a cure. Nobody honest will. What I'll promise is
> that I'll look carefully, I'll get you real data, and I'll tell you the truth
> about what I find — including when the answer isn't me.
>
> If any of this sounded like you, or like somebody you love, the first
> conversation is free and there's no pressure at the end of it.
>
> The number's on the screen. Give me a call.
>
> Thanks for watching.

*Shot note: stop talking and hold still for two full seconds before you cut. It
makes the ending feel intentional.*
