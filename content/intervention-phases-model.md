# The Three Phases of Sleep & Airway Intervention

**A Structural Airway Chiropractic framework**
**Author:** Dr. Robert Zeravica, Chiropractic Sleep Academy
**Status:** Draft v1 — foundational concept asset
**Created:** 2026-07-08

---

## Why this framework exists

The sleep and airway conversation is crowded with treatments — CPAP,
oral appliances, medications, surgery — but it has no shared **map** for
*when* each one belongs. Doctors, dentists, and patients argue about
which tool is "best" without first agreeing on where each tool sits in
the arc of care.

Structural Airway Chiropractic introduces that map: a simple,
three-phase model that orders every sleep-and-airway intervention by
how early it acts and how invasive it is.

> **Early → Middle → Late.**
> Start with structure and alignment. Add support when structure alone
> isn't enough. Reserve the most invasive tools for when they're truly
> needed.

This is not a claim that chiropractic replaces medicine. It is a claim
that **the arc of care has a beginning** — and that beginning has been
missing from the sleep-and-airway model.

---

## The model at a glance

| Phase | Focus | Representative tools | Question it answers |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Early** | Alignment & structure | Structural evaluation; chiropractic adjustment of vertebral, craniofacial, and visceral-cranial subluxations; airway/expansion treatment where indicated | *Is the structure in alignment — and if not, can it be corrected?* |
| **Middle** | Support & management | CPAP, oral appliances | *If the airway still needs help, how do we support it while the patient sleeps?* |
| **Late** | Intervention & alteration | Pharmaceuticals, surgery | *If support isn't enough, what more aggressive step is warranted?* |

Each phase has a legitimate, honorable place. The framework does not
rank the *people* who provide these treatments — it ranks the
**treatments by how early and how conservatively they act.**

---

## Phase 1 — Early Intervention: Alignment

Early intervention asks the first question no one has been asking:
**is the structure in alignment?** If it is not, it is adjusted into
alignment.

Three categories of structural misalignment are evaluated and, where
present, corrected:

1. **Vertebral subluxations** — spinal misalignments affecting the
   nervous system, posture, and the head-neck relationship that governs
   airway position.
2. **Craniofacial subluxations** — misalignments of the cranial and
   facial bones that shape the upper airway and its function.
3. **Visceral-cranial subluxations** — misalignments involving the
   structures of the visceral cranium (the facial/airway skeleton). When
   evaluation reveals a **visceral-cranial airway** limitation,
   **expansion treatment** is indicated to address the airway itself.

**The role of evaluation.** None of this is assumed. Early intervention
begins with structural evaluation to *discover* what is actually
misaligned before anything is corrected. Adjustment follows findings —
not the other way around.

This is the phase chiropractic is uniquely built to own. It is the
skull-down, sleep-first, structure-first foundation of Structural Airway
Chiropractic.

---

## Phase 2 — Middle Intervention: Support

When structure has been evaluated and corrected as far as it can be, and
the airway still needs help during sleep, **support** is the right next
step.

Middle intervention includes:

- **CPAP** — pneumatically splints the airway open during sleep.
- **Oral appliances** — reposition the jaw/tongue to hold the airway
  open.

These are proven, valuable tools. For many patients they are exactly
what the situation calls for — and for some, they are life-changing and
even life-saving. Structural Airway Chiropractic **respects and
collaborates with** the dentists and physicians who provide them.

The framework's only point about the middle phase is one of
**sequence**: support works best when it sits on top of a structure
that has first been evaluated and, where possible, aligned.

---

## Phase 3 — Late Intervention: Alteration

Late intervention is the most aggressive tier — it alters the body's
chemistry or anatomy.

Late intervention includes:

- **Pharmaceuticals** — medications affecting sleep, breathing, or
  related systems.
- **Surgery** — surgical alteration of the airway or surrounding
  structures.

These are essential and appropriate for the right cases. Some airways
genuinely require surgical correction; some conditions genuinely require
medication. Structural Airway Chiropractic does not oppose late
intervention — it honors it as the **right tool when earlier phases are
insufficient or unsafe to rely on.**

The framework's point, again, is sequence and stewardship: the most
invasive, most irreversible tools deserve to be reached for **after**
the more conservative phases have been genuinely considered.

---

## The core principle: sequence matters

The heart of this framework is not any single phase. It is the
**order**.

Medicine already believes this. It's called **conservative care first** —
try the least invasive effective option before escalating to the more
invasive one. It's why you rest and rehab before you operate, why you
try physical therapy before spinal surgery, why "step therapy" exists.

Structural Airway Chiropractic simply names the step that the sleep-and-
airway world has been skipping:

> **Before you support an airway (Middle) or alter it (Late), first
> evaluate whether the structure is in alignment — and correct it if it
> isn't (Early).**

Skipping the structural evaluation isn't just a chiropractic
preference. It's a gap in the standard of care that conservative-care-
first medicine would already recognize.

## The dental-screening opening — CSA as the airway liaison

There is a structural tailwind that makes the Early phase a real
business, not just a philosophy.

**What the ADA actually says.** In **October 2017** the American Dental
Association adopted an official policy on dentistry's role in
sleep-related breathing disorders. It states that **all dentists should
screen patients for sleep-related breathing disorders** (snoring,
witnessed apneas, sleepiness, and risk factors like obesity,
retrognathia, hypertension) as part of a comprehensive medical and
dental history, and **refer as needed to physicians for diagnosis.**

> Precision note (per §5 — never fake authority): this is the ADA's
> official *policy/standard of care*, adopted 2017. Say "the ADA's
> policy directs dentists to screen" — **not** "dentists are legally
> mandated." The professional expectation is real and strong; the word
> to use is *policy/standard*, not *law*.

**Why this is the opening.** The policy turns every screening dental
practice into a **funnel of screening-positive patients** — but it
leaves the dentist with a gap:

- The dentist **screens**, but does not diagnose (a physician does).
- The dentist may **fabricate an oral appliance** (Middle phase) — but
  only with a physician's Rx, and only after diagnosis.
- **No one owns the Early phase.** No one is evaluating and correcting
  the *structure* — the vertebral, craniofacial, and visceral-cranial
  alignment — before the patient is escalated to an appliance or beyond.

That gap is exactly the Early phase. It is where a
**CSA-certified chiropractor** belongs.

**The liaison role (scope-safe).** A DC certified through Chiropractic
Sleep Academy becomes the **structural airway liaison** between the
dentist, the dental patient, and the wider care team:

- Receives the dentist's screening-positive referrals.
- Provides the **Early-phase structural evaluation and care** — the
  piece neither the dentist nor the physician provides.
- Coordinates the **HST → physician diagnosis** pathway so the patient
  gets a real diagnosis from the right professional.
- Keeps the dentist, physician, and patient aligned and communicating —
  a quarterback for the structural side of the case.

> **Scope line.** In a cash model, framed as **structural upper-airway
> obstruction**, the CA DC can credibly own the diagnosis for their lane
> (CA §302, "chiropractic methods") and refer/co-manage severe cases with
> a physician. See "The diagnosis question" below for the full analysis,
> the winning frame, and the guardrails (Board opinion, referral
> protocol, terminology) — and confirm the §302 ceiling before public
> copy states it.

**Why a dentist says yes.** The ADA policy has handed dentists a
screening duty they don't have a downstream system for. CSA gives them
one: a trained, ethical, structure-first chiropractor who takes the
screened-positive patient, runs the conservative Early phase, diagnoses
and manages the structural airway obstruction (co-managing severe cases
with a physician), and sends a well-managed patient back — a patient who
may now be a far better oral-appliance candidate. The
dentist gets a **complete, compliant care pathway** instead of a dead
end. CSA becomes the connective tissue of the airway team.

**Where it plugs into the pathway.** This is a top-of-funnel source for
Step 2 (Sleep Baseline / HST) and Step 3 (Airway Baseline / CBCT) in
the productized pathway (CLAUDE.md §8), and it gives the Step 6 CSA
Doctor certification a concrete, marketable purpose: *"Get certified so
you can be the airway liaison your local screening dentists need."*

## The diagnosis question — can the CA cash-based DC own it?

This is a live question Robert raised, and after working through it the
answer moved substantially toward **yes, within the right frame.** CSA
is a **cash-based practice** (no insurance), which changes the analysis.

### What is NOT the governing authority
- **AASM guidelines are not law.** The AASM is a private professional
  society. Its "physician must order and interpret" position binds
  through *insurance reimbursement* and *medico-legal standard-of-care*
  arguments — not by statute. In a **cash model, the insurance lever is
  gone.**
- **Historical context:** in *Wilk v. AMA* (1987, aff'd 7th Cir. 1990),
  the AMA was found guilty of violating §1 of the Sherman Act — an
  unlawful conspiracy "to contain and eliminate the chiropractic
  profession." A medical guild's "standard" is not a neutral fact.
  (Use this as internal backbone, **not** as public marketing — see the
  brand note below.)

### What IS the governing authority — CA Title 16 CCR §302
A CA-licensed chiropractor *"may diagnose, so long as such diagnosis is
done in a manner consistent with chiropractic methods and techniques and
so long as such methods do not constitute the practice of medicine."*
So the real test is not "is OSA a medical disease" — it's **"is this
diagnosis done by chiropractic methods, and does it cross into practicing
medicine?"**

### The winning frame — diagnose the *structural airway obstruction*
Robert's own framing is the strongest scope position:

> *OSA is not a pharmaceutical-deficiency disease, nor an
> equipment-deficiency disease. It is an upper-airway obstruction. What
> obstructs it is anatomy — and chiropractors deal with anatomy.*

Diagnosing the **structural upper-airway obstruction ("SAO")** (the
anatomy), using the HST as objective evidence (AHI / RDI / ODI read like
a film), sits closest to "chiropractic methods." That is more defensible
than claiming the ICD-coded medical label "OSA," which is where a Board
could argue the DC crossed into practicing medicine. **Same clinical
reality — defensible framing.**

> **SAO as CSA's term.** "Structural Airway Obstruction (SAO)" is worth
> adopting as CSA's own defined diagnostic language — it ties directly to
> *Structural Airway Chiropractic*, keeps the DC inside anatomy/scope,
> and distinguishes CSA's finding from the sleep-medicine label "OSA."

**Glossary (the objective metrics an HST produces):**
- **AHI — Apnea-Hypopnea Index:** apneas + hypopneas per hour of sleep.
  Common severity bands: 5–15 mild, 15–30 moderate, >30 severe.
- **RDI — Respiratory Disturbance Index:** AHI *plus* RERAs (respiratory
  effort-related arousals) per hour — a broader count that catches
  subtler airflow disruptions the AHI misses. RDI ≥ AHI.
- **ODI — Oxygen Desaturation Index:** how many times per hour blood
  oxygen drops by a set threshold (typically ≥3–4%) — the measure of
  intermittent low-oxygen stress on the body.

> **§302 caveat (be honest internally):** §302 enumerates X-ray,
> thermography, and *neuromusculoskeletal* diagnostic ultrasound — it does
> **not** enumerate sleep/cardiopulmonary testing devices, and CA lists
> venipuncture/blood analysis as not appropriate for chiropractic. So the
> HST-in-scope position is an *argument*, not settled text. The Board
> opinion (below) is what converts it from argument to answer.

### Honest caveat on the blood-test analogy
The "if I can diagnose Syndrome X from a CMP, why not OSA from an HST?"
logic is structurally sound, but its *premise* is itself contested in
CA: §302 explicitly authorizes X-ray, thermography, and diagnostic
ultrasound — it does **not** clearly authorize DCs to order/draw blood
(hence AB-2143 having to exist). So the analogy doesn't *prove* HST
authority; the **§302 "chiropractic methods vs. practice of medicine"
test is the real argument.**

### The three guardrails (protect the practice, don't gatekeep it)
1. **Written Board opinion — framed correctly.** Ask the CA Board of
   Chiropractic Examiners (and a CA healthcare attorney): *"Is ordering /
   interpreting an HST and diagnosing a structural upper-airway
   obstruction within 'chiropractic methods' and not the practice of
   medicine under §302?"* This is the shield if ever challenged.
2. **Severe-case referral protocol.** Cash removes payers, not tort
   liability. Document a referral option for high-AHI / cardiac-risk /
   drowsy-driving cases. Referring the dangerous cases *strengthens* the
   position — it's responsible care and keeps physician collaboration
   (§5) where it genuinely matters.
3. **Terminology discipline.** Prefer "structural upper-airway
   obstruction / sleep-related breathing disturbance" over the bare
   medical label "OSA" in clinical documentation until the opinion
   letter confirms the ceiling.

### Brand note
The *Wilk* / antitrust / "illegitimate power structure" conviction is
legitimate and is Robert's backbone — but it stays **internal.** Leading
public marketing with an anti-AASM crusade trips §5 ("no angry, fringe,
anti-science energy") and scares the dentist/physician partners the model
needs. Public voice stays the calm doctor-teacher: *"I diagnose and
manage the structural airway obstruction — here is the objective data."*

### Bottom line
In a cash model, framed as **structural airway obstruction**, the CA DC
can credibly own the diagnosis for their lane and refer/co-manage the
severe cases — maximizing authority *and* keeping physician collaboration
where it counts. Confirm the ceiling with a written §302 opinion before
public copy states it.
