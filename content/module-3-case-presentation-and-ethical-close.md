## 1. The CSA closing philosophy (why we're different from AMC)

We keep AMC's **relational craft**. We strip its **sales psychology.**

**We keep:** professionalism and respect, tying every recommendation to
objective data, the bed partner as a clinical witness, teaching "normal" before
findings, honest hope backed by real before/after.

**We strip (CLAUDE.md §5 red lines):** fear-selling ("this leads to a heart
attack"), manufactured urgency, "the need behind the need" as a lever,
cure claims, over-spiritualizing, and any recommendation bigger than the
findings justify.

**The one rule that governs the whole module:**
> The patient's interest comes before the sale. Always. If the close and the
> patient's best interest ever conflict, the patient wins.

---

## 2. The role split — protect the healer relationship

The doctor should not be the person quoting dollars. Keep the clinical
relationship clean:

- **Doctor** presents the *clinical roadmap* and the *recommended next step* —
  the "what" and the "why."
- **A team member** (or a clearly separate "logistics" moment) handles the
  *dollars, scheduling, and payment* — the "how."

This is straight from the ROF close ("my team will sit down with you… no
pressure"). It keeps you the doctor, not the salesperson.

---

## 3. Present the pathway as a clinical roadmap — not a menu

The stepped pathway is a sequence of **clinical stages**, each with a purpose.
It is **not** a price list to upsell.

| Stage | Purpose | Investment (CLAUDE.md §8) |
|---|---|---|
| Alignment / Consult | Fit + direction | Free |
| **Sleep Baseline** (HST) | The objective anchor — the "X-ray" of sleep | $300 |
| **Airway Baseline** (CBCT) | See the airway | $500 |
| **VAET Trial Experience** | Live the structural work | ~$5,500 |
| **Full Guided Case Plan** | Doctor-quarterbacked, multi-provider | $15,000–$25,000 |

> **Critical teaching point:** Not every patient needs the top of the ladder.
> Recommend the **right next step for *this* patient's findings.** Recommending
> a $25K plan to someone whose findings call for a $300 Sleep Baseline is a
> red-line violation of "patient interest over sale."

## 4. The Case Presentation flow (script skeleton)

**1 — Reconnect & get permission.**
"Before I show you what I'd recommend — is it alright if I walk you through the
plan?" (Never present without a yes.)

**2 — One-line recap.** Tie structure ↔ the HST data from the ROF, in a breath.
"We saw [X] on your sleep test, and it lines up with [structural finding]."

**3 — Show the roadmap.** Lay out the stages. Show them *where they are* and
what the honest next stage is. Frame it as care, not tiers.

**4 — The recommendation.** One clear next step, with the why.
"Based on what we found, the right next step for you is [stage], because [reason]."

**5 — Time & investment.** State it plainly, or hand to your team. Nothing hidden.
"That step is [time]. The investment is [$]." — then stop talking. Let it land.

**6 — Give room to decide.**
"Take the time you need. My job is to give you the right care — not to get a yes

**7 — Answer questions.** Objections handled with honesty, never pressure (§5).

## 5. Handling common objections — ethically

- **"It's a lot of money."** Offer the honest low-friction entry — start with the
  **$300 Sleep Baseline.** Never discount-pressure or "today only."
- **"Let me think about it."** Respect it. Give them what they need to decide and
  follow up like a doctor, not a closer. No hounding.
- **"I need to talk to my spouse."** Invite the bed partner *in* — as a clinical
  witness to the snoring/apnea, not as a closing tactic.
- **Silence.** Let it sit. Silence is not a problem to solve with more selling.

## 6. What we NEVER do (red lines — CLAUDE.md §5)

- Claim VAET or chiropractic **cures** sleep apnea.
- Promise outcomes or guarantee results.
- **Fear-sell** ("if you don't do this…").
- Manufacture urgency or scarcity.
- Recommend a bigger plan than the findings justify.
- Imply the DC diagnoses OSA — the physician reads the HST; the DC is structural.

---

## 7. Teaching notes for the DC-student

- Rehearse the one-breath recap until it's automatic.
- Practice **saying a number and then stopping.** Most closes die from the doctor
  talking past the price.
- Role-play the "let me think about it" response until it feels like service.
- Remember: the close is a **moment of service, not persuasion.** If you've done
  Modules 1–2 with honesty, the close is just the natural next sentence.
