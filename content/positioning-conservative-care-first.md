# Positioning — The Conservative-Care-First Standard

**Saved 2026-07-17.** The argument that trying **low-risk, conservative (structural)
care first — where clinically appropriate — is the ethical default**, and CSA's move to
extend it into sleep and airway. Pairs with the states-of-matter doctrine (early =
structural), the VAET "early, honestly framed" stance (CLAUDE.md §18), and the
closed-loop legitimacy principle.

---

## The standard (one line)
> **Where a case allows it, try the lowest-risk effective option first — and escalate
> when the evidence says to.**

Not "conservative *instead of* medical care." **Conservative *first*, then escalate.**
It is an argument about **order**, not about good guys and bad guys.

## Why this isn't invented — it's already half-built
This is not a fringe claim. The order already exists in mainstream care:
- **Clinical guidelines** put **non-drug, conservative care first** for common
  complaints (e.g., first-line care for low-back pain).
- **Payers already require it.** Insurers routinely demand a **documented trial of
  conservative care** before authorizing surgery or advanced imaging. That *is* the
  conservative-first principle, already operating.
- **Stepped care / value-based care** are built on the same logic: start low-cost,
  low-risk; escalate by need.

So CSA isn't inventing a standard — it's **naming one that's already in the system** and
extending it.

## The ethical backbone — "first, do no harm"
The principle of **non-maleficence** — *"first, do no harm"* (primum non nocere), long
associated with medical ethics *(a paraphrase, not a verbatim line of the classical
Hippocratic Oath — state it that way to stay airtight)* — supports the same order:

- **Every intervention carries risk.** Every drug label lists side effects and warnings;
  surgery carries surgical risk. That's not an attack — **it's the label.**
- **Conservative structural care also has risks and limits** — say so honestly; that's
  what keeps this credible and non-hypocritical.
- Given two reasonable options, **"first, do no harm" favors trying the lower-risk one
  first** — when the case allows it. That is the ethical case for conservative-first,
  stated without disparaging anyone.

## How minds actually change (observed, honest)
Many physicians revise their view of the Doctor of Chiropractic **through personal
experience** — often their own back pain that medication didn't resolve, relieved by
conservative care. This isn't a "gotcha"; it's how conviction usually shifts: **lived
result, not argument.** It's also the quiet case for CSA's whole model — *let the
measured result do the persuading.*

## CSA's extension — conservative-first in sleep & airway
CSA's contribution is to carry the same order into **sleep and airway:** where a case is
appropriate, assess and address the **whole-body structure** early — measured, alongside
(not instead of) the medical pathway — before assuming the only options are device or
surgery. The structural, whole-body dimension is the **under-addressed early step.**

## How to build it into the standard (the long game)
Conservative-first becomes *the* standard the same way any standard does:
**evidence → guidelines → education → payer policy.**
1. **Evidence** — the CSA Outcomes Registry (pre/post, non-responders).
2. **Consensus/CE language** — "try the low-risk structural step first, where appropriate."
3. **Payer prior-auth** — the same "trial conservative care first" gate, applied to airway.

## ⚠️ Guardrails (these keep it credible and red-line-safe)
- **"Where clinically appropriate" is not optional.** For **severe or emergent** disease
  (severe OSA with cardiac risk, red-flag conditions, cancer), aggressive early medical
  intervention **is** the standard. Never delay necessary care. Conservative-first applies
  to **appropriate** (mild-moderate, non-emergent) cases — say so every time.
- **Never a cure claim; never "instead of CPAP/appliance/surgery"; never tell a patient
  to stop a prescribed therapy** (§5).
- **State drug/surgery risks factually, never as an attack** (§5: no disparaging MDs,
  drugs, or sleep medicine). "Every label lists risks" is fact; "drugs are poison" is a
  red-line breach. Hold the line — the argument is **order and risk-matching**, not
  anti-medicine.
- **Be honest that structural care has its own risks and non-responders.** One-sided
  risk talk is how you get waved off.
- **Measured, always** — the standard only has force if outcomes are honestly recorded.
