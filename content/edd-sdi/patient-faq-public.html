California Disability (SDI) — Patient FAQ

Dr. Robert Zeravica, DC · Telehealth Temporary Disability Evaluations · Serving California W-2 employees

If you're a California W-2 worker who can't do your usual job right now because of pain, injury, stress, burnout, or sleep problems, you may qualify for a state benefit you've already been paying for. Here are the questions we hear most.

The basics

1. What is SDI, and how is it different from workers' comp?

California State Disability Insurance (SDI) can pay part of your wages when you can't work because of an illness or injury from outside of work. Workers' comp only covers injuries that happen on the job — SDI is different.

2. Haven't I already been paying for this?

Yes. Look at your paycheck for the "CASDI" line — that's money you've paid into this insurance. It's a benefit you own, funded through payroll deductions. Most people never use it simply because they don't know it's there.

3. Do I qualify?

You may qualify if you are (or recently were) a California W-2 worker, you earned more than $300 during your base period (roughly the last 5–18 months), and you're unable to do your regular work because of your condition. EDD makes the final decision on eligibility and amount.

4. How much would I receive?

Typically 70–90% of your regular wages (lower earners are toward the higher end). The exact amount is set by EDD based on your earnings.

8. What kinds of conditions can qualify?

Non-work conditions that keep you from your usual work — for example pain or an injury that happened outside of work, stress or burnout, sleep problems, and other functional limitations. Every case is evaluated individually.

9. Is approval guaranteed?

No — and be cautious of anyone who says otherwise. Submitting a claim does not guarantee approval. EDD determines eligibility and benefit amounts. Our role is an honest evaluation and accurate documentation.

10. What is this service NOT for?

It is not for Workers' Compensation, Federal Disability, SSI, or SSDI. It is specifically for California SDI temporary disability.

Filing & process

11. Do I need to tell my employer first?

Yes — the usual order is to notify your employer that you're going on medical/temporary disability leave, then file your claim with EDD. We'll walk you through it.

12. Is SDI the same as Paid Family Leave or unemployment?

They're three different EDD programs. SDI (Disability) is for your own non-work illness or injury — that's what we help with. Paid Family Leave is for caring for a family member or a new child. Unemployment is for when you're able to work but out of a job.

13. What date do I use as my disability start date?

Your claim starts when your condition actually prevents you from doing your usual work. If you have an ongoing condition that recently flared up, the start date reflects that current inability to work. We document the honest clinical picture, and EDD decides from there.

14. Do I need all my medical information ready to file?

You can begin the claim (your identity and employment details) before the medical certification is finished. The medical part is completed by your certifying provider, Dr. Z, based on your actual evaluation.

15. How do I actually file — is it online?

Yes, most claims are filed through EDD's SDI Online portal. Tip: it tends to work best in Google Chrome. We can guide you through it, step by step.

16. What if my employer or insurance sends me forms?

Send them to us. We complete the provider portion and return them. Beyond CA SDI, we also complete many employer and private-insurance disability certifications (including FMLA and short-term disability forms).

17. Who fills out the medical part of the claim?

Dr. Z does, as your certifying provider — documenting your real symptoms and limitations. You provide honest information; he provides the clinical certification; EDD makes the decision.

18. My claim is "awaiting medical certification" — can you complete it?

Yes, that's exactly our role. Have your EDD claim number and the condition/body part involved ready so it matches your evaluation.

19. How do I keep my benefits going / extend my certification?

When your condition still keeps you from working, we can extend your certification (ongoing support is $150/month while medically supported). Reach out before your current period ends so there's no gap.

20. My claim was denied — can you help me appeal?

Yes, we can help with EDD appeals. Send us the determination letter and any forms, and note the deadline printed on them — appeal windows are short, so don't wait.

21. What will you ask me during the evaluation?

Your basic details, your occupation and employer, your last day worked, your main health problems and how long you've had them, prior treatment, and a short sleep screen (things like snoring, waking up tired, or morning headaches). It helps us understand your whole picture.

22. Why do you ask about my sleep?

Because recovery depends on sleep — if your sleep isn't restored, your body can't fully heal, and healing is what returns you to work. It's part of what makes our evaluation different. (It is not a promise to cure any condition.)
