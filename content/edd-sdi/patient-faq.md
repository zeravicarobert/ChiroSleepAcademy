## The Most-Asked Questions

### 1. What is SDI, and how is it different from workers' comp?
SDI stands for **California State Disability Insurance**. It's a state program that can pay you a portion of your wages when you can't work because of an illness or injury — one that happened **outside** of work.

The key difference: **workers' comp only covers injuries that happen on the job.** SDI is different. If your pain, injury, stress, burnout, or sleep problems come from your everyday life — not a workplace accident — SDI is the program that may apply to you.

### 2. Haven't I already been paying for this?
Yes. If you're a W-2 employee in California, look at your last paycheck for the line marked **"CASDI"** (or "SDI"). That amount is money **you** have already paid into this insurance. It's a policy you own. Most people never use it simply because they don't know it exists or don't know how to access it.

### 3. Do I qualify?
You may qualify if **all** of the following are true:
- You are (or recently were) a **W-2 worker in California**
- You **worked and earned more than $300** during your base period (roughly the last **5–18 months** of work)
- You are **unable to do your regular work** because of your condition

Your "base period" is usually **the first 4 of the last 5 completed calendar quarters** before your claim start date. In plain terms: the state looks at what you earned over the roughly year-and-a-half before your claim.

**Important:** Meeting these does not guarantee approval. **EDD makes the final decision** on eligibility and benefit amount. Dr. Z's role is to evaluate and document your condition honestly.

### 4. How much money will I receive?
Our materials describe benefits worth **up to about 90% of your regular income**, paid by the state while you're unable to work. The **exact amount is set by EDD** based on your earnings history — not by our office.
> ⚠️ **Confirm with Dr. Z** before quoting a specific percentage to a patient; EDD's published rate can change year to year.

### 8. What kinds of conditions can qualify?
SDI is for conditions that keep you from your usual work and are **not** job-site injuries — for example:
- Pain from your lifestyle or an **injury that happened outside of work**
- **Stress or burnout** that's left you unable to keep going
- **Sleep problems** and other functional limitations

Every case is evaluated individually and honestly. If it's a possible fit, our team reaches out to discuss next steps.

Because Dr. Z's approach connects **recovery to sleep**. His view: if your sleep isn't restored, your body can't fully heal — and healing is what gets you back to work. That's why the intake asks about snoring, waking up tired, morning headaches, and whether you've had a sleep study. ## Appendix — Quick Summary: What is "EDD"? (the basics)

*This part just states the obvious, for anyone who's never heard the terms.*

- **EDD = Employment Development Department.** It's the **California state agency** that runs several worker programs — including unemployment and disability.
- **SDI = State Disability Insurance.** It's the **benefit program (run by EDD)** that pays part of your wages when you can't work due to a non-work illness or injury.
- **In one line:** *EDD is the agency; SDI is the disability benefit it pays out.* Most W-2 employees fund SDI automatically through the "CASDI" deduction on every paycheck.
