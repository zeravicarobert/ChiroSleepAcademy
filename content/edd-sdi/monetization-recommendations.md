# Monetization Recommendations — CA EDD / SDI Service

**Independent strategic recommendations** for turning the EDD/SDI service into durable revenue.
Grounded in Dr. Z's own model and pricing (from his materials). These are recommendations to weigh — not a plan you've committed to.

> **Guardrail first:** Every idea below assumes the honest-documentation standard holds — *"not about gaming the system."* Monetization that pressures volume over legitimacy would put the license and the whole business at risk. Revenue follows credibility, not the other way around.

---

## 1. The engine you already have: recurring certification

Your current pricing:
- **$400** to start (initial telehealth evaluation + first month of certification support)
- **$150 / month** ongoing, while disability remains medically supported
- Free consult up front; occasional **$50-off** promo

The quiet power here is **$150/month recurring per active claimant.** That's the asset. One patient certified for, say, 4–6 months is **$400 + (~3–5 × $150) ≈ $850–$1,150** in lifetime value — from a fully remote workflow.

**Simple math to internalize:**

| Active monthly claimants | Recurring MRR (@ $150) | Annualized recurring |
|---|---|---|
| 10 | $1,500 | $18,000 |
| 25 | $3,750 | $45,000 |
| 50 | $7,500 | $90,000 |
| 100 | $15,000 | $180,000 |

*(Recurring only — before the $400 initial fees and any cross-sells.)*

**Recommendation #1:** Treat monthly active claimants as your North-Star metric. Everything else is in service of growing and retaining that number **ethically** (i.e., only while genuinely medically supported).

## 2. Tighten the unit economics

- **Know your cost-to-serve per monthly cert.** If a re-cert takes X minutes of your time plus staff admin, you want most of the $150 to be margin. Push routine steps (reminders, form prep, records requests, EDD calls via **EDD Caller**) to staff and tools so your time is spent only where a doctor is required.
- **Reduce involuntary churn.** Most drop-off is administrative, not clinical — a missed re-cert date, a lost form. A simple monthly cadence (calendar + ChiroCat text + call) protects revenue that's already earned.

**Recommendation #2:** Build a one-page "monthly re-cert SOP" so a staff member can run 80% of it and hand you only the clinical decision.

## 3. Cross-sell the recovery, not just the certification

This is where the EDD service becomes far more valuable than $150/month. Per your **Temporary Disability-to-Reinvention Pathway**, the claimant's real problem is often sleep/airway. The disability window is peak motivation to fix root causes.

Natural, ethical ladder for a patient who wants to actually **get better** (not just certified):
1. **Sleep/airway screen** (already on your intake) →
2. **CSA Sleep Baseline** (HST) — your $300 step →
3. **CSA Airway Baseline** (CBCT) — $500 →
4. **Structural care / VAET** as clinically appropriate →
5. Oral-appliance coordination with a partner dentist (DC order within scope)

**Recommendation #3:** Add a standard "recovery track" offer to every SDI patient who wants more than a certification. Same patient, higher value, better outcome — and it feeds CSA directly.

## 4. Productize the system for OTHER chiropractors (the big one)

You've built something most DCs don't have: a **compliant, remote SDI evaluation-and-certification workflow** with intake, POA, forms process, EDD-call handling, and a recurring-revenue model. That is itself a product.

Two B2B revenue lanes:
- **"EDD/SDI Certification System for California DCs"** — a paid playbook/training: the intake form, the POA template, the SOP, the compliance guardrails, the EDD Caller workflow, the fee model. One-time course + optional monthly support.
- **Referral / overflow network** — DCs who don't want to run it themselves refer California patients to you, or you white-label the back-office.

This slots directly into **CSA's Step 6 (Doctor Training / Representation)** — SDI becomes another module DCs pay to learn.

**Recommendation #4:** Package the SDI system as a DC-facing product once your own numbers prove it. This is likely higher-margin and more scalable than adding claimants one at a time.

## 5. Pricing moves to test (carefully)

- **Hold the $400 / $150 as the floor.** It's already positioned well: real value, not cheap, not predatory.
- **Consider a modest annual/renewal review** rather than discounting — the value is the ongoing relationship, not a coupon war. Use the **$50-off** only as a time-boxed lead accelerator, not a standing price.
- **Bundle the recovery track** (Section 3) as a named package so the higher-value path is a clear choice, not an upsell surprise.

**Recommendation #5:** Compete on credibility and outcomes, never on being the cheapest. Cheapest attracts the exact volume/fraud risk you must avoid.

## 6. Sequence (what to do in what order)

1. **Stabilize recurring:** re-cert SOP + retention cadence (protect existing MRR).
2. **Systematize intake→eval→cert** so staff/tools carry the load.
3. **Add the recovery track** cross-sell into CSA.
4. **Prove the numbers** for ~a quarter.
5. **Productize for DCs** (B2B) once the model is documented and repeatable.

---

## Compliance red lines for monetization (read every time)

- Never tie staff or provider incentives to **certifying more people** — only to serving legitimate patients well.
- Never promise **approval, benefit amounts, or "guaranteed money."** EDD decides.
- Never market to induce **false or exaggerated claims.** The intake and evaluation exist precisely to screen those out.
- Keep marketing claims about outcomes **honest and non-medical-miracle** — same standard as CSA (no cure claims).

*Revenue built on honest documentation is durable. Revenue built on volume-at-any-cost is a liability with a license attached.*
