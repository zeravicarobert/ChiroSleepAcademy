## The three flows (the method)
**1. Committee.** The **AMA's Committee on Quackery** (formed 1963–64; H. Doyl Taylor,
head of the AMA Department of Investigation, drove it).

**2. Goal.** In the AMA's **own internal words**, the aim was *"first, the
**containment** of chiropractic and, ultimately, the **elimination** of chiropractic."*

**3. Liability.** In *Wilk v. American Medical Association*, a federal court found the
AMA **liable for an unlawful conspiracy under §1 of the Sherman Antitrust Act.** US
District Court, **Judge Susan Getzendanner, 1987**; **affirmed by the 7th Circuit,
1990**; a **permanent injunction** issued, and the AMA was ordered to publish the
order in *JAMA.*

## The COQ-disbanded-but-function-persisted distinction
- The **committee** was **disbanded ~1974–75.** Do **not** say "the COQ operates
  today" — that hands opponents an easy wave-off. The literal committee is gone.
- But the **court itself found the effects continued:** after disbanding the COQ, the
  AMA **never publicly repudiated** the boycott or told members it was over, so the
  **lingering effects of the conspiracy persisted** — which is *why* the injunction
  issued years later. **They retired the committee, not the agenda.**
- **Today:** containment is **diffuse, partial, and coexists with real normalization**
  the profession won (Medicare, VA, military, low-back-pain guidelines). Present-day
  containment is **argument, not verdict** — never borrow *Wilk's* legal finality for
  a modern grievance.
