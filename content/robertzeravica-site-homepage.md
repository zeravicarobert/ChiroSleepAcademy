## SECTION 2 — THE STORY (the hook)

**Headline:**
## "Doc — I slept like a baby that night."

**Body:**
A patient came to me for low back pain. I helped his back, and he told me
he slept like a baby that night. I filed it away.

Then another man came in for the same thing — and this time, honestly, I
*didn't* get his back better. He came back and said the exact same words:
*"Doc, I slept like a baby that night."*

That stopped me. If I didn't fix his back and he slept better anyway, then
whatever was happening on my table wasn't only about the pain. It was
something **structural** — and it kept happening, patient after patient,
until I couldn't ignore it.

## SECTION 3 — WHAT IS STRUCTURAL AIRWAY CHIROPRACTIC (the category)

**Headline:**
## The airway is anatomy. And chiropractors deal with anatomy.

**Body:**
When someone can't breathe well at night, the world moves fast to
equipment, then to medication and surgery. Those are real, valuable tools
— and I work alongside the doctors and dentists who provide them.

But the airway is a structure — cranial, facial, cervical, and postural
anatomy. And there's a phase of care that comes *before* the equipment:
**evaluating and addressing that structure.** It's the most conservative
place to start, and it's the phase almost no one was owning.

**The three phases (simple visual row):**
| **EARLY** | **MIDDLE** | **LATE** |
|---|---|---|
| Structure & alignment | Support | Alteration |
| *The structural airway obstruction (SAO)* | *CPAP, oral appliances* | *Medication, surgery* |

**Pull-line:** *You can't skip the beginning of care. Evaluate structure
first — then support, then alter.*

*(Design: the 3-phase row is the centerpiece graphic — reuse the visual
diagram once built. Keep it respectful: every phase has an honest place.)*
