## Part 1 — The Abraham principles that apply here

Seven of Abraham's core doctrines map directly onto CSA:

1. **The Strategy of Preeminence.** Become the most trusted advisor in
   the room. You don't *sell* — you serve. Fall in love with the
   dentist's and the patient's *outcome*, not with CSA. Dr. Z is "the
   airway quarterback," not a vendor.

2. **Relational capital / hidden assets.** The most valuable assets you
   own are the relationships you already have — and, more powerfully,
   the relationships *other people* already have with your ideal
   patients. Most of your growth is already sitting in a spreadsheet.

3. **Host–Beneficiary relationships.** Find someone who already has the
   trust and access to your ideal audience (the **host**) and arrange
   for them to introduce/endorse you (the **beneficiary**). The
   screening **dentist is the perfect host**: ADA-directed to screen,
   trusted by the patient, and structurally unable to serve the Early
   phase themselves.

4. **Endorsed relationships & third-party credibility.** A warm
   endorsement from a trusted figure outperforms any cold pitch by an
   order of magnitude. You already know airway *thought leaders* who can
   endorse Dr. Z to entire networks.

5. **Risk reversal.** Remove all risk from the other party and put it on
   yourself. The one who carries the risk earns the trust — and the
   business.

6. **The three ways to grow (applied to lifetime value).** More clients
   × higher average transaction × greater frequency. Never judge a
   relationship by the first sale — judge it by its **marginal net
   worth** (the full stream it produces).

7. **Give first / reciprocity of value.** Lead by handing the host
   something genuinely valuable *before* asking for anything. The
   relationship compounds from there.

## Part 3 — The core play: the dentist as Host-Beneficiary

This is the engine. Read it twice.

**The setup.** The ADA's 2017 policy directs every dentist to screen new
patients for sleep-related breathing disorders. Screening creates a
stream of screening-positive patients — but the dentist:

- cannot diagnose (a physician must),
- can only make an oral appliance *after* diagnosis and with an Rx, and
- has **no one who owns the Early / structural phase.**

**The host-beneficiary arrangement:**

> The dentist (host) refers screening-positive patients to the
> CSA-certified chiropractor (beneficiary), who runs the Early-phase
> structural evaluation and care, coordinates the HST → physician
> diagnosis, and returns a well-managed, better-prepared patient.

**Why the dentist says yes — the reciprocal loop (this is the genius part).**
Abraham never wants a one-way taker. CSA gives *back*:

- The patient who completes Early-phase structural care is often a
  **better oral-appliance candidate** — so CSA hands the dentist a
  *more successful appliance case and a return referral.*
- CSA makes the dentist **ADA-compliant with a real downstream system**
  instead of a screening dead-end.
- CSA makes the dentist look like the hub of a complete airway team.

So it's not "send me your patients." It's **"I complete your care
pathway, I make your appliances work better, and I send patients back to
you."** That is a host-beneficiary relationship a dentist keeps for
years.

**Scope guardrail (never cross this):** the DC does **not** diagnose
OSA. Diagnosis stays with the physician. CSA owns structure,
coordination, and education. Every script below respects this.

## Part 4 — The monetization stack

Abraham's "three ways to grow," mapped onto CSA's pathway (CLAUDE.md §8):

### 1) More clients — borrowed, not bought
Every screening dentist is a *renewable* source of screening-positive
patients. Ten host dentists each referring a handful of patients a month
is a patient-acquisition engine you didn't have to advertise for.

### 2) Higher average transaction — the value ladder
A referred patient doesn't stop at the front door. They ascend:

> $300 Sleep Baseline (HST) → $500 Airway Baseline (CBCT) →
> ~$5,500 Tecopa VAET Trial → $15K–$25K Full Guided Case Plan

One dentist relationship can seed patients across the *entire* ladder.

### 3) Greater frequency / recurring — the back end
- Follow-up HST re-tests (before/after outcomes tracking)
- Ongoing case management inside Guided Case Plans
- **The DC side (Step 6):** $297/mo memberships, $1.5–3.5K onboarding,
  $10K implementation, $25K Inner Circle — *recurring and high-ticket.*

### Two flywheels, not one
- **Patient flywheel:** dentist hosts → screened patients → CSA pathway.
- **Doctor flywheel:** each certified DC becomes a *new host-beneficiary
  hub* in their own town, recruiting their own local screening dentists.
  CSA monetizes the DC (Step 6) *and* the patients that DC's dentists
  refer. This is how one relationship becomes a **network**.

### Think in marginal net worth, not first sale
A host dentist isn't worth one $300 HST. They're worth a multi-year
stream of patients ascending a $300 → $25K ladder, plus the return
appliance referrals, plus their word-of-mouth to other airway dentists.
**Price and invest in the relationship accordingly** — you can afford to
be extraordinarily generous up front.

## Part 5 — Risk reversal (the irresistible offer)

Abraham: *the party who removes the risk wins the trust.*

**To the host dentist:**
> "Send me your screening-positive patients. I'll run the structural
> early-phase, coordinate their diagnosis with a physician, and send
> them back to you as better-prepared patients — often better appliance
> candidates. If a referred patient isn't served well and you're not
> more confident in your outcomes, it costs you nothing and I stop.
> You carry zero risk. I carry it all."

**To the DC considering Step-6 certification:**
> Tie a meaningful part of the fee to their actually landing local
> dentist relationships / first referred patients — a
> performance-weighted guarantee. They risk far less; you prove the
> model works.

Risk reversal is what turns "interesting" into "yes."

## Part 6 — The Strategy of Preeminence (the posture underneath all of it)

In every interaction — dentist, DC, patient, physician:

- **You are the trusted advisor, not the vendor.** Lead with their
  outcome. The sale is a byproduct of being the most valuable person in
  their airway network.
- **Give first.** Hand the dentist a ready-made screening-to-referral
  system, patient-education material, and a compliant pathway *before*
  asking for referrals.
- **Educate, don't pitch.** CSA's whole brand voice (CLAUDE.md §4) — the
  doctor-teacher — *is* the Strategy of Preeminence already.
- **Protect everyone's dignity.** Respect the dentist, the physician,
  CPAP, appliances, surgery (§5). Preeminence is incompatible with
  attacking anyone.

Preeminence is why the "incomplete standard of care" framing beats the
"malpractice" framing on *business* grounds, not just ethics: the
trusted advisor completes the arc — he doesn't insult the people already
in it.
