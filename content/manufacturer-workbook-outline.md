# How Oral Appliances Interact With the Whole Skeleton
### A Biomechanical Primer for Sleep Appliance Manufacturers

**Robert-authored, saved 2026-07-16. Status: outline/skeleton** (like the dental
workbook started). The **third audience** in the provider triad — Doctor of
Chiropractic → Dentist → **Manufacturer.** Ties to the industry white paper's
"bidirectional education" idea. Source: Robert's `.docx`.

## Workbook purpose — help manufacturers:
- reduce device failure
- reduce clinician complaints
- improve outcomes
- differentiate their brand
- support interdisciplinary care

## Sections (outline)
1. Why airway success ≠ biomechanical success
2. How mandibular advancement affects cervical load
3. Cranial motion and nasal resistance
4. Pelvic compensation after appliance use
5. Posture changes dentists never see
6. When appliance choice matters biomechanically
7. Feedback manufacturers should request from Doctors of Chiropractic
8. Designing devices with biomechanical awareness

## ⚠️ Guardrails (before building this out)
- **Copy rule:** "Doctor of Chiropractic," never "chiropractor."
- **The "Endorsed Compatibility Badge" carries real legal weight.** Endorsing a
  device means CSA shares reputational — and potentially legal — exposure if that
  device underperforms or harms. Ties to the licensing/"renting credibility" model
  (`content/strategy-renting-credibility.md`). **Attorney must design the badge
  criteria, disclaimers, and revocation terms before it exists.**
- **Keep the "monetize" language INTERNAL.** In manufacturer-*facing* copy, lead
  with **risk reduction + outcome improvement + brand differentiation** (as the
  purpose list does) — not "monetizing what everyone's missing" (that's your
  private strategy note, not the pitch).
- **Red lines still apply:** respectful of devices and dentistry (this is pro-device
  — it makes devices *work better*), no cure claims, measured outcomes.
- **Brand:** black + champagne gold, logo on cover, "Doctor of Chiropractic" copy.
