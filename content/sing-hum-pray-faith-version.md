## The practice

The airway is a wet, living, structurally organized system. The things that move
it are ordinary: breath, voice, vibration, tongue, palate, airflow, pressure.

You do not need a device to engage them. You need to use your voice.

**Sing. Hum. Pray out loud. Make melody.**

Singing and humming create:

- vibration through the oral and craniofacial spaces
- repeated movement of the tongue and soft palate
- changing pressures inside the oral and upper-airway spaces
- airflow, resonance, and voice-induced vibration

There is even a measured effect: humming increases nasal nitric oxide roughly
15-fold compared to quiet breathing, by improving ventilation of the sinuses
(Weitzberg and Lundberg, 2002).

**Said plainly, because honesty matters more than enthusiasm:** these practices
are not established treatments for obstructive sleep apnea, and they are not
proven to reposition cranial bones. The nitric oxide finding is real; what it
means for sleep outcomes is not established. This is a simple, safe way to use
voice, breath, vibration, pressure, and palatal function together. It is not a
treatment, and it should never be offered as one.

## The connection

Scripture repeatedly ties breath, voice, and singing to worship.

> **"O sing unto the Lord a new song."** — Psalm 96:1

> **"Speaking to yourselves in psalms and hymns and spiritual songs, singing and
> making melody in your heart to the Lord."** — Ephesians 5:19

> **"Singing with grace in your hearts to the Lord."** — Colossians 3:16

The act of worship engages the same physical systems: voice, vibration, airflow,
tongue, palate, and craniofacial resonance. The breath that carries a song is the
breath that moves through the airway.

**Sing Scripture. Hum. Pray out loud. Make melody.**
