# Positioning — The Closed Loop vs. the Grounded Loop (legitimacy principle)

**Saved 2026-07-17.** A CSA philosophy note. Pairs directly with
`content/positioning-clinical-consequence.md` (the Independence + Clinical
Consequence shield). Governs how CSA talks about power, legitimacy, and its own
credibility.

---

## The principle (one line)
> **A closed loop that answers only to itself trends toward illegitimacy. A loop
> that stays open to correction from outside itself stays legitimate.**

Legitimacy is not about how powerful or intact a system is. A power structure can be
fully operational, well-funded, and still illegitimate. Legitimacy is about **what
the system is grounded to** — itself, or something outside itself.

## Where it comes from — the "circuit"
"Circuit" (judicial *and* electrical) shares one root: a **closed loop that
circulates power along fixed channels and returns it to a source.** A circuit is
**neutral infrastructure** — the same wiring powers a hospital or an electric chair.
The loop itself is never the problem. **What it's grounded to** is.

- **Closed, self-certifying loop → illegitimate.** It defines the rule, judges by
  the rule, and enforces the rule — all inside its own circuit. It answers to no
  external standard. *Power certifying its own power.*
- **Grounded loop → legitimate.** It stays open to an external standard — evidence,
  measured consequence, adjudication — that it does **not** control.

## The AMA example (why this isn't abstract)
- The AMA's **Committee on Quackery** was a **closed, self-certifying loop.** It
  defined "quackery," judged it, and enforced it — inside its own circuit, answering
  to nothing outside itself. That is the illegitimacy.
- What **opened** it was **another circuit** — the federal court (7th Circuit) —
  carrying an **external standard** (the Sherman Antitrust Act, evidence,
  adjudication). The court forced the AMA's self-grounded power to answer to
  something outside itself. (See `content/founding-legitimacy-wilk-ama.md`.)
- Lesson: you don't beat a closed loop by building a bigger closed loop. You beat it
  by connecting it to an **external ground** it can't control.

## The CSA application (this is our shield)
CSA stays legitimate the same way: by **grounding in measured external reality** —
pre/post outcomes, non-responders reported honestly, the Outcomes Registry — instead
of self-certification. This *is* the shield principle restated:

> **CSA doesn't judge truth claims. CSA judges clinical consequence.**

CSA is the **grounded loop**: owned by none (Independence), open to being measured
from outside (Consequence). That is exactly what makes it the legitimate thing in any
room — and what protects it from becoming the very kind of closed system it critiques.

## Guardrails
- **The loop is neutral; don't call "the circuit" itself illegitimate.** The court
  was a circuit too — and it was the *corrective.* Legitimacy = what a system is
  grounded to, not the fact that it's a system.
- **The moment CSA asserts power from inside its own loop** — a claim without
  external grounding, an accusation without proof — **it becomes the closed system.**
  Stay grounded. Measure. Report honestly, including non-responders.
- **Disarming line for skeptics/MDs:** *"CSA doesn't argue the mechanism is true — we
  measure the clinical consequence and report it."* (from the shield note.)
