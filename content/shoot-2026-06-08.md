## BLOCK 3 — THE CLINICAL CORE (25-35 min)

**Setup:** Whiteboard preferred. Robert draws as he teaches.

### Topics in order

#### Skull anatomy series
- [ ] **The skull is an extremity** — most don't know this. Reframe
      it on camera.
- [ ] **Each bone of the skull** — Robert teaches each with
      anatomical name + function. Crew capture the close-up of his
      hands pointing at the model/diagram/whiteboard.
  - Frontal, parietals, temporals, occipital, sphenoid, ethmoid
  - Maxilla, palatine, zygomatic, lacrimal, nasal, inferior nasal
    conchae, vomer, mandible
- [ ] **Viscerocranium vs. Neurocranium** — define the two parts
  - **Neurocranium** = the brain case (8 bones)
  - **Viscerocranium** = the face (14 bones)
- [ ] **The two parts make a PUMP** — together they create the
      cranial mechanism that pumps cerebrospinal fluid

#### CSF and the cranial-sacral pump
- [ ] **CSF — what it is, where it's made, where it flows**
  - ⚠️ Production correction (from CLAUDE.md content/README.md):
        primarily LATERAL ventricles via choroid plexus, exits via
        4th ventricle through foramina of Magendie + Luschka
- [ ] **The brake-fluid analogy** — CSF is a constant-pressure
      tubular system, just like an automotive brake line
- [ ] **Lung breathing vs. Cranial-Sacral Pump breathing** —
      teach the comparison explicitly. Two respiratory systems in
      the body:
  - **Lung breathing** = the obvious one. Diaphragm + intercostals.
    What everyone knows. Starts at birth.
  - **Cranial-Sacral Pump** = the original respiratory mechanism.
    Predates lungs. Pumps CSF. Never stops. Most doctors don't
    even know it exists.
- [ ] **The cranial-sacral pump as the original respiratory
      mechanism** — *"Before you had lungs, you had a pump in your
      skull and sacrum that moved fluid that fed your brain. That
      pump never stops working."* (Foundational SOT/PRI/PRM
      teaching from Robert's lineage.)
- [ ] **PRM / PRI** — name them on camera. **PRM** = Primary
      Respiratory Mechanism. **PRI** = Primary Respiratory Impulse.
      These are the technical names for the cranial-sacral pump's
      function, taught in SOT + osteopathic cranial work + CFR.
- [ ] **Why this pump matters for sleep + airway** — if cranial
      mechanism is restricted, fluid moves wrong, structure
      compresses, airway suffers

#### CFR + nasal anatomy (the nasal balloon technique)
- [ ] **Cranial Facial Release (CFR)** — what it is + why it works
- [ ] **Three nasal canals (meatuses) per nostril:**
  - **Superior** — beneath the superior nasal concha (turbinate)
  - **Middle** — beneath the middle nasal concha
  - **Inferior** — beneath the inferior nasal concha
- [ ] **What the balloon does** — inflates within these canals,
      mobilizes sphenoid + ethmoid + maxilla + palatine bones,
      releases viscerocranial restrictions that compress the
      airway from the inside out
- [ ] **Malpractice carrier approval** — covered separately in
      Block 5 (Credentials)

#### The poetic frame (Robert's signature)
- [ ] **CSF and light** — *"CSF behaves like a light-carrying
      medium. Subluxation reads as low light. When I correct
      structure, light returns to the system."* (Soften from "glows
      in the dark" pending source verification.)
- [ ] Connect to **ADIO** (Above-Down-Inside-Out) — classical
      chiropractic vitalistic principle. Above (skull, brain) →
      down (cord, body) → inside (innate) → out (expression).

## BLOCK 4 — WHAT THE ASSESSMENT LOOKS LIKE (10-12 min)

**Setup:** Robert at desk or with a model. Could include a Zoom
window mockup on monitor behind to show "this is what the visit
looks like."

### Topics in order
- [ ] **"This is a Zoom assessment, not a clinic visit"** — set the
      frame. Most prospects don't know how much can be done remotely.
- [ ] **The visual oral exam via Zoom** — patient opens mouth to
      camera, Robert evaluates:
  - **Uvula** — position, size, length, deviation
  - **Soft palate, tonsillar pillars** — visibility
  - **Tongue position + tongue scalloping**
- [ ] **The Mallampati chart** — show the chart on camera or
      diagram it. Four classes:
  - **Class I** — soft palate, uvula, fauces, tonsillar pillars all
    visible
  - **Class II** — soft palate, uvula, fauces visible (no pillars)
  - **Class III** — soft palate + base of uvula visible only
  - **Class IV** — only hard palate visible
  - Higher class = higher airway-collapse risk. *"Class III and IV
    are your warning signs. I can see them on a Zoom call."*
- [ ] **"Record yourself sleeping"** — practical instruction to
      patient. *"Before our second visit, set your phone on your
      nightstand and record your sleep. I want to hear what you
      sound like when you don't know you're being heard. Snoring
      patterns, breathing pauses, position changes — that audio is
      diagnostic."*
- [ ] **What I look for in the sleep video** — the gasp, the
      apneic pause, the position shift, the partner's report
- [ ] **What happens next** — *"If what I see + what you record
      flags positive, we move to HST + CBCT. Then we talk about a
      structural plan."*
