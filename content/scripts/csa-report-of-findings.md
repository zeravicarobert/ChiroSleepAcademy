# CSA Report of Findings Script

*Structural Airway Chiropractic — the HST-anchored Report. CSA adaptation
of the AMC 1st Report (Dr. Bearden).*

> **Voice & guardrails.** Report = **Mouth** — but speak with restraint
> and honesty. The Report is anchored on the **HST** (read by a
> physician), not on your opinion. No cure claims. No fear escalation. The
> ethical financial close protects the patient's interest over the sale.
> Faith stays out of the clinical copy.

---

## Teaching notes (for the doctor, before the room)

This is the visit where the patient understands. You have the HST read
back (and CBCT if done). Your job is to make the objective data plain,
connect it to what they already felt and what you already showed, lay out
an honest pathway, and let them make a free, informed decision.

Two AMC habits are **removed** here on purpose: (1) the fear ladder
("degenerative disease → heart attack"), and (2) the manufactured
requirements used as leverage. What stays: review → permission →
orientation → the objective picture → a real patient's honest example →
recommendation → ethical close → the bed partner → next step.

The physician's read is the diagnosis. You are interpreting the
**structural-airway** meaning and coordinating the pathway — that is your
lane, and staying in it is what makes CSA credible.

---

## The script

### Review the journey

> "Hello again, Ms. \[Name] — and you brought \[partner], good. When we
> first talked, you told me about the exhaustion, the snoring, the
> mornings. Then we looked at your airway and structure together. Now I've
> got your sleep data back and read, and I want to walk you both through
> exactly what it shows."

### Permission (straight talk)

> "I can soften this or I can give it to you straight. Which do you want?
> …Straight. Good — you deserve to actually know."

### Orientation — teach normal first

> "Before I show you yours, let me show you what a healthy night looks
> like, so yours means something. In good sleep, air moves freely, oxygen
> stays up here, and the brain gets through its full cycles undisturbed.
> That's the target."

### The objective picture (the HST — the "films")

> "Here's your night. This number is the AHI — how many times an hour your
> breathing was interrupted. Here's your oxygen — see how it dips here,
> here, and here, each time. And here's your sleep getting fragmented —
> your body kept having to rescue your breathing, so you never got the
> deep, restorative sleep you were reaching for. *That's* why you wake up
> unrefreshed no matter how long you're in bed.
>
> Now — the physician who read this has made the medical call; that's
> their role and it's on your report here. My job is the structural-airway
> side: connecting this data to the airway and structure we looked at, and
> helping you decide what to do about it."

*(State the facts. Do not embellish the numbers, do not predict
catastrophe. If the patient asks about risk, tell the truth soberly:
untreated sleep-disordered breathing is *associated* with cardiovascular,
metabolic, and daytime-safety risks — which is exactly why getting it
addressed matters. No heart-attack theater.)*

### Connect it back — structure and airway

> "Remember the crowded throat, the low tongue, the forward head posture,
> the mouth-breathing? Line those up against this data. They're the same
> story from two directions — what I saw in the room, and what the machine
> recorded in your bed. That's not a coincidence; that's the structural
> airway picture."

### An honest example (hope, without a promise)

> "I want you to see you're not alone in this. Here's another patient —
> similar picture. Here's where they started, and here's where they are
> now after structural airway care and \[the right combination for them].
> I *can't* promise you their exact result — no honest doctor can — but I
> can promise you I'll give you the same honest effort and the same real
> pathway."

### The recommendation — the potluck / first table

> "Here's what I recommend, and here's the honest shape of it.
>
> CSA's table is the **first table** — early structural airway care:
> improving how your airway is built and how your head and neck carry, so
> your airway has the best chance of staying open, especially at night.
> That's what I do, and I've shown you today why it applies to you.
>
> And you're free to use the other tables too, for what you genuinely
> need: a sleep physician for ongoing medical management, a dentist for an
> oral appliance, an ENT if the nose or tonsils need it, CPAP if it's
> right for you — some people truly need these, and that's good. You're
> also free to leave a dish untouched if it isn't wanted or needed for
> you. No one at my table will pressure you toward the next table or away
> from it. Early intervention first; everything else by real need."

### Confirm the structural picture (honest testing)

*(Use orthopedic/postural/airway confirmation the way AMC used
ortho/neuro — to back up the structural findings honestly, not as
theater.)*

> "Let me confirm a couple of structural things so my recommendation
> stands on more than one leg…" *(perform, report plainly)*

### The ethical financial close

> "So — is getting this addressed something you want to move forward on?
> …Then let me do my homework and build you a clear plan: exactly what
> it involves, what it costs, and how long it takes, with nothing hidden.
> I'll have my team sit down with you on the investment so it's clean and
> unpressured. You should never feel rushed into your own health."

*(No manufactured 'requirements.' If there's a genuine orientation/education
step, offer it as help, not as a hoop. Price is transparent and handled
by the team; the patient decides freely.)*

### Bed partner & next step

> "\[Partner] — thank you for being here. You've heard the nights; now you
> understand them, and you're the best support she has for following
> through. \[To patient:] Let's get your plan built. I'll look forward to
> going over it with you both."

## Debrief — highlights

- **Anchored on the HST**, read by a physician. You interpreted the
  **structural-airway** meaning and kept the diagnosis in the physician's
  lane — that's what makes CSA credible.
- **Taught normal first**, then showed the real data plainly — no
  inflation, no fear ladder.
- **Connected exam to data** — same story from two directions.
- **Hope via a real example, never a promise.**
- **The potluck/first-table posture** gave the patient real freedom —
  early intervention here, other tables by genuine need, no pressure
  either way.
- **Ethical close** — transparent plan, unpressured decision, patient
  interest over sale.

**Never:** claim a cure, escalate fear to drive a yes, imply you made the
sleep-apnea diagnosis, invent requirements as leverage, or let money
outrank the patient. The data tells the truth; you tell it kindly and
let them choose.
