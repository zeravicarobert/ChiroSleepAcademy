# CSA Structural Airway Exam Script

*Structural Airway Chiropractic — the objective screen that leads to the
Home Sleep Test. CSA adaptation of the AMC Exam (Dr. Bearden).*

> **Voice & guardrails.** Exam = **Eye**. You observe and show. Findings
> are real and shown honestly. No cure claims, no fear, no diagnosing OSA.
> The exam does not prove sleep apnea — it identifies *indicators* that
> justify objective testing (the HST).

---

## Teaching notes (for the doctor, before the room)

The patient has waited the 3–5 minutes; they're gowned or seated, and the
room has that exam feel. Break the tension, then teach them their own
airway. The exam's job is not to diagnose — it's to make the invisible
*visible*, so that when the HST comes back, the patient already
understands why it matters.

In AMC this stage taught "the 5 factors of a subluxation." In CSA it
teaches **the Airway & Structure screen** — five honest indicators you
can show a patient in their own body.

**The Airway & Structure screen (the CSA five):**

1. **Airway history & sleepiness** — snoring, witnessed pauses, gasping,
   morning dry mouth/headache; Epworth and STOP-BANG.
2. **Crowded throat** — modified Mallampati score (how much of the
   oropharynx you can see).
3. **Tongue & mouth-breathing signs** — tongue scalloping, low resting
   tongue posture, tongue-tie, mouth-breathing.
4. **Cranial-postural pattern** — forward head posture (FHP), and cranial
   symmetry (frontal, zygomatic, TMJ/earlobe, occipital).
5. **Nasal breathing** — patency, deviation, nose vs. mouth breather.

None of these *diagnose* anything. Together they tell you whether this
person's structure and airway warrant objective sleep data — and they
almost always do when the history is there.

## The script

### Ice-breaker

> "Well, they've got you settled in — good. Let me tell you what we're
> actually looking for today, and I'm going to show it to you in your own
> body, so none of this is a mystery."

### What we're looking for (frame it honestly)

> "In sleep and airway work, I'm not looking at your spine the way you
> might expect. I'm looking at the whole skull-down picture — your
> airway, your tongue, your jaw, your head posture — because all of that
> affects how easily air moves, especially at night when everything
> relaxes.
>
> I'm going to check five things. Some you can feel or see yourself.
> Together they tell me whether we need real data on your sleep. They
> don't, by themselves, diagnose anything — that's what the objective
> test is for. Fair?"

### 1 — Airway history & sleepiness

> "You told me you snore and you're exhausted by afternoon. On this scale
> \[Epworth], where do you land? …And these questions here \[STOP-BANG] —
> let's go through them. See how they're stacking? That's not a verdict.
> That's a reason to look closer."

### 2 — Crowded throat (Mallampati — explain, then show)

> "Open for me and say 'ahh.' …Now, see on this chart — some people, I can
> see the whole back of the throat wide open. Others, the tongue and soft
> tissue crowd the space, like this — which is closer to yours. A crowded
> airway when you're awake and upright tends to get more crowded lying
> down and asleep. Makes sense, doesn't it?"

### 3 — Tongue & mouth-breathing

> "Stick your tongue out. See these scalloped ridges along the edges?
> That's the tongue pressing against the teeth — often a sign it doesn't
> have room and it's sitting low. And you mentioned you wake with a dry
> mouth — that's mouth-breathing at night. The nose is built to breathe;
> the mouth is the backup. When the backup runs all night, the airway
> pays for it."

### 4 — Cranial-postural pattern

> "Stand up for me. Look straight ahead. …See in the mirror how your head
> carries forward of your shoulders? Every inch forward adds load and
> narrows the airway in front of the spine. And look here — \[frontal,
> zygomatic, TMJ/earlobe, occipital symmetry]. Structure and airway
> aren't separate systems; the shape of the head and neck helps set the
> size of the airway."

*(Show the autonomic/airway chart the way AMC used the ANS chart — but to
*educate*, not to frighten. Connect structure to breathing honestly.)*

### 5 — Nasal breathing

> "Press one nostril, breathe. Now the other. Feel the difference? If air
> won't come through the nose, the mouth takes over — and we're back to
> the airway paying at night."

### Explain every instrument before you use it

> "This little device is a pulse oximeter — it just reads your oxygen off
> your finger, it doesn't do anything to you, here, feel it. And this
> \[phone app / SnoreLab] simply records the sound of your sleep. Nothing
> here bites. I'll always show you the tool before I use it."

### Bring the bed partner in (genuine — not a tactic)

> "Did anyone come with you today? In sleep work, the person who sleeps
> next to you is one of my most important witnesses — they've *heard* the
> snoring and *seen* the pauses that you sleep right through. If they're
> here, I'd love them to see this. If not, I'll want them at the Report."

### Move to the objective step (the HST — the "films")

> "Here's where we are: your history and this screen line up. But I refuse
> to guess about your sleep, and I can't watch you sleep. So the honest
> next step is a **Home Sleep Test** — you wear a small recorder in your
> own bed for a night or two, and a board-certified physician reads it.
> That's the objective data — the sleep-and-airway equivalent of an
> X-ray. \[If airway imaging is indicated: and a CBCT scan to see the
> airway's actual dimensions.]
>
> Have you ever had a sleep study? …No? Then it's time we stopped
> wondering and got the truth.
>
> Here's what happens next: my team will get you set up with the device
> and go over everything. Once the read comes back, I'll sit down with
> you — and ideally your \[partner] — and give you a full Report of
> Findings: what the data shows, what's driving it, what I can help with,
> and honestly where else you might want to go. Fair enough?"

*(Do **not** promise treatment today or claim you'll fix it. You are
gathering truth. If asked "aren't you going to adjust me / cure this
today?": "I won't guess about your health, and I won't promise you
something I can't yet see. We get the data first, then I tell you the
truth.")*

### Close

> *(Servant handshake.)* "Ms. \[Name], it was a real pleasure. Let's get
> you the answers you've been missing. Bring \[partner] to the Report —
> this matters for both of you."

## Debrief — highlights

- **Broke the tension, then taught** the patient their own airway — five
  honest indicators, shown in their own body.
- **Every finding was real and shown**, never invented, never "a little"
  (weak words). Exclamatory only where the finding earns it.
- **Instruments explained and handled** before use.
- **The bed partner** was invited as a genuine clinical witness — more
  legitimate in sleep than anywhere.
- **The HST was framed as the objective anchor** — the X-ray equivalent —
  and the DC's structural role was kept honest: we screen and guide; the
  physician reads and diagnoses.

**Never:** state or imply you've diagnosed sleep apnea, predict
catastrophe, promise a cure, or pressure. The exam earns the test; the
test tells the truth.
