# What CSA Holds — The Three Pillars

**Status:** Structural definition. Captured 2026-08-18 from Robert's own words.
**Where it sits:** Above everything else. CLAUDE.md §7–8 describe how CSA is
*sold*. This file describes what CSA *is* — the container and the three things
inside it. When a new asset gets built, it belongs to one of these three
pillars, or it doesn't belong in CSA yet.

---

## The one-line definition

> **Chiropractic Sleep Academy is the entity that holds three things: the
> clinical knowledge, the practice model, and the network.**

Anything that isn't one of those three is either downstream of them (funnel,
pricing, outreach) or outside CSA's scope.

---

## Why an entity at all

Structural Airway Chiropractic didn't have a home. The anatomy was scattered
across dental sleep CE, sleep medicine, and cranial work. The business model
didn't exist — DCs had no idea how to open a sleep case, let alone finish one.
And the practitioners who *could* co-treat had no way to find each other.

CSA is the container built to hold all three in one place, taught in one voice,
under one standard of ethics. The category becomes real when a chiropractor can
learn it, run it, and not be alone in it.

---

## Pillar 1 — The Clinical Foundation

**Anatomy and physiology of the head, neck, and jaw as they relate to sleep and
breathing.**

This is the knowledge layer. Skull-down, sleep-first. The upper airway, the
structures that crowd it, and the physiology of what happens to breathing during
sleep — taught until the DC is fluent, not just interested.

Fluency is the point. A chiropractor who can speak accurately about the
oropharynx, the genioglossus, the maxilla, the hyoid, and what an AHI actually
means is a credible voice in a room with a dentist, an ENT, and a sleep
physician. One who can't, isn't.

**Scope line (non-negotiable):** This pillar makes DCs *fluent*, not
diagnosticians. Chiropractors do not diagnose obstructive sleep apnea. A
physician reads and diagnoses the home sleep test. The DC's contribution is
structural — assess, screen, educate, refer, and deliver structural care.

**Built:**
- `content/module-1-anatomy-and-physiology.md` — the foundation module.

**Not yet built:**
- Patient-facing versions of the same anatomy (what the DC shows on the wall,
  in the ROF, on the screen).
- The visual layer: airway diagrams, collapse-zone illustrations, CBCT airway
  reads, before/after structural images.
- The screening instruments as a taught set: Epworth, STOP-BANG, Mallampati,
  tongue scalloping, the cranial-postural visual screen.

## Pillar 2 — The Practice Model

**How to start and run new sleep and airway cases inside a DC practice —
including marketing, sales, and closing.**

This is the operating layer, and it's the one most clinical programs skip. A DC
can know the anatomy cold and still never open a single sleep case, because
nobody taught them how the case *starts*, what replaces the X-ray, how the
report of findings changes, or how to present a plan and a fee without turning
into a salesperson.

CSA's answer replaces the old DC flow:

> Consult → Structural Airway Exam → **Home Sleep Test** → Report of Findings
> → Case Presentation → Ethical Financial Close

The HST is the sleep-and-airway equivalent of the X-ray. It's the objective
anchor the whole conversation stands on.

**Scope line (non-negotiable):** Marketing, sales, and closing here mean
*clarity and professionalism*, not pressure. No fear-selling, no manufactured
urgency, no recommendation bigger than the findings justify. The patient's
interest comes before the sale, always.

**Built:**
- `content/module-3-case-presentation-and-ethical-close.md` — plan, fee, and the
  ethical close.
- Pricing pathway, Steps 1–6 (CLAUDE.md §8).
- `site/index.html`, `site/doctors.html` — the two-door front end.

**Not yet built:**
- **Module 2 — the Report of Findings.** The gap between Module 1 and Module 3.
  Highest-value missing piece in this pillar.
- Marketing: the lead magnet, the screening offer, the patient-education talk.
- Outreach copy: database re-engagement, cold DC, partner intros.
- The Tecopa proposal one-pager (what's included, what isn't — every time).

## Pillar 3 — The Network

**A network of sleep and airway practitioners for co-treatment of cases.**

No one finishes these cases alone. A structural airway case routinely needs a
sleep physician to read and diagnose, a dentist for the oral appliance or
expansion, an ENT when the obstruction is surgical, an HST vendor, a CBCT
provider, and often another chiropractor with a discipline the treating DC
doesn't have.

The network is what makes the other two pillars usable in the real world. It is
also the pillar that turns CSA from a course into a profession — DCs stop being
isolated practitioners and become part of a referral body with a shared model
and a shared standard.

**Scope line (non-negotiable):** CSA collaborates, it does not compete. We are
respectful toward MDs, DDS, ENTs, sleep medicine, CPAP, and oral appliances.
The structural layer is what CSA *adds* to the airway conversation — never a
replacement for what already works.

**Built (relationships, not yet a network):**
- `CSA Relationship Strategies` sheet — DCs, dentists, MDs, Parker contacts.
- HST: Sleep Doctor (active), Will Spriggs RPSGT/RST, vendors in evaluation.
- Strategic liaisons: Sal Rodas (Foundation for Airway Health, SleepArchiTx),
  Mark Amici (dental/Henry Schein connections).
- ~28 opted-in DCs in the warm database.

**Not yet built:**
- Any actual structure — a directory, a referral protocol, a co-treatment
  agreement, a standard of membership. Right now this pillar is a contact list,
  not a network.
- The broadcast channel (`SESSION_NOTES_2026-07-27.md`) — the most concrete
  proposal on the table for giving the network a room to stand in.
- The rule for who's in: what a practitioner must know, do, and never claim to
  carry the CSA name.

**This is the least-built pillar and the one with the most leverage.** Pillars 1
and 2 have real assets. Pillar 3 has relationships and no container.

## How the pillars map to the pathway

The "Heal the Healer First" pathway (CLAUDE.md §7–8) is how a DC moves through
all three pillars in order — as a patient first, then as an operator, then as a
peer.

| Pathway step | Pillar being delivered |
|---|---|
| Step 1 — Alignment Call (free) | Front door to all three |
| Step 2 — Sleep Baseline ($300) | 1 — lived, as the patient |
| Step 3 — Airway Baseline ($500) | 1 — lived, as the patient |
| Step 4 — Tecopa VAET Trial (~$5,500) | 1 — lived, in the body |
| Step 5 — Guided Case Plan ($15K–$25K) | 2 — watched, run by Dr. Z |
| Step 6 — Doctor Training / Representation | 2 taught + 3 joined |

The DC *experiences* Pillar 1, *learns* Pillar 2, and *joins* Pillar 3. That
order is deliberate: a doctor who has lived the model teaches it honestly.

## The test for anything new

Before CSA builds, buys, or says yes to something, it answers one question:

> **Which pillar does this serve — the knowledge, the model, or the network?**

If the answer isn't one of the three, it's a distraction, no matter how good it
looks.
