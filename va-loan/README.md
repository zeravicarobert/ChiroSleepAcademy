## 0. The one thing that decides your timeline

These items have a **strict order**, and **filing the back taxes gates almost
everything else**:

1. You can't set an *accurate* IRS or FTB payment plan until the returns are
   filed — the returns determine the balances owed.
2. Underwriters **typically want ~3 months of documented, on-time payments** on a
   tax payment plan before they'll count it as "satisfactory." That seasoning is a
   **calendar cost you cannot shortcut.**
3. Self-employed income (chiropractic + CSA) usually can't be *used to qualify*
   without filed returns/transcripts, and often needs **2 years** of them.

**Realistic runway: 3–6 months, and the clock starts when the returns are filed.**
So: file now, and run everything else in parallel.

## 3. How to obtain each flagged item

**IRS payment plan proof**
- Set up online: IRS.gov → "Online Payment Agreement" (or Form 9465 by mail).
- Proof = the **approval notice (CP523/CP89/agreement letter)** *and/or* your
  **IRS Online Account** → Account Transcript showing the active agreement.
- ⚠️ Check for a **recorded federal tax lien**. A lien is more serious than a
  balance — it may need to be subordinated before closing. Ask the CPA to check.

**CA FTB payment plan proof**
- Set up online: FTB.ca.gov → "Payment plan / installment agreement."
- Proof = the **FTB confirmation letter** + payment history from your FTB account.
- Same lien caution as IRS applies at the state level.

**Child support — current status**
- If administered through **CA Dept. of Child Support Services (DCSS)**: log into
  your case (childsupport.ca.gov / county office) and request a **case
  payment-history statement** showing current status, or call your caseworker.
- If court-ordered directly: request a **payment printout** from the court or the
  State Disbursement Unit.
- Template to request it: `status-letter-requests.md`.

**Student loans — current status**
- Federal: **StudentAid.gov** shows loan status; a defaulted loan will say so.
- Pull a **statement or status letter from each servicer** showing current /
  in-repayment (or deferment terms) and the monthly payment.
- ⚠️ **A defaulted federal student loan is a hard stop for VA (CAIVRS).** If any
  loan is in default, the fix is **rehabilitation / bringing it current** — start
  that immediately, it also takes months.

**The 3 back tax years**
- Engage a **CPA or Enrolled Agent** now — especially because self-employment
  back-filing sets the balances that drive the IRS/FTB plans.
- File **federal + CA** for each missing year. Get **IRS Wage & Income + Account
  transcripts** to reconstruct anything missing.

## 4. Two things that quietly matter

**CAIVRS (federal-debt screen).** VA runs your SSN through CAIVRS. **Delinquent
federal debt — defaulted student loans, unresolved federal tax — can block the
loan outright.** "In a good-standing plan / current" is what clears it. This is
*why* items 1 and 5 above aren't optional paperwork — they're pass/fail gates.

**DTI (your real qualification gate).** Every monthly obligation here —
IRS plan payment, FTB plan payment, child support, student-loan payment —
**counts against your debt-to-income ratio**, which is what caps how much house
you qualify for (VA is 0-down, so the gate is income, not the down payment).
Trade-off worth discussing with the lender: a **lower monthly** payment plan
preserves borrowing power, even if it stretches the payoff longer. Structure the
plans with DTI in mind, not just speed of payoff.

## 5. Parallel move: talk to a VA lender *now*

Don't wait for the cleanup to finish before engaging a lender. A **VA-approved
lender** will, in one conversation:
- Tell you their **exact seasoning requirement** on the tax plans (3 payments? 6?).
- Run a soft pre-qual so you know your **real price range** (this decides San Diego
  vs. inland CA vs. Las Vegas metro — see the strategy notes).
- Confirm the **funding-fee waiver**: if you have a **service-connected VA
  disability rating**, the VA funding fee is waived — thousands of dollars saved.

**Ask the lender these five up front:**
1. How many months of payments do you need seasoned on an IRS/FTB installment plan?
2. Do you have investor overlays on tax liens, or just the VA guideline?
3. How will you treat my self-employed CSA income (years needed, add-backs)?
4. Can projected rental income from the other 3 units help me qualify (4-unit)?
5. Am I exempt from the funding fee based on my disability status?
